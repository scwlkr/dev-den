# John Carmack: frameworks

Built from 53 accepted distinct works and 730,256 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Unnamed — Value, effort, and timing. Start with the user outcome, available capabilities, and the effort budget. List candidate improvements with their likely benefit, implementation cost, side effects, and destabilization risk. Look for changes that serve several goals at once, then reassess as hardware and the market change. Inferred decision rule: prefer an improvement with a clear user-visible effect and manageable integration risk over a technically interesting feature with no demonstrated use. There is no universal numerical score. The later reconsideration of Rage also makes elapsed time a cost: a better engine delivered years later may be a worse product decision. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Find the value-curve knee. Identify the quality dimension users actually perceive, compare successive improvements, and stop concentrating effort when further gains become hard to notice. Carmack used Doom as a retrospective example of a cohesive product where substantial additional rasterizer work might have had little effect. For VR head motion, he estimated a steep improvement between roughly 30 and 20 milliseconds, with smaller gains harder to distinguish below that range. Those are observations for particular tests, not universal certification thresholds. Continue if a specific workload, accessibility need, or safety requirement has a steeper value curve than the average case. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Establish a ceiling and trace the gap. Construct a best-case experiment that bypasses suspect stages, measure the delivered result, and inspect the difference stage by stage. For VR video Carmack loaded uncompressed frames into phone memory to establish a display-quality ceiling before investigating capture, compression, delivery, and playback. For responsiveness, film the physical input and screen together with a high-speed camera and count frames; compare a real game with a minimal input-to-color test. The gap is an opportunity, not proof that any one stage caused it. Account for uncertainty in the exact instant of physical actuation. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Bottleneck and whole-system speedup. Profile the representative application, identify the fraction limited by the proposed optimization, and estimate the best possible total gain before investing in specialized code. If 20 percent of runtime is accelerated infinitely, the theoretical total speedup is only 1.25 times; real gains are smaller and incur integration cost. Re-profile afterward because the bottleneck moves. Quake 3 measurements distinguished CPU work from graphics output, while Rage eventually became limited by game logic rather than the renderer. A fast isolated kernel, high FLOPS rating, or favorable timedemo is not enough if gameplay, drivers, or memory dominate. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Representative workload before architectural victory. Specify the real scene mix, target hardware, drivers, and user-visible failure modes; compare alternatives on those conditions instead of a flattering demonstration. In the 2004 shadow discussion, a tight spotlight on a small object concealed costs that common omnidirectional lights and room geometry could expose through repeated shadow-map draws. Also test quality and memory, not just frame rate. Carmack regarded shadow volumes as appropriate for Doom 3 hardware and shadow buffers as a possible later alternative, while noting that his proposed approach was then slower. Change the decision when the hardware and workload change. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Diagnose an artifact at its approximation. Describe where the defect appears, identify the mathematical or hardware approximation responsible, and compare a more accurate calculation with its cost and artistic effect. Interpolating nonlinear half-angle results across large triangles can make a specular highlight change shape with triangulation; per-pixel reflection calculations can stabilize it but cost more and alter the look. For a different failure, explicitly visualizing normal-map derivatives exposed blocky hardware behavior. Use diagnostic views and pathological geometry rather than assuming every artifact needs a new renderer. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Budget a frame around the experience. Choose the target deliberately, subtract fixed work, then decide what quality and gameplay can fit in the remainder. A 60 fps frame is about 16.7 milliseconds; 30 fps is about 33.3 milliseconds, and fixed passes can make the remaining discretionary budget differ by more than twofold. Rage traded effects and encounter complexity for responsiveness, used reactive resolution reduction, and allowed brief tearing when late rather than repeatedly falling to 30 fps. This is a game-specific presentation choice, not a general VR recommendation: headset tearing and missed frames can be especially disruptive. ([carmack-5a4adf2bdc77d8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NxFdsEtr-TY&t=121) / evidence carmack-5a4adf2bdc77d8-df41303b1ed4; [carmack-9f803187a68816](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zgYG-_ha28&t=2018) / evidence carmack-9f803187a68816-45a072bd369f; [carmack-1c7da098216752](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MH2hjhcfWic&t=889) / evidence carmack-1c7da098216752-d0ef742a2927; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Unnamed — Trace latency and queues end to end. Map sensing, filtering, polling, scheduling, simulation, rendering, GPU queues, swap, scanout, and display processing. Measure the complete chain, then remove or shorten buffers where their throughput benefit does not justify their response cost. QuakeWorld deliberately avoided filling a congested modem link, preferring fewer updates to a growing queue. Rage's parallel animation added a frame to firing feedback; a bypass can start an immediate effect while deferred work completes. Do not assume high frame rate guarantees low latency, or that removing every buffer is safe under variable scheduling. ([carmack-cf65cae91089c7](https://fabiensanglard.net/fd_proxy/doom3/pdfs/johnc-plan_1996.pdf) / evidence carmack-cf65cae91089c7-92b0389427dc; [carmack-29baaed19e019f](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lHLpKzUxjGk&t=1382) / evidence carmack-29baaed19e019f-aebe50580dac; [carmack-c86857a9afcd44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aHRxc3mW_A&t=5202) / evidence carmack-c86857a9afcd44-2d12fecbfee4; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Unnamed — Late information with bounded approximation. When a full pipeline cannot respond quickly enough, update the most time-sensitive information near presentation. Time warp uses a newer orientation to reproject an already rendered image, decoupling display updates from scene rendering. Schedule that work with sufficient priority and margin; long GPU tiles or queues can still cause missed scan deadlines. The approximation works particularly well for rotation but cannot reconstruct newly exposed regions, independent animation, or arbitrary positional changes. Hand geometry and occlusion may need late latching or another path. Use it to reduce a known error, not to declare the underlying scene pipeline instantaneous. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Step through the frame and turn defects into constraints. Periodically single-step a whole frame, skipping repeated loop iterations, to discover hidden ordering, work, and extra-frame delay. Record recent crash causes and inspect whether the same pattern occurs elsewhere. Carmack's error log found repeated copy-paste component mistakes; a loop, template, or other shared representation can remove the duplicated decision. Then add automated checks where practical. This is source-level visibility, not a claim that inlining function calls is inherently faster. Keep exceptions for code whose duplication or performance trade-off has been measured. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Make mistakes difficult and feedback immediate. Assume even skilled programmers produce recurring errors. Run complementary static analyzers in normal builds, annotate contracts and format functions, simplify opaque preconditions, and make actionable warnings hard to ignore. Carmack reports that null dereferences and format-string errors were major categories in his codebase. An analyzer warning that is technically false can still identify code that maintainers cannot easily understand. Balance enforcement against noise and configuration cost: he did not make the whole codebase clean under every tool, and disposable prototypes need not use the same gate as long-lived shipping code. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Functional core, explicit boundary. Gather required inputs, compute an output in a self-contained function, then handle necessary mutation and I/O at an explicit boundary. Prioritize breaking up heavily entangled state before polishing already nearly pure functions. This helps tests, reuse, analysis, and parallel reasoning, but a const reference is not safe if another thread can mutate or free the object. Copying a framebuffer per triangle would be an unreasonable price; output parameters and controlled mutation can be appropriate when their lost guarantees are understood. Carmack advocates applying the style in C++, not an unconditional language migration. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Parallelize across explicit dependencies. Begin with the actual bottleneck and the number of processors. For game code that is hard to make thread-safe, retain a comprehensible main logic thread and dispatch expensive collision, animation, or pathfinding jobs; consume results later only where that latency is acceptable. Rage used deferred work, which trades bookkeeping and delayed results for avoiding pervasive locking. Carmack also explored immutable prior-frame snapshots and event lists, but two entities can both decide to occupy the same space, so conflict resolution remains an open issue. Above roughly two or three cores he considered fine-grained jobs generally more promising than manual thread scheduling, not automatic speedup. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Preserve the production loop. Prototype gameplay and high-risk mechanics before polishing expensive assets; make edits visible in the running game as quickly as practical. Doom 3's shared editor and renderer shortened lighting iteration and avoided duplicated code. Carmack distinguished interactive feedback below a second, ideally below a tenth of a second, from minutes or hours that discourage tuning. However, a fast preview is useful only if artists can judge the relevant result; incomplete lighting may be unacceptable for some decisions. Use rough maps for gameplay and reserve full-quality bakes for decisions that need them. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Build to the actual device. Identify the limiting input, screen, network, memory, thermal, and distribution constraints before selecting mechanics. Carmack's Doom RPG prototype used step-based movement suited to phone buttons rather than transplanting a PC action game. He later called a high-end-first Orcs & Elves strategy a mistake: cuts still failed a 280 KB Java limit and delayed the low-end version. Inferred rule: establish a viable core on the constraining target first, then add optional enhancements, unless the product explicitly accepts losing that audience. Test controls over a long session, not merely in a convincing demo. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Evaluate a subsystem boundary by removing it. Map the complete goal and ask whether cooperating components can eliminate a subsystem rather than optimize it locally. Carmack's lunar-lander simulator used padded pressure-fed tanks for landing instead of separate landing gear. In software, direct BSP construction from volumes removed a separate CSG phase and its epsilon-sensitive boundary, though the rest of the toolchain still needed integration. This is useful when teams have optimized individual components while preserving expensive interfaces. The physical example is a simulator design, not evidence that eliminating safety-critical components is generally safe. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Sandbox according to the trust boundary. Identify who supplies code, what it may access, and what debugging, portability, and performance are required. For Quake Arena, downloaded client mods motivated C compiled to a bounded virtual machine with restricted system calls; native modules remained useful for debugging or some server-side cases. In 1999 the interpreter missed its performance target, so Carmack implemented dynamic code generation, measuring 52.9 native, 50.2 compiled, and 43.9 interpreted in one demo, at roughly five times bytecode size. Later he emphasized that an MMU and OS isolation can secure native applications too. These are design claims and historical measurements, not a modern sandbox guarantee. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Attack the idea before committing the organization. Keep multiple candidate approaches, make a cheap prototype or existence proof, and actively seek the case that breaks the favorite. Record what failed as well as what worked. Carmack connects this posture to antifragility: a failed idea can be useful information rather than a personal loss. He also warns that untestable ideas can become cherished pet theories. A prototype does not establish production readiness; Quake 3 renderer components had worked as demos without yet forming a usable replacement. Escalate commitment only after testing integration, representative use, and the remaining unknowns. - **inferred**: Unnamed — VR comfort as a design trade-off. First test stationary or straight-line experiences, allow physical turning where feasible, and separately evaluate artificial yaw, acceleration, and visual-vestibular mismatch. Carmack suggested immediate transitions to steady translational speed rather than a ramp in a particular locomotion discussion, while acknowledging sensitivity varies. Snap turns can reduce sickness but compromise smoothness and presence. Do not infer that unpopular motion is universally unacceptable: repeat viewing of roller-coaster videos challenged a blanket prediction of failure, without proving comfort for everyone. Measure dropout and discomfort as well as popularity. - **inferred**: Unnamed — Evaluate learning agents under deployment constraints. Use a diverse suite not designed around the researcher's own algorithm, report learning performance under a bounded experience budget, and perturb timing and environment. Carmack values Atari 100K's approximately two-hour experience budget as a measure of learning speed, while recognizing controversy over the benchmark. His team tested a physical camera-and-joystick setup; learning in place did not establish transfer to different locations. Add action delay and test sequential tasks separately for forgetting and faster learning of new tasks. Multiple seeds improve confidence but compete with experiment velocity; no single score establishes general intelligence.
