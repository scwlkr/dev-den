# John Carmack: principles

Built from 53 accepted distinct works and 730,256 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Optimize the user's complete experience, not an isolated metric. A higher benchmark, more elaborate shader, or cleaner internal subsystem matters when it improves an outcome people actually receive. Carmack later criticized his own tendency to focus on static analysis without enough attention to customer value. This does not make quality work optional: reliability, clarity, and maintainability can produce user value indirectly. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Treat hardware specifications as hypotheses until the application and driver are measured. A card can be setup-limited in one workload and fill-limited in another; drivers, memory layout, API path, and tool support affect the result. Benchmark comparisons must account for differences such as precision and vendor-specific paths. Do not generalize historical board rankings to current hardware. - **inferred**: Measure the whole program before optimizing its fastest-looking part. A large speedup in a small fraction of work has a bounded total effect, and the next bottleneck can erase expectations based on a kernel benchmark. Micro-optimization remains useful when it addresses a material measured cost and does not make the system disproportionately fragile. - **inferred**: Treat responsiveness, throughput, and smoothness as distinct budgets. Parallelism and buffering can improve throughput while adding latency; filtering input can improve smoothness while delaying it. In 1998 Carmack estimated roughly 12 milliseconds of added latency for one mouse-smoothing method and found his earlier two-frame average excessively laggy at low frame rates. Re-evaluate the trade for the actual input device and presentation context. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Prefer predictable frame behavior to a better average that conceals disruptive spikes. In his 2007 frame-loop discussion, doing work consistently and inhibiting its result could be safer than conditional execution that skips state updates; the cost still matters. Mobile VR adds a sustained thermal constraint: peak clocks that later throttle can cause judder, so select CPU and GPU budgets for the intended session length. - **inferred**: Make correctness easier to inspect and enforce, rather than assuming experts will remember every rule. Static analysis, explicit contracts, restricted subsets, and educational code review address recurring human mistakes in long-lived systems. Select controls according to product economics and lifetime; safety-critical processes are not automatically appropriate for every game, and noisy tools can bury real defects. - **inferred**: Make state and side effects visible, but buy purity where it pays. Isolated functions reduce hidden coupling and make concurrency easier to reason about. The biggest improvement can be moving from deeply tangled state to mostly explicit state, not eliminating every final mutation. Large copies, shared references, and legacy toolchains can make a rigid rule counterproductive. Corroboration: medium (2 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Keep abstractions that earn their cost, and look beneath them when they hide important work. A clean API can accelerate experiments and reduce bugs, while a graphics driver can conceal copies, swizzling, and synchronization that make real-time updates expensive. Prefer direct access when the measured benefit warrants the added responsibility and platform coupling; do not discard portability as a matter of ideology. - **inferred**: Count future code paths and production changes as part of a feature's price. In 1997 Carmack declined another native renderer despite an estimated 10–15 percent gain because each path would multiply future feature work. Later he cautioned that tessellation added after the content pipeline was established could amount to a feature bullet rather than a useful capability. A separate path can still be justified by a sufficiently large, sustained user benefit. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Exercise portability while building, not only when a port is due. Carmack found the Quake 2 IRIX port harder after Windows-focused development and planned earlier alternative-platform testing. Later id Tech 5 work used frequent builds across targets to expose memory and compatibility failures near the change. A common source base need not mean settling for the easiest common denominator; individual platforms may require additional work. - **inferred**: Distinguish a compelling prototype from a deployable product. A renderer demo can omit integration, while an early VR headset can lack tracking, ergonomics, menus, and content even when its core effect is exciting. Offer such work to experimenters with explicit limits rather than promising a finished consumer experience. Production testing must include real creators and their pathological content, not just scenes the engine programmer chooses. - **inferred**: Revisit a prior technical position when the constraints or evidence change. - **inferred**: Treat timeliness as part of product quality. Carmack later recanted an absolute willingness to wait until a project was done, saying Rage's long cycle cost opportunities to ship and learn. Re-scope a target that cannot arrive at a useful time rather than merely lowering standards at the end. This does not imply that every deadline is reasonable or that an unsafe or unusable product should ship. Corroboration: medium (2 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Limit simultaneous reinvention and retain valuable working systems. Carmack retrospectively questioned Quake's pursuit of several major advances at once and later favored rewriting impactful sections while reusing the rest. Reuse is not complacency: reexamine old assumptions and replace components that block the current goal. The appropriate pace depends on how much production, training, and integration a change disrupts. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Match the architecture to the experience instead of maximizing every capability. Quake Arena prioritized multiplayer speed, while Doom 3 prioritized unified dynamic lighting and single-player flexibility. Rage later traded some of that uniform elegance for a 60 Hz target. - **inferred**: Choose approximations deliberately, with a model of what they omit. Physical correctness can guide choices even when full simulation is unaffordable; ambient occlusion, for example, can darken near geometry that would actually reflect useful light. A visually effective shortcut can be right for a game, but its failure cases, authoring cost, and impact on users should be explicit. - **inferred**: Use real usage and experience to challenge plausible design assumptions. Carmack accepted that repeat viewing of uncomfortable-looking VR content contradicted a universal prediction of failure, and a low-persistence demonstration corrected one of his own assumptions. Neither an enthusiastic demo nor popularity establishes suitability for every user; retain comfort and failure-mode testing. - **inferred**: Separate game AI's entertainment objective from AI research. Simple enemy behavior can produce the intended player experience without maximizing tactical skill; multiplayer opponents or simulations may have different goals. For AGI, Carmack describes an unknown research path and proposes tests of learning, timing, and transfer rather than claiming that his preferred approach is proven. - **inferred**: Protect security and reliability at the actual boundary, including during maintenance. Carmack prioritized server-crashing attacks and proposed command limits and connection challenges, while weighing less damaging attacks against defense cost.
