# John Carmack: positions

Built from 24 accepted distinct works and 471,558 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Whole-system optimization: Profile the actual workload before choosing an accelerator or parallel design. In the 2006 keynote, Carmack used the example that making 20% of execution infinitely fast leaves at most a 1.25× overall speedup. Specialized hardware can also impose transfer, scheduling and development costs. Use the bound to reject implausible performance targets, then measure the complete game rather than just the improved kernel. - **documented**: Parallelism: Independent offline tasks can scale very well, but a whole game contains dependencies, irregular work and communication. His visibility tool achieved roughly 14:1 on 16 processors in 1997; his 1998 generic OpenGL threading experiment initially ran slower because of command copying and memory traffic. Treat processor count as capacity, not a promised speedup, and include synchronization, cache disruption, latency and driver reliability in the decision. ([carmack-3cfb25e43ff7a1](https://github.com/ESWAT/john-carmack-plan-archive/blob/master/by_year/johnc_plan_1997.txt) / evidence carmack-3cfb25e43ff7a1-39ccf3d708f3; [carmack-0dc84858363862](https://www.fabiensanglard.net/fd_proxy/doom3/pdfs/johnc-plan_1998.pdf) / evidence carmack-0dc84858363862-90f65ddc2323; [carmack-24129fb05ae7a1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fis3AlTaml4&t=285) / evidence carmack-24129fb05ae7a1-05dcb1e54247; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Game-thread architecture: Keep creative game logic understandable and dispatch expensive collision, pathfinding and animation as jobs when their results can arrive later. The 2009 architecture accepted deferred work rather than requiring gameplay programmers to coordinate pervasive locks. Later, he explored immutable world snapshots and per-entity events, but identified simultaneous movement conflicts and did not present that design as production-proven. ([carmack-c86857a9afcd44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aHRxc3mW_A&t=5520) / evidence carmack-c86857a9afcd44-b6c092f93ddc; [carmack-98215f87b417d5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W3RsQCGiTgA&t=5738) / evidence carmack-98215f87b417d5-1a922fea1764; [carmack-98215f87b417d5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W3RsQCGiTgA&t=5778) / evidence carmack-98215f87b417d5-323f052ab81b; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Frame pacing versus average speed: A steady worst case can matter more than an improved average in a 60 Hz game. In 2007 he considered doing work unconditionally and inhibiting its effect when that avoided missed state updates; a later comment qualified that choice for power- and thermal-limited mobile hardware. Do not turn the rule into unconditional extra work: measure deadline misses, energy and the cost of the supposedly skipped path. ([carmack-2f2600ed648988](https://cbarrete.com/carmack.html) / evidence carmack-2f2600ed648988-c7f4b292b1c5; [carmack-2f2600ed648988](https://cbarrete.com/carmack.html) / evidence carmack-2f2600ed648988-7c5e517b2c50; [carmack-2096338268fad7](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nqzpAbK9qFk&t=4067) / evidence carmack-2096338268fad7-20cc32655176; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Responsiveness versus throughput: Additional pipeline stages can raise frame throughput while adding input latency. Rage deferred animation and could add a frame to firing relative to movement; Carmack proposed bypassing nonessential stages for immediate feedback. Measure from physical input to visible response, not only frames per second, and preserve consistency between early feedback and the authoritative action. ([carmack-3363b418bd9a31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wt-iVFxgFWk&t=3407) / evidence carmack-3363b418bd9a31-50736df965a9; [carmack-c86857a9afcd44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aHRxc3mW_A&t=5202) / evidence carmack-c86857a9afcd44-2d12fecbfee4; [carmack-3363b418bd9a31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wt-iVFxgFWk&t=3187) / evidence carmack-3363b418bd9a31-cba352d42cc2; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Dynamic resolution and frame deadlines: Rage targeted 60 Hz and reduced resolution when rendering became expensive. If a spike still missed the deadline, it temporarily permitted tearing rather than repeatedly falling between 60 and 30 fps. This was a product-specific compromise, not a universal preference for tearing; Carmack also proposed anticipating known spikes such as explosions rather than relying solely on reactive scaling. - **documented**: Rendering architecture and hardware: Revisit assumptions when hardware, drivers and the product change. Doom 3-era shadow volumes were a reasonable choice for their target, while later shadow buffers offered different quality and scaling possibilities but were still slower in his tests. Compare representative omnidirectional lights, repeated geometry draws, artifacts and target drivers; do not infer a general winner from a favorable spotlight demo. - **documented**: Shadow quality: Shadow-map bias trades surface acne against detached shadows. Carmack's front-and-back-face midpoint approach was robust in his tests but doubled shadow-buffer rendering; a cheaper default with an optional higher-quality path was a plausible compromise. Soft shadow blur also supplies artistic control without automatically reproducing the geometry-dependent penumbra of an extended light. - **documented**: Rasterization, ray tracing and representation: In 2008 he judged rasterization more efficient for conventional triangle geometry while exploring sparse-voxel ray casting. By 2013 he expected ray tracing to become more attractive as computation increased, but saw substantial remaining cost and revised his expectation that voxel-space tracing would displace triangle-based workflows. Rendering algorithm and authored representation are separate decisions; neither a forecast nor a demo establishes a current production winner. - **documented**: Lighting realism: Understand the physical reference before choosing an approximation. An ambient term or ambient occlusion can provide useful visual cues but does not calculate indirect transport; nearby bright surfaces are a counterexample to indiscriminate darkening. Material inputs also matter: implausible diffuse and specular values can undermine a sophisticated renderer. Accuracy must still be balanced against art direction, build time and artist workflow. - **documented**: Unified lighting versus product-specific hacks: Doom 3 pursued consistent treatment of dynamic objects and world geometry. For Rage, Carmack accepted less uniform techniques to meet responsive driving and first-person gameplay at 60 Hz. On revisiting Doom 3 he found its hard shadows and limited ambient light strikingly harsh, while acknowledging that Rage's attractive environments sacrificed some interactivity. Architectural elegance is therefore subordinate to the intended experience. - **documented**: Virtual texturing: MegaTexture let artists place unique surface detail without holding the complete texture in GPU memory. Its production costs included page feedback, fragmented content, streaming, storage, transcoding and weaker general dynamic lighting. Carmack later considered hardware virtual texturing useful but found replacement and feedback harder, and its advantage over an existing software pipeline smaller, than he had expected. - **documented**: Content and tools: Renderer sophistication does not compensate for weak assets or an unusable workflow. Carmack favors predictable tools and short feedback loops, while recognizing that artists may reject previews too unlike final lighting. Prototype gameplay with cheap assets before expensive polish; then budget for production-quality tools, iteration and the artist training required by new material assumptions. - **documented**: Graphics APIs and code paths: In 1996 Carmack preferred OpenGL's simpler procedural interface, while recognizing Direct3D's coverage and some useful features. In 1997 he rejected a possible 10–15% native Glide gain because another renderer path would multiply future feature work. His preference was conditional: he would adapt if OpenGL support vanished, and later isolated API-specific code instead of making the whole engine depend on one interface. - **documented**: Cross-platform development: Exercise every important target throughout development, not after a PC version is finished. Carmack attributed Quake 2 portability trouble partly to Windows-only development and later advocated continuous shared-code builds and tests across console targets. A shared source tree should not mean refusing platform-specific work: the difficult platform may require extra engineering to achieve comparable results. - **documented**: Pure functions and state: His 2012 C++ essay advocates collecting explicit inputs, computing results in pure functions and isolating necessary side effects. This improves testability and reduces hidden coupling, but a const reference is not safe if another thread can mutate or free the referenced data. Purity is a gradient: untangling a state-heavy system may be more valuable than perfecting an already simple function, and copying large data can be prohibitive. - **documented**: Inlining and code visibility: In 2007 he advocated considering source-level inlining of single-use functions and stepping through a whole frame to reveal ordering and hidden work, while preferring a shared function over duplication. His later comment became more enthusiastic about pure functions as a more direct solution to unexpected state dependencies. This is a maintainability and reasoning argument, not a blanket claim that removing calls speeds execution. - **documented**: Static analysis: Carmack moved from an unsuccessful early experience with noisy linting to advocating complementary analyzers integrated into normal builds. Treat repeatable high-signal warnings seriously and simplify code whose safety depends on obscure preconditions, but recognize configuration cost and false positives. His own project still had thousands of unreviewed PC-lint warnings, so a clean result from one tool is not proof of defect-free software. - **documented**: Security and mod execution: Quake 2's native DLL direction offered speed and debugging but introduced portability and trust costs. - **documented**: Network prediction: In 1996 Carmack exchanged the simple terminal-like client for local prediction reconciled with server state, then quickly reduced the proposed scope after long prediction created projectile and interaction inconsistencies. - **documented**: Network congestion: Bandwidth and latency are different constraints. In the dial-up QuakeWorld design, sending less when a link was congested was preferable to letting queues produce enormous latency; later Quake III guidance warned that setting the rate too high could cause seconds of delay. The appropriate update budget depends on visible activity, connection behavior and loss, not player count alone. - **documented**: Audio complexity: Carmack twice described replacing sophisticated conventional-game audio processing with a simpler reliable mixer that designers preferred.
