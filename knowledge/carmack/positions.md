# John Carmack: positions

Built from 53 accepted distinct works and 730,256 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Engine architecture and reuse. Carmack treats an engine as a set of assumptions, programmable boundaries and creator workflows, not merely a renderer. In 2000 he had already reconsidered his pride in clean-sheet rewrites and advocated incrementally replacing subsystems while critically examining inherited code. After Rage, he placed still more weight on stable production pipelines and rewriting only sections with material impact. Reuse is not an excuse to preserve obsolete hardware assumptions; it is a way to buy shipping and learning time. - **inferred**: Simplicity is subordinate to value. Prefer fewer paths, predictable behavior and interfaces that serve several uses, but do not reject a messy feature that genuinely improves the product. In 1997 he declined a separate Glide path despite an estimated 10–15 percent speedup because every path makes later features more expensive. His retrospective on Wolfenstein push walls is the counterexample: implementation elegance was not the important outcome when the feature added worthwhile player experience. ([carmack-3cfb25e43ff7a1](https://github.com/ESWAT/john-carmack-plan-archive/blob/master/by_year/johnc_plan_1997.txt) / evidence carmack-3cfb25e43ff7a1-7c084acf2b80; [carmack-0dc84858363862](https://www.fabiensanglard.net/fd_proxy/doom3/pdfs/johnc-plan_1998.pdf) / evidence carmack-0dc84858363862-a8ad2f293c70; [carmack-b339461e156a0e](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QvAkaJsvAXs&t=977) / evidence carmack-b339461e156a0e-3a67db2e388b; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Profiling and whole-system optimization. Establish the real bottleneck before choosing threads, SIMD or an accelerator; calculate the maximum whole-program gain from the fraction affected. In a 1998 server investigation, movement simulation consumed 60 percent of execution time, but concurrent client commands implied extensive shared-server changes. A fast isolated kernel is not enough if queueing, copies, synchronization or the rest of the application dominate. Where possible, remove work or reduce its frequency before making the work more intricate. ([carmack-0dc84858363862](https://www.fabiensanglard.net/fd_proxy/doom3/pdfs/johnc-plan_1998.pdf) / evidence carmack-0dc84858363862-8452dfabece4; [carmack-24129fb05ae7a1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fis3AlTaml4&t=1046) / evidence carmack-24129fb05ae7a1-276ab9813fa0; [carmack-a3e6dfd2fae827](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=00Q9-ftiPVQ&t=2179) / evidence carmack-a3e6dfd2fae827-2317aceae576; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Multicore architecture. Parallelism must match dependencies and platform costs. Doom 3 split rendering work for PC driver overhead; Rage separated game and rendering work and used finer jobs across cores. Carmack favored job systems over manual scheduling once beyond roughly two or three cores, but kept much game logic single-threaded and dispatched expensive collision and animation work as jobs. Deferred results add bookkeeping and sometimes a frame of latency; avoiding uncontrolled shared-state locking does not make parallelism free. ([carmack-36e4d3f6014c6b](https://fabiensanglard.net/doom3/interviews.php) / evidence carmack-36e4d3f6014c6b-758ed9a3ad65; [carmack-c86857a9afcd44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aHRxc3mW_A&t=5520) / evidence carmack-c86857a9afcd44-b6c092f93ddc; [carmack-95a1b9e29da965](https://fabiensanglard.net/fd_proxy/doom3/pdfs/johnc-plan_1999.pdf) / evidence carmack-95a1b9e29da965-0464d14a180b; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Performance predictability. For a fixed-rate game, missed deadlines and jitter can matter more than average throughput. In 2007 Carmack sometimes preferred performing work and suppressing its result over conditional execution that might omit a required update. In a later comment he qualified that approach for mobile, where unnecessary work consumes power and thermal budget. Treat the frame deadline, sustained heat and correctness as separate constraints rather than blindly maximizing average FPS. ([carmack-2f2600ed648988](https://cbarrete.com/carmack.html) / evidence carmack-2f2600ed648988-c7f4b292b1c5; [carmack-2f2600ed648988](https://cbarrete.com/carmack.html) / evidence carmack-2f2600ed648988-7c5e517b2c50; [carmack-2096338268fad7](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nqzpAbK9qFk&t=4067) / evidence carmack-2096338268fad7-20cc32655176; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Frame-rate targets. Rage prioritized 60 fps and responsiveness over effects, enemy counts and some rendering flexibility; the practical budgets are approximately 16 ms at 60 Hz and 33 ms at 30 Hz. Fixed costs can make the additional budget for expensive effects proportionally greater than the raw difference suggests. Carmack later acknowledged that rigid enforcement cost dramatic moments and that variable refresh could make occasional slower, richer scenes more palatable. This is a product-specific trade, not a universal 60 fps commandment. ([carmack-5a4adf2bdc77d8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NxFdsEtr-TY&t=121) / evidence carmack-5a4adf2bdc77d8-df41303b1ed4; [carmack-9f803187a68816](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zgYG-_ha28&t=2326) / evidence carmack-9f803187a68816-939f062a66a1; [carmack-1c7da098216752](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MH2hjhcfWic&t=2640) / evidence carmack-1c7da098216752-a4a97ba9f0f6; [carmack-1c7da098216752](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MH2hjhcfWic&t=763) / evidence carmack-1c7da098216752-8666d5854c5a; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Latency versus throughput. Measure from physical input to visible output, including polling, scheduling, simulation, render queues, driver buffers and scanout. A high-speed camera capturing both input and screen gives a useful end-to-end measurement; simplified input-to-color tests help isolate overhead from the game. Pipelining can increase throughput while adding frames of delay, as with Rage weapon animation. The right latency target depends on the input and user experience, not an FPS benchmark alone. - **documented**: Network prediction and buffering. QuakeWorld moved toward unreliable packets and local prediction to improve modem responsiveness, but Carmack quickly narrowed an initial prediction scheme: about 300 ms produced unacceptable interaction inconsistencies in a twitch game, while less than 100 ms appeared useful for smoothing. In 1999 he still preferred client-predicted control despite remote jerkiness. Avoid overfilling network queues; reducing update rate can be preferable to seconds of accumulated lag. The historical figures are observations for those games and networks, not modern protocol defaults. - **documented**: Reproducibility and simulation timing. Fixed steps reduce frame-rate dependence; variable steps accommodate arbitrary frame rates but introduce timing variation. In 1999 he described subdividing frames longer than 66 ms, accepting extra CPU cost to limit extremes. He had earlier regretted relying on partial journaling and adaptive timing without a complete replay solution. A later Doom 3 attempt to unify saves, demos and networking through synchronous execution failed on consistency and slow replay-based loading, so determinism is valuable but not automatically a cheap universal architecture. - **documented**: Graphics hardware and APIs. Evaluate features, throughput, image quality, drivers, tools and actual workload together. In 1996 Carmack favored OpenGL's procedural interface over contemporary Direct3D execute buffers while acknowledging Direct3D's consumer driver coverage. He later said Direct3D improved and was better in some periods. Low-level access can expose useful capabilities, but a new API does not automatically transform an engine; architecture and deployment effort determine whether the benefit is realized. - **documented**: Rasterization and ray tracing. Carmack has repeatedly found ray tracing attractive for understandable shadows and reflections, while judging production rasterization difficult to displace. In 2008 he considered rasterization much more efficient for conventional geometry on contemporary hardware; by 2013 he expected ray tracing eventually to win some uses but still required a compelling case to leave rasterization. A 720p real-time demonstration did not cover the costs of animation, dynamic geometry and blending in a complete game. ([carmack-7e889701cb5527](https://www.fabiensanglard.net/fd_proxy/doom3/pdfs/johnc-interviews.pdf) / evidence carmack-7e889701cb5527-a5c8cab81b42; [carmack-f9838b7edc089d](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P6UKhR0T6cs&t=3888) / evidence carmack-f9838b7edc089d-9bd11fb2e294; [carmack-3363b418bd9a31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wt-iVFxgFWk&t=8751) / evidence carmack-3363b418bd9a31-beb87f090a93; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Geometry representations. Separate the question of tracing rays from the question of storing geometry as voxels or triangles. Carmack investigated sparse voxels as a route to detailed virtualized worlds, but later became less certain that they would replace meshes: existing artists and pipelines already produce triangles, and conversion itself has costs. More polygons or smoother surfaces are not automatically valuable; test visibility in representative scenes and close-ups before committing bandwidth and authoring effort. - **inferred**: Lighting and physical plausibility. Doom 3's unified dynamic lighting made moving objects and the environment obey consistent rules, but Carmack later found its hard shadows and lack of ambient light excessively stark. Rage's more pragmatic choices produced attractive environments but sacrificed some interactivity. His later guidance is to understand physical light transport and materials well enough to choose approximations deliberately, while giving artists predictable controls and fast feedback rather than treating physical correctness as the only goal. - **documented**: Virtual texturing. Large virtual textures give artists more direct control over unique surfaces, but working-set fragmentation, page feedback, streaming latency and content tools can dominate a clean demonstration. Rage's small visible fragments could each require a full 128-by-128 page. Carmack later judged hardware page replacement and feedback harder, and the advantage over an existing software system smaller, than he had expected. Test fast camera turns and constrained storage, not just continuous terrain and installed demos. - **documented**: Content tools and iteration. The technology is not complete until creators can use it predictably. Carmack valued interactive editing and shared runtime rendering because short feedback loops encourage experimentation; he contrasted sub-second editing with attempts separated by minutes or hours. But Doom 3 also taught him that faster tools do not necessarily shorten production: artists may spend the saved time achieving greater polish. Measure both iteration speed and the content quality actually reached. - **documented**: Mobile platform design. Build around the actual screen, controls, download constraints, thermals and audience rather than shrinking a PC game unchanged. Doom RPG used step-based movement and combat suited to phone buttons. In 2006 Carmack concluded that Orcs & Elves should have established its low-end core before adding high-end features; cutting down the ambitious version ran into a 280 KB Java limit and delayed the schedule. Dropping a minimum target can be reasonable, but explicitly count the users excluded. - **documented**: VR latency and reprojection. Carmack reported that roughly 20 ms motion-to-display latency made a headset world feel notably more stable than 40–50 ms in his experiments; this is a perceptual observation, not a universal safety threshold. Time warp samples orientation late and reprojects an existing image, reducing apparent rotational delay and decoupling display updates from scene rendering. It cannot fully repair translation, independently moving objects, missing image regions or hand geometry, and it depends on reliable scheduling. - **documented**: VR display persistence. Carmack initially expected fast refresh alone to be sufficient, then reversed course after Valve demonstrated a directly switchable low-persistence display. Low persistence reduces motion smear, but makes missed or repeated frames conspicuous and requires appropriate display timing. In the 2014 Gear VR context, he considered 120 Hz attractive partly because it divides evenly into 60 Hz and 24 Hz content; that compatibility argument does not establish a universal headset requirement. - **documented**: VR comfort and interaction. Visual motion that conflicts with vestibular cues can make locomotion uncomfortable even with excellent tracking. Carmack often favored physical turning and straight movement over continuous controller-driven yaw, while acknowledging variation between people and the appeal of more adventurous experiences. In Minecraft, smoothing acceleration improved comfort but added input delay; exploiting known jump trajectories offered a more targeted compromise. Comfort should be tested alongside responsiveness and enjoyment, not inferred from one person's tolerance. - **inferred**: VR tracking and hardware scope. Evaluate tracking by latency, precision, accuracy, coverage and failure behavior, not merely whether a demonstration follows motion. - **documented**: VR audio. Carmack replaced sophisticated game-audio processing when designers bypassed it and a smaller mixer proved more predictable. - **documented**: Functional style and state. Make inputs and outputs explicit and extract difficult computations into testable, preferably pure functions. Carmack recommends adopting functional techniques within C++ rather than assuming a whole team should switch languages. Purity is a continuum: disentangling a state-heavy subsystem can be more valuable than perfecting an already simple function. Value inputs help isolation, but a const reference is not thread-safe if another thread can mutate or free its underlying data; copying large buffers can also be too costly. - **documented**: Security and extension code. In 1997 Carmack valued native game DLLs for speed and debugging, estimating a 5–10 percent overall gain for full-size levels. For downloaded Quake III client mods he instead pursued a restricted virtual machine, trading interpreter cost and interface work for portability and isolation; later dynamic compilation improved performance while retaining checks. His security claims describe design intent, not independent proof. For browser graphics he separately argued that marginal speed is not worth sacrificing security. - **documented**: Game AI versus AI research. Enemy behavior should serve the intended player experience, not maximize tactical competence or algorithmic sophistication; simple Doom behavior could appear cunning, while highly effective enemies could undermine an action-game power fantasy. This does not imply that general intelligence is unimportant: Carmack distinguishes commercial encounter design from research into learning and agency. Games provide controlled research environments, but physical sensing and actuation introduce additional problems that simulation can hide. - **documented**: AGI and reinforcement learning. In 2025 Carmack described LLMs as useful but, in his judgment, not the whole answer to learning new capabilities; this is a research position, not an impossibility proof. He values diverse Atari games and the approximately 100,000-frame benchmark for exposing sample-efficiency weaknesses, while recognizing benchmark controversy. He also tests action delay, sequential forgetting and transfer separately, because a high score under immediate simulator control can conceal failures in continuous physical environments. - **documented**: Product scope and timeliness. Carmack later recanted an absolute preference for waiting indefinitely until a product is done. Rage's six-year cycle led him to favor reducing ambition and shipping earlier, potentially learning from two releases instead of one. That is not an endorsement of shipping a broken product: the operational choice is to re-scope around valuable, supportable capabilities and account for market changes, rather than treating a desired feature set and deadline as simultaneously fixed.
