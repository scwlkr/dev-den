# Dennis M. Ritchie: stories

Built from 30 accepted distinct works and 129,100 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: From B to C on the PDP-11. Ritchie reported that B's word-oriented model made character handling awkward and required pointer scale conversion on a byte-addressed machine. In 1971 he added a character type and changed the compiler from threaded code to PDP-11 machine code, creating an intermediate language he called NB. The motivation was practical fit and speed, with type safety and interface checking gaining importance later. The historical account does not make every later C feature an individual invention, and no NB source appears to survive. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-782e6bb24b19; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-386ca4d91206; [ritchie-02c48db77f1ae0](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/primevalC.html) / evidence ritchie-02c48db77f1ae0-8437d9a250d0; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Arrays inside structures. While developing C, Ritchie found that B-style stored array base pointers did not fit structures intended to describe data such as directory contents: there was no satisfactory place to store or initialize the pointers. His solution generated a pointer when an array name appeared in an expression rather than storing that pointer as part of the array. This enabled structured types while preserving much existing B code; programs that reassigned array origins needed repair. The episode shows compatibility achieved through a semantic change, not compatibility without cost. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-5c5b9dd1158f; [ritchie-50a70b87adf27f](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cman.pdf) / evidence ritchie-50a70b87adf27f-4bea9c72626f; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: The kernel rewrite and structures. Ritchie recalled that Thompson's first attempt to rewrite Unix in C failed before the language had structures; process and file tables were awkward to describe. Structures supplied the needed way to represent operating-system data, and the 1973 rewrite succeeded. The coauthored 1978 Unix paper reports a roughly one-third size increase against the earlier assembly system but also functional improvements. The comparison does not isolate the language change from those improvements. ([ritchie-56c00e367e631d](https://www.stroustrup.com/From-C-to-Cpp-dmr.pdf) / evidence ritchie-56c00e367e631d-50f223c8244a; [ritchie-c6c3e1227aedef](https://ftpmirror2.infania.net/sites/unixarchive/Documentation/OralHistory/transcripts/ritchie.htm) / evidence ritchie-c6c3e1227aedef-bd96c02dcd2a; [ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-06ce23761ef4; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Splitting logical and bitwise operations. Ritchie describes a B and earlier BCPL convention in which the same operators performed sequential truth tests in one context and bit operations in another. It preserved useful short-circuit behavior but was difficult to explain. At Alan Snyder's suggestion, Ritchie introduced separate logical operators. He also records an unfortunate precedence consequence from trying to ease conversion from B. The case supports making a semantic distinction visible while checking the compatibility compromises around it. ([ritchie-33d5c334116698](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bcpl.html) / evidence ritchie-33d5c334116698-744887b35c39; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-9ed25ca58e0a; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Device files and shared credit. In Ritchie's recollection, Thompson, R. H. Canaday, and he sketched the filesystem design, with most of it attributable to Thompson. Ritchie tentatively claimed the idea of representing devices as files. The resulting design reused names, read/write conventions, and protection for devices and ordinary files. His attribution is a qualified memory, not proof that he alone designed the filesystem or every later extension of the model. ([ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hist.html) / evidence ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9-9e97afe4d556; [ritchie-c6c3e1227aedef](https://ftpmirror2.infania.net/sites/unixarchive/Documentation/OralHistory/transcripts/ritchie.htm) / evidence ritchie-c6c3e1227aedef-7808dcb753ce; [ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-1d7ab0b69aef; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Pipelines and the spelling checker. Ritchie credits McIlroy with persistent advocacy of Unix pipes and identifies an earlier McIlroy proposal as historical background. In his retrospective, a spelling checker combines existing translation, sorting, and comparison tools with a dictionary and derivative generator. Ritchie's lesson is that ordinary commands gain new uses through a shared stream interface. The example does not mean every task fits a linear pipeline; his account notes notation and deadlock difficulties for general graphs. ([ritchie-1bd73c8b9f492d](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/mdmpipe.html) / evidence ritchie-1bd73c8b9f492d-0caad08c1037; [ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hist.html) / evidence ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9-d728fd6527c4; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-ac37ba2fc657; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Recursive shells exposed file-offset ownership. Ritchie recounts an early design in which each command inherited an unchanged process-local output offset. In a recursive shell, a later command could consequently overwrite output from an earlier one. The solution was a system table holding open-file I/O pointers independently of the process that opened the file. The concrete lesson is to test whether inherited state represents a shared open instance or an independent copy; this account does not identify an individual implementer of the repair. ([ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hist.html) / evidence ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9-be7497928907; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Interdata portability exposed a hidden coroutine dependency. During the first kernel port, a process-exchange mechanism relied on an assumption about compiler-generated return sequences that happened to hold on the PDP-11 but not on Interdata. After about a week, Ritchie and Steve Johnson stopped trying to adjust the compiler and redesigned the control-passing primitives. Separately, the 1978 portability paper credits Thompson with much of the operating-system porting work. The incident is a warning about undocumented compiler/runtime contracts, not evidence that every porting problem was a hardware issue. ([ritchie-c3458b73539dc9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/odd.html) / evidence ritchie-c3458b73539dc9-bc85a8489992; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-5627628b41e7; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: The portability project separated checking from compilation. Johnson and Ritchie describe lint as a separate tool for detecting dubious constructs, including pointer/integer associations, without preventing a compiler from producing code or making a checker bug block ordinary work. Their porting account says declarations and replacement of constants with parameters accounted for much of the effort, and credits Thompson for the described kernel portability work. The approach is useful for migrating existing code where immediately outlawing all suspicious constructs would be impractical; warnings still require review. ([ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-2032cd3e9b85; [ritchie-8aefaea5632426](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/firstport.html) / evidence ritchie-8aefaea5632426-0ebc410f4808; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-5627628b41e7; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: A compatibility choice that did not travel. Johnson and Ritchie report that their IBM/370 implementation adopted a local BLISS calling convention to reuse its I/O library. They later judged this unwise: the library was not useful in other IBM environments, and the convention made some standard operating-system calls difficult. Their conclusion was not to reject compatibility categorically; an existing environment can justify call overhead or special conventions when the actual implementation goals warrant them. ([ritchie-5d25d1308143eb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html) / evidence ritchie-5d25d1308143eb-592a18f2cf69; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Microcode savings did not justify scarce resources. In the 1981 calling-sequence report Johnson and Ritchie describe trying writable microcode on Interdata. An argument-free call with no register variables improved by only 10–15 percent, with less improvement for more complicated procedures. They judged the gain insufficient given limited microstore and the need to load it before running a C operating system. The numbers apply to that experiment; the transferable decision is to compare a local speedup with the full resource and operational cost. ([ritchie-5d25d1308143eb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html) / evidence ritchie-5d25d1308143eb-fc0c58a1cf5c; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Streams traded code size for data and exposed new limitations. In his 1984 paper Ritchie reports a VAX system whose program text fell from about 130 KB to 106 KB after driver and protocol rewriting, while stream, queue, and block data used about 32 KB on the stated 32-user configuration, more than the comparison character lists. He considered the exchange desirable. Direct-terminal output was about 10 percent slower in one test while a network-terminal case improved, but he cautioned that properly comparable timing was unavailable. This is a configuration-specific trade, not a universal benchmark. ([ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-2ac80d3b8f12; [ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-6d82eac160f0; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Unicos guest testing favored debuggability over a perfect virtual machine. In joint work with David Slowinski, described in Ritchie's 1988 sole-authored paper, the team built a self-hosted guest facility so developers could reboot test systems without disrupting production users. Cray hardware did not support a general virtual machine, so the design used a specialized guest and placed much of the simulator in a user process, accepting lower efficiency for easier development and few kernel changes. Raw-disk writes required an exclusive test partition, and networking remained unsupported. It was a development tool, not a production virtualization claim. ([ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/unicos.html) / evidence ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d-0195e22bcecb; [ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/unicos.html) / evidence ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d-aab96e8b2349; [ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/unicos.html) / evidence ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d-d7a43f8c2fec; [ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/unicos.html) / evidence ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d-16f4a2a12e0b; [ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/unicos.html) / evidence ritchie-6d2bfd1e17da6d-858daa459e22; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: QED and the cost of expressive power. Ritchie wrote a GE time-sharing QED in assembly, compiling its regular expressions to machine code. He later described that version as unusually complicated and acknowledged criticism of its awkwardness as a scripting language. He regarded the much smaller expression repertoire of Thompson's early Unix ed as little practical loss, although later ed versions added complexity again. The case favors judging expressive features by actual use and implementation burden, not by formal power alone. ([ritchie-61c3ca31326df9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html) / evidence ritchie-61c3ca31326df9-fcc9f3f75f12; [ritchie-61c3ca31326df9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html) / evidence ritchie-61c3ca31326df9-46f43eb70436; [ritchie-61c3ca31326df9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html) / evidence ritchie-61c3ca31326df9-b62782898ace; [ritchie-61c3ca31326df9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html) / evidence ritchie-61c3ca31326df9-1b8eb614c9e1; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: M-209 cryptanalysis as a bounded experiment. Ritchie implemented an attack described by James Reeds: a statistical stage recovered wheel pins, and a more combinatorial stage recovered lugs, with contributions to the latter from Reeds and Robert Morris. He reported readable decryptions for about half the tested English messages longer than 2,000 characters and most above 2,500, using stated prior assumptions but no guessed plaintext. He explicitly distinguished this controlled experiment from a practical attack that could use guesses and feedback. The result is not a general success rate for arbitrary messages. ([ritchie-a77aa1fa81ba48](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/crypt.html) / evidence ritchie-a77aa1fa81ba48-c478322f0db3; [ritchie-a77aa1fa81ba48](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/crypt.html) / evidence ritchie-a77aa1fa81ba48-6c6614deddad; [ritchie-a77aa1fa81ba48](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/crypt.html) / evidence ritchie-a77aa1fa81ba48-63d42f498ceb; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: The Unix time horizon moved rather than vanished. Ritchie's commentary on the first-edition manual describes an early 32-bit time representation in sixtieths of a second from 1971, followed by epoch changes and the 1973 move to seconds from 1970. The latter delayed the overflow issue to 2038 rather than eliminating it. His role in this evidence is historical commentator; the passage does not establish him as the individual author of each change. The operational lesson is to calculate the new horizon explicitly when changing units or epochs. ([ritchie-6b65455de680f1](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/1stEdman.html) / evidence ritchie-6b65455de680f1-4861603320b5; 1 independent supporting works)
