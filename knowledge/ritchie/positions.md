# Dennis M. Ritchie: positions

Built from 31 accepted distinct works and 138,939 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: C as a systems language. Ritchie saw C as a practical way to express the programs his group needed, not as an ideal teaching language. Pointers and machine-grounded operations made low-level work possible, while sufficient abstraction allowed portability with care. In 1993 he also acknowledged that objections to array safety had substance. Choose C for its fit to a systems problem, not on the assumption that its power supplies safety by itself. ([ritchie-56c00e367e631d](https://www.stroustrup.com/From-C-to-Cpp-dmr.pdf) / evidence ritchie-56c00e367e631d-90569147f93d; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-cdac9257111c; [ritchie-983276307fabd9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hopl.html) / evidence ritchie-983276307fabd9-1253476f1779; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Type systems and changing priorities. In Ritchie's account, early C types addressed practical PDP-11 problems: characters, byte addressing, pointer scaling and anticipated floating-point hardware. Type safety and interface checking became more important later. By about 1977, portability work encouraged unsigned types, explicit conversions and discouragement of integer-pointer assignments. Do not project the later safety rationale backward as the sole original motive. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-782e6bb24b19; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-9518f1758b1e; [ritchie-50a70b87adf27f](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cman.pdf) / evidence ritchie-50a70b87adf27f-bde0a203fcf3; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Function prototypes. The early language omitted argument types partly because Ritchie avoided difficult checking across separately compiled files and had not fully absorbed the consequences of moving to a typed language. He later judged this a significant weakness. In 1989 he supported prototypes but warned that mixing old and new declarations could cause erroneous expectations about conversions; in 1993 he accepted the committee's compatibility compromise as difficult but justified. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-18f89eebbfe2; [ritchie-56c00e367e631d](https://www.stroustrup.com/From-C-to-Cpp-dmr.pdf) / evidence ritchie-56c00e367e631d-cc2ca5ccb053; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-bbb0a9f24a36; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Aliasing and optimization. Ritchie recognized that pointer aliasing makes optimization harder, particularly for array-derived arguments on vector machines. In the 1989 standards discussion he opposed a proposed no-alias facility because its rules were incorrect, not because the underlying optimization problem was imaginary. In 2000 he considered C99 restricted pointers useful. The practical distinction is between a real need and a proposed contract whose semantics have been made correct. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-6d90f6ed835c; [ritchie-56c00e367e631d](https://www.stroustrup.com/From-C-to-Cpp-dmr.pdf) / evidence ritchie-56c00e367e631d-c695af655ac1; [ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1605643/the-future-according-to-dennis-ritchie-a-2000-interview.html) / evidence ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8-541dde5ed009; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Const and hidden mutation. In the 1989 interview Ritchie distinguished a pointer's read-only access path from a guarantee that the underlying data cannot change through another path. He considered the committee's definition adequate for its stated read-only-storage purpose, while questioning whether its different intended meanings had been resolved. Do not infer a global immutability or optimization guarantee merely from a const-qualified access path. ([ritchie-56c00e367e631d](https://www.stroustrup.com/From-C-to-Cpp-dmr.pdf) / evidence ritchie-56c00e367e631d-c0fb38435c90; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Declarations that mirror use. The historical C manual explains a declarator by the expression form that accesses the declared object. In 1993 Ritchie defended this as a unifying principle while recognizing difficult nested forms and acknowledging a proposed alternative. In a later interview he was less certain overall. Use the analogy to understand declarations, but do not treat its logic as proof that every complex declaration is easy to read. ([ritchie-50a70b87adf27f](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cman.pdf) / evidence ritchie-50a70b87adf27f-fda9f7b91135; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-afda41a8616a; [ritchie-65e5badd1cd1d9](https://www.ibiblio.org/pub/linux/docs/linux-doc-project/linuxfocus/English/July1999/article79.html) / evidence ritchie-65e5badd1cd1d9-09982e3defb0; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Arrays and strings. Ritchie regarded C's strings as an economical application of general array rules, with a special case for literal initialization. He also named the costs: finding terminators can require a scan, few operations are built in, and storage management falls more heavily on users. The 1993 safety qualification matters: a compact language definition does not remove the burden of correct bounds and lifetime management. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-481181022e03; [ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-394d27ee788a; [ritchie-983276307fabd9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hopl.html) / evidence ritchie-983276307fabd9-1253476f1779; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Runtime-sized multidimensional arrays. Ritchie saw a real need in numerical-library interfaces, but existing array semantics made extensions difficult to fit. His September 1990 proposal carried adjustable bounds in pointers rather than declaring variable-sized objects, requiring restrictions so bounds were available at runtime. It also admitted an ergonomic cost: accessing through a whole-array pointer was awkward, while a first-row pointer lost the first bound. This was a proposal, not a description of modern C. ([ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html) / evidence ritchie-91fa14fdc827e6-0acff0562cfe; [ritchie-b899843558a2e0](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/vararray.html) / evidence ritchie-b899843558a2e0-145ac8d24205; [ritchie-b899843558a2e0](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/vararray.html) / evidence ritchie-b899843558a2e0-f7cd6fe63b4b; [ritchie-b899843558a2e0](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/vararray.html) / evidence ritchie-b899843558a2e0-66848952c2b7; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Evaluation order. The historical manual left ordinary expression evaluation order undefined while specifying ordering for selected operators. Johnson and Ritchie later explained that tighter argument-order requirements could impose inefficiency or compiler complexity on different stack organizations. For portable code, separate dependent side effects rather than assuming the order observed on one implementation. The historical wording should not be substituted for the rules of a current C standard. ([ritchie-50a70b87adf27f](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cman.pdf) / evidence ritchie-50a70b87adf27f-24a1124aa374; [ritchie-5d25d1308143eb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html) / evidence ritchie-5d25d1308143eb-d675acef5093; [ritchie-50a70b87adf27f](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cman.pdf) / evidence ritchie-50a70b87adf27f-ddac0747fbd7; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Portability requires separating assumptions. Early C code assumed comparable integer and pointer representations and interchangeable pointer forms; the portability work exposed machines where those assumptions failed. The 1978 paper also distinguished portable source from portable encoded files: differing byte order complicated transferring a filesystem image even when the programs themselves were insensitive to byte order. Audit interfaces, representations and stored data separately. ([ritchie-8aefaea5632426](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/firstport.html) / evidence ritchie-8aefaea5632426-a9d0a8393e52; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-f94692f59206; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-49e2f4e6e593; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Port the environment when it is the obstacle. Early work moving Unix tools found operating-system interfaces and file conventions more troublesome than the hardware or language differences themselves. Johnson and Ritchie therefore chose to move Unix, not merely imitate its primitives on each host. This reduces one class of incompatibility, but is not free: target-specific device, interrupt and memory-management work remains, and the project explicitly declined full compatibility with existing target software and formats. ([ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-ed5c5dc9c4f4; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-e0a32e7cfa46; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-ca6d166a6742; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-0a5cb5376f68; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Portable interface versus portable implementation. Ritchie's standard-I/O appendix gives efficiency, simplicity and a cross-machine interface as goals even where implementations differ. His 1989 account says the original interface took several attempts because target operating systems did not all have Unix-like read and write calls. Define the behavior callers need, then let each host supply an implementation; do not equate portability of the interface with unchanged implementation code. ([ritchie-fb84bd56b432fc](https://wolfram.schneider.org/bsd/7thEdManVol2/uprog/uprog.pdf) / evidence ritchie-fb84bd56b432fc-e273c87793e4; [ritchie-fb84bd56b432fc](https://wolfram.schneider.org/bsd/7thEdManVol2/uprog/uprog.pdf) / evidence ritchie-fb84bd56b432fc-b9da259f2ee9; [ritchie-56c00e367e631d](https://www.stroustrup.com/From-C-to-Cpp-dmr.pdf) / evidence ritchie-56c00e367e631d-f1675eadbb13; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: High-level implementation and measured cost. Ritchie and Thompson reported that the 1973 C rewrite made Unix easier to understand and modify, while its system size grew about one-third and functionality also increased. Ritchie's 1977 retrospective estimated typical C object-size inflation at 20–40 percent, explicitly as a guess rather than a comprehensive measurement, and discussed profiling hot code. The decision is a workload-specific exchange, not a universal performance multiplier. ([ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-06ce23761ef4; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-e3a7f531ddae; [ritchie-be88b7b72521cb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portpap.html) / evidence ritchie-be88b7b72521cb-4eaf89778fa3; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Uniform byte streams. Unix leaves ordinary file structure to the programs that use it; specialized formats can still exist above the basic interface. Ritchie argued in 1977 that a common text representation helps independently written programs interact. The abstraction does not eliminate physical costs: his 1972 notes recommend larger transfers for I/O-bound programs as an efficiency measure, not a logical requirement on file contents. ([ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-0db7c2c23d73; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-583a37c2b61f; [ritchie-f9fdc2ceaea02e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/notes.html) / evidence ritchie-f9fdc2ceaea02e-c8b8f22a4bcc; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Devices as files, with qualifications. The Unix paper credits the interface with common I/O, naming and protection for files and devices. Ritchie tentatively recalled contributing the device-file idea, while crediting most of the early filesystem design to Thompson. Uniformity is a design objective rather than a claim that every device behaves identically: his retrospective identifies read-ahead as a case where sharing an input stream between programs can fail. ([ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-1d7ab0b69aef; [ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hist.html) / evidence ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9-9e97afe4d556; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-aa6b408f4508; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Pipelines and composable output. Ritchie valued pipelines because the same ordinary commands can run alone or be connected, without special stream-module implementations. Output conventions matter: unnecessary headings or compulsory prompts can spoil downstream processing, although he allowed that some tables need headings. His account also recognizes the limit of linear notation and the danger of deadlock in more general pipe graphs with finite buffers. ([ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hist.html) / evidence ritchie-7ca8eec9ac8af9-d728fd6527c4; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-4a8ba6644246; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-ac37ba2fc657; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Shell-centralized policy. Redirection and filename expansion can be implemented once in the shell, giving commands consistent behavior without duplicating code. Ritchie also supplied a counterexample: expansion against locally present filenames cannot generally express restoration of files that are absent locally. Centralize only the policy for which the central layer has the information needed; keep exceptions visible rather than forcing them through an unsuitable convention. ([ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-39f880ab09d2; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-25ac5d9813cf; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Minimal primitives, not denial of missing needs. In 1977 Ritchie preferred to implement mechanisms using existing primitives and add a primitive when serious inefficiency was demonstrable. He nevertheless called the lack of general interprocess synchronization a weakness in some uses and identified genuine asynchronous-I/O needs. An undated interview adds a caution: the team's own limited needs led to omitted communication capabilities and general weaknesses. ([ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-efadf4f35931; [ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-8a9c03798f09; [ritchie-c6c3e1227aedef](https://ftpmirror2.infania.net/sites/unixarchive/Documentation/OralHistory/transcripts/ritchie.htm) / evidence ritchie-c6c3e1227aedef-0f75bf673e1d; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: File locking depends on the workload. In the Unix paper, Ritchie and Thompson rejected ordinary user-visible file locks as neither necessary nor sufficient in their environment: they lacked large independently updated single-file databases, and locking a file would not solve every editor-copy race. They retained internal interlocks for filesystem consistency and acknowledged that simultaneous writes could scramble content. This is not an argument against concurrency control for database workloads. ([ritchie-4495b38593615b](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html) / evidence ritchie-4495b38593615b-3b931cb731a0; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Distributed resources as files. In the 1999 Styx paper, Pike and Ritchie advocate hierarchical resource names and a common protocol for local or remote access. A window's client-specific mouse file illustrates both access control and network export; these are dynamic resources, not necessarily disk files. The authors favor identical local and network behavior, but explicitly rely on reliable byte-stream transport and discuss authentication and optional channel protection rather than treating naming as security by itself. ([ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0](https://inferno-os.org/inferno/papers/styx.html) / evidence ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0-6022d3c10326; [ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0](https://inferno-os.org/inferno/papers/styx.html) / evidence ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0-275b40a69016; [ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0](https://inferno-os.org/inferno/papers/styx.html) / evidence ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0-523ae9f6ea40; [ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0](https://inferno-os.org/inferno/papers/styx.html) / evidence ritchie-032d8ab9a984c0-d098bc84d03f; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Namespaces and kernel organization. Ritchie described Plan 9 as combining file-like resources, remote filesystems and process-group-private namespaces. In a 1999 interview he preferred a structured common namespace and resource-access mechanisms to making the microkernel versus monolithic distinction the primary criterion. He still allowed that a microkernel might help internal structuring and that small specialized operating systems could suit appliances. ([ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1605643/the-future-according-to-dennis-ritchie-a-2000-interview.html) / evidence ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8-f0e92c38f62e; [ritchie-65e5badd1cd1d9](https://www.ibiblio.org/pub/linux/docs/linux-doc-project/linuxfocus/English/July1999/article79.html) / evidence ritchie-65e5badd1cd1d9-4c4d7139c5de; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Security is operational as well as architectural. In 1977 Ritchie warned that a theoretically adequate permission scheme could fail through unsafe defaults, raw-disk access or writable privileged programs; he also noted denial-of-service and lack of formal kernel verification. The Inferno coauthors later described restricting an application's resources through its namespace and controlling additions to it. Treat that as the intended model, not independent proof that an implementation has no bypasses. ([ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-b4d77bdd26bf; [ritchie-53a93514dbf681](https://www.inferno-os.org/inferno/papers/bltj.html) / evidence ritchie-53a93514dbf681-ef40bb86f774; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Streams trade copying for boundary complexity. In the 1984 stream-I/O paper, Ritchie chose references to blocks so data need not be copied at every module boundary and many characters can be transferred together. The price is that a module must handle arbitrary chunk boundaries, including a protocol header split across blocks or combined with following data. The interface must specify this burden; a block is not automatically a complete logical record. ([ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-7cba78c0a380; [ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-24812916f8e7; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Stream concurrency under resource constraints. Ritchie regretted not giving every processing module an independent blocking process, but estimated that systems might need as many as 1,000 queues and could not afford the associated storage. Servers instead saved state and returned. He also found that neither pure calls nor pure queueing sufficed. This is a concrete historical trade-off, not a general verdict that explicit state machines are always preferable to coroutines. ([ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-2cced736db5e; [ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-43286e64059c; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Measure the workload and expose weak comparisons. Ritchie's 1977 block-size example weighed a potentially much faster copy against roughly 15 percent more occupied space and possible cache losses, while warning that an idle-machine copy is an extreme, nonrepresentative test. His 1984 streams measurements likewise showed direct-terminal output slower and network-terminal output faster, with different implementation languages and no proper matched comparison. Neither result establishes a universal winner. ([ritchie-504320fb4a7e46](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html) / evidence ritchie-504320fb4a7e46-b879833d643e; [ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/st.html) / evidence ritchie-5cfbec4274be6e-6d82eac160f0; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Calling conventions must preserve runtime invariants. Johnson and Ritchie explain that stack state must remain valid at interruptible points: for example, reserve a frame before saving state into it, and restore needed state before releasing it. They also recommend designing unwinding before committing to a frame format. For structure returns, caller-owned space avoids both the vulnerability of a dead callee frame and the non-reentrancy of a static return area. ([ritchie-5d25d1308143eb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html) / evidence ritchie-5d25d1308143eb-96d903e24349; [ritchie-5d25d1308143eb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html) / evidence ritchie-5d25d1308143eb-50370e959c65; [ritchie-5d25d1308143eb](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html) / evidence ritchie-5d25d1308143eb-cd9025feb682; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Standards and language growth. In 2000 Ritchie valued standardization for public comment and a basis for challenging nonconformance, but worried that crowd-driven evolution could erode the unity associated with a small design group; he also warned against excessive early control. He preferred the compactness of the 1989/1990 C standard to C99, while accepting the need to evolve and noting he had not participated in the C99 work. ([ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1605643/the-future-according-to-dennis-ritchie-a-2000-interview.html) / evidence ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8-7674e7ad909f; [ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1605643/the-future-according-to-dennis-ritchie-a-2000-interview.html) / evidence ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8-541dde5ed009; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Language success is not just elegance. Ritchie argued in 1993 that users need an understandable definition, an available implementation and a language that connects to real tasks and environments. His 2000 account tentatively credited useful abstraction in Unix and C while also naming popular hardware and relatively liberal distribution. He explicitly stopped short of a definitive explanation. Do not infer adoption from technical merit alone. ([ritchie-983276307fabd9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hopl.html) / evidence ritchie-983276307fabd9-0adfa051a12e; [ritchie-983276307fabd9](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hopl.html) / evidence ritchie-983276307fabd9-07da9dc80445; [ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1605643/the-future-according-to-dennis-ritchie-a-2000-interview.html) / evidence ritchie-4cf698ce0647a8-7928b40bcccb; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Reliability and real-time response are separate properties. Ritchie's 1972 notes distinguish collecting terminal characters promptly from scheduling a user program quickly enough to act on them; the latter was constrained by the system described. His 1977 retrospective separately discusses uptime, preservation and correctness, and says crash recovery was not automatic. A responsive interface or healthy-looking process does not establish a deadline guarantee or durable storage.
