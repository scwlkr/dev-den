- **inferred**: Five key frameworks, inferred:
1. Port the environment: isolate machine dependencies, retarget the compiler, and test a substantial system on another architecture.
2. Test the abstract machine: compare pointer, character, stack, and calling assumptions with actual targets and their costs.
3. Move complexity above the primitive: share a narrow operation while retaining escape routes for cases it handles poorly.
4. Compose through standard streams: let the shell connect ordinary programs, preserving a separate diagnostic path.
5. Represent resources as files: use shared names, operations, protection, and namespaces, while testing dynamic and remote behavior. - **inferred**: Where this lens misfires: Minimalism fails when excluded coordination or safety requirements are central. The Unix paper’s rejection of user-visible locks reflected an environment without large shared databases, not a general database rule. Bring workload-specific safety, concurrency, and reliability evidence before applying this lens.
