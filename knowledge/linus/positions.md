# Linus Torvalds: positions

Built from 52 accepted distinct works and 220,774 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Userspace compatibility: Protect existing workflows, not merely the formal ABI. An ordinary fix or improvement that breaks a working application calls for another solution, even when the application relied on accidental behavior. In 2019 he reverted an I/O improvement that exposed an older bug. Serious security hazards and unsustainable support for obsolete architectures can justify exceptions; neither makes routine breakage acceptable. ([linus-304b9f77dac0a3](https://lkml.org/lkml/2019/9/15/241) / evidence linus-304b9f77dac0a3-bebbf837b86a; [linus-304b9f77dac0a3](https://lkml.org/lkml/2019/9/15/241) / evidence linus-304b9f77dac0a3-ee78e066c175; [linus-08aa2a6a795956](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s4wlrxFf2lM&t=1965) / evidence linus-08aa2a6a795956-9e93745d3c21; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Compatibility and internal interfaces: User–kernel interfaces are expensive to repair because old applications remain in circulation. Internal subsystems can be rewritten while an adaptation layer preserves old behavior. In 1994 he described the cost of adding supersets and old-call paths; later he argued that internal scalability and complex algorithms can be larger sources of complexity than compatibility itself. ([linus-7bbc785cf8b52c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/interview-linus-author-linux) / evidence linus-7bbc785cf8b52c-215a31d98f05; [linus-afd2746598daa0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nn-SGblUhi4&t=800) / evidence linus-afd2746598daa0-16041f205bb3; [linus-6578a80cdda3fc](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/25-years-later-interview-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-6578a80cdda3fc-0e4adc26648e; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Configuration migration: A reorganization must preserve the behavior of existing configurations rather than simply enable every new option. In his 2025 sound Kconfig review, Torvalds proposed using the old option as the default for split functionality while keeping genuinely new subdrivers disabled. He also warned that moving between revisions during bisection can silently lose configuration, even when forward migration looks adequate. ([linus-546ff318d2ab76](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgO0Rx2LcYT4f75Xs46orbJ4JxO2jbAFQnVKDYAjV5HeQ@mail.gmail.com/) / evidence linus-546ff318d2ab76-dc9b820eb0eb; [linus-546ff318d2ab76](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgO0Rx2LcYT4f75Xs46orbJ4JxO2jbAFQnVKDYAjV5HeQ@mail.gmail.com/) / evidence linus-546ff318d2ab76-9e9111c734b1; [linus-546ff318d2ab76](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgO0Rx2LcYT4f75Xs46orbJ4JxO2jbAFQnVKDYAjV5HeQ@mail.gmail.com/) / evidence linus-546ff318d2ab76-fe6bd8eecd76; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Releases and unresolved regressions: Prefer short, predictable, staggered cycles to a large batch followed by indefinite stabilization. In 2007 he argued that waiting for every tracked regression can accumulate a larger problem for the next release; experimental, development and stable trees can overlap. This is not permission to ignore a known serious breakage: remove an unfixable feature, and let stable maintainers continue work on remaining issues. ([linus-bd03dce03d41ae](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/regression_tracking.html) / evidence linus-bd03dce03d41ae-dd807e8617bc; [linus-bd03dce03d41ae](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/regression_tracking.html) / evidence linus-bd03dce03d41ae-2ec64cb3338f; [linus-d30f26be4ebd06](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf) / evidence linus-d30f26be4ebd06-43723a478b9c; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Merge windows and late changes: The release process needs both a period for tested features and a quieter period for fixes. His 2021 account describes roughly two weeks for new code and six to eight weekly release candidates, with unresolved changes sometimes disabled or reverted. He has also accepted a clock pull containing many recent commits while warning against the practice; the figures in that case were not a universal cutoff. ([linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-3cd3b96ad638; [linus-375e3883627248](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/179338320614.1671436.10546377232510425224.pr-tracker-bot@kernel.org/T/) / evidence linus-375e3883627248-2c969f6ae19a; [linus-414fed225795f2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OvuEYtkOH88&t=218) / evidence linus-414fed225795f2-8277ab0f5d80; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Integration and review: Integrate worthwhile work early enough for people to inspect and test it in context, but do not treat early merging as acceptance of poor code. In 2008 he favored pipelined testing and integration trees over review-count gates that could add latency or be gamed. At the top of the chain, he expects prior review and examines pull explanations and history rather than micromanaging every subsystem. ([linus-b84c7a5bad786d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/development_speed.html) / evidence linus-b84c7a5bad786d-9fbb24f3e64a; [linus-b84c7a5bad786d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/development_speed.html) / evidence linus-b84c7a5bad786d-3db12e30a84d; [linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-a04435751c4e; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Maintainer trust: Large projects scale through people whose judgment is established by work, not through one person checking every line. A maintainer must evaluate other people's approaches, communicate context and sustain availability or arrange coverage. Trust includes accepting some decisions the integrator might not have made personally. Specialized subsystems with only one knowledgeable person create a particular continuity risk. ([linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=1756) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-c2aca2c0a832; [linus-414fed225795f2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OvuEYtkOH88&t=758) / evidence linus-414fed225795f2-9e7eb9a8c281; [linus-414fed225795f2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OvuEYtkOH88&t=892) / evidence linus-414fed225795f2-b3af3a61275f; [linus-08aa2a6a795956](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s4wlrxFf2lM&t=693) / evidence linus-08aa2a6a795956-c4ee00ef10c3; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Pull requests and conflict review: A useful pull explanation gives the integrator the purpose and shape of a change and a route back to relevant history if something fails. A merge summary should emphasize meaningful changes rather than enumerate every minor cleanup. Conflicts deserve inspection because they reveal concurrent work in the same area that may have incompatible assumptions; mechanical resolution alone is not the whole review. ([linus-f5107ddc45b2bc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRHqRWINWFQ&t=518) / evidence linus-f5107ddc45b2bc-8b95f747ccc4; [linus-375e3883627248](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/179338320614.1671436.10546377232510425224.pr-tracker-bot@kernel.org/T/) / evidence linus-375e3883627248-a0622ba3e0bb; [linus-f5107ddc45b2bc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRHqRWINWFQ&t=640) / evidence linus-f5107ddc45b2bc-4a596734a3da; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Distributed version control: Local repositories allow experimentation, commits and backups without central write permission. BitKeeper showed Torvalds the value of distributed merging; Git grew from the need for that workflow with sufficient speed and integrity. Distribution reduces the politics of commit access, not all politics. He acknowledged in 2007 that centralized systems can work in tightly controlled organizations. ([linus-09876410887a34](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/blog/10-years-of-git-an-interview-with-git-creator-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-09876410887a34-3dc71f0bf923; [linus-09876410887a34](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/blog/10-years-of-git-an-interview-with-git-creator-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-09876410887a34-ce0b96d75883; [linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=1192) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-44c5b78c80eb; [linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=2319) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-150661c73b73; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Rebasing and published history: In his 2009 guidance, rebasing was useful for a private leaf developer's patch queue, including his own Git work. The objection concerns maintainers rewriting other people's commits or publishing newly rebased work as if earlier testing covered the rewritten result. For a published branch not expected to be rebased, he suggested retaining it and making a replacement branch from the common point. ([linus-2ebac3ff7df379](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/git_basic.html) / evidence linus-2ebac3ff7df379-10bf3eca7824; [linus-2ebac3ff7df379](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/git_basic.html) / evidence linus-2ebac3ff7df379-7727689b7db4; [linus-13f9fd772b4e8a](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/git_branches.html) / evidence linus-13f9fd772b4e8a-f9a1a58697ed; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Git usability and scale: He conceded in 2015 that early Git deserved its difficult reputation because core functionality preceded polish. Start with basic operations; adopt commits and branches when work requires durable, stacked changes rather than a collection of independent stashes. He also acknowledged that very large monorepositories and large files were not the original design target, without claiming those users were wrong to choose them. ([linus-09876410887a34](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/blog/10-years-of-git-an-interview-with-git-creator-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-09876410887a34-a7ddd8b53728; [linus-2ebac3ff7df379](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/git_basic.html) / evidence linus-2ebac3ff7df379-ccc5eda17771; [linus-a8bb54066c8b4f](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCr_gb8rdEI&t=2212) / evidence linus-a8bb54066c8b4f-9fb81915fedd; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: C and Rust: In 2016 he doubted language choice would solve the hardest kernel problems; in 2019 he saw no better replacement for C in the kernel proper. By 2021 he supported evaluating Rust as an addition, not a wholesale replacement, and in 2023 expected integration to take years. He reads some Rust but does not claim expertise to maintain it himself. C remains personally simpler, while its simplicity also permits mistakes. ([linus-b6d2497d71a413](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2247741/linux-at-25-linus-torvalds-on-the-evolution-and-future-of-linux-2.html) / evidence linus-b6d2497d71a413-bbc23a18cad9; [linus-6578a80cdda3fc](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/25-years-later-interview-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-6578a80cdda3fc-d1f0156a032b; [linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-c1302882d239; [linus-414fed225795f2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OvuEYtkOH88&t=360) / evidence linus-414fed225795f2-2644ba0cd035; [linus-414fed225795f2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OvuEYtkOH88&t=523) / evidence linus-414fed225795f2-1d5f2f2c3d7f; [linus-caebe7efd979f4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OM_8UOPFpqE&t=651) / evidence linus-caebe7efd979f4-39f7e5bf461d; 5 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Language safety and tooling: Kernel C is not just the language specification: infrastructure, static checks, locking checks and slower debug configurations can find classes of errors. Torvalds values those tools without claiming they provide all of Rust's safety. His sparse example adds an orthogonal user-pointer annotation to the normal pointee type, making an otherwise accidentally working misuse visible to analysis. ([linus-caebe7efd979f4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OM_8UOPFpqE&t=835) / evidence linus-caebe7efd979f4-b588ff2508e7; [linus-957a3fd5175e48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKkEe-PxW10&t=1119) / evidence linus-957a3fd5175e48-0bcafaa6b9e4; [linus-10d31f031745b3](https://lwn.net/Articles/37719/) / evidence linus-10d31f031745b3-7238dd44f167; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Code readability and style: Favor small, understandable functions, shallow nesting and expressions whose meaning is immediately visible. In 2007 he distinguished genuine boolean tests from negative error values and preferred retaining informative error codes. Style is not an excuse for indiscriminate churn: surrounding shared code should match, while a future maintainer of an isolated driver may have more latitude. ([linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-49f3a2e6132d; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-27dd6527623c; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-2c904a10d21d; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-7fab6b349365; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Patch structure and history: Separate code movement from cleanup so reviewers can establish whether the move changed behavior and history tools have a better chance of following it. Whitespace-aware history does not solve every case, especially when lines are split. This is a reviewability and debugging trade-off, not a demand that style never be improved. ([linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-e082a0f3fa65; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-2c904a10d21d; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Documentation and comments: His personal preference is readable, well-named code and small functions over explaining confusing code with comments. He has explicitly distinguished that preference from a claim that documentation is bad, and recognized that a later, larger kernel has a different context from his original one-person codebase. Division of work can let others produce documentation he does not personally prioritize. ([linus-d2e94cff4da2ec](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/documentation.html) / evidence linus-d2e94cff4da2ec-21c631ed6a00; [linus-d2e94cff4da2ec](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/documentation.html) / evidence linus-d2e94cff4da2ec-e808cbcd6cfc; [linus-d30f26be4ebd06](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf) / evidence linus-d30f26be4ebd06-d10fe522f076; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Locking and memory ordering: In 2005 he cautioned that avoiding locks does not automatically avoid cost: barriers may approach locking costs, and ordering differs across architectures. In 2009 he preferred an existing reader/writer semaphore for a suspend/resume proposal, citing established semantics, lockdep and architecture-specific optimization. Specialized lock-free designs can be justified, but their ordering and usage assumptions must be demonstrated. ([linus-1b5c4554df6af6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/locking.html) / evidence linus-1b5c4554df6af6-f935e0743a7f; [linus-1b5c4554df6af6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/locking.html) / evidence linus-1b5c4554df6af6-7d3fb615805b; [linus-1b5c4554df6af6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/locking.html) / evidence linus-1b5c4554df6af6-ee167993c723; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Lock granularity: Finer locking can improve multiprocessing scalability, but granularity itself has overhead. In 2001 he argued that important areas had moved beyond a big kernel lock without going so fine that locking degraded performance. The operational question is therefore where contention and lock costs actually fall, not whether the design has the largest possible number of locks. ([linus-169e2539c1a323](https://www.osnews.com/story/161/interview-with-linus-torvalds/) / evidence linus-169e2539c1a323-d20ff5c26a8c; [linus-1b5c4554df6af6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/locking.html) / evidence linus-1b5c4554df6af6-f935e0743a7f; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: System boundaries and layering: Evaluate a split by the complexity of the whole system, including communication, coherence, cross-boundary races and debugging. His 2003 criticism of filesystem separation did not reject layering: he praised a shared VFS cache and page-table abstractions. Choose boundaries with simpler interface requirements, and recognize where a boundary hides state needed by the other side. ([linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-5f33ec5cd62a; [linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-64b21bcb6d81; [linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-d82a2eb30c3a; [linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-74c1c8c43a7f; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Userspace filesystems and memory pressure: Moving a filesystem into a process does not remove the circular dependency between filesystem I/O and VM reclaim. In 2004 he noted that clients can consume memory needed by the filesystem daemon, and dirty pages complicate recovery. Limits, restricted behavior and allocation-context interfaces each have costs; access to emergency reserves is not safe unless the process is actually freeing memory. ([linus-3f0355fb73006d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/user_space_filesystems.html) / evidence linus-3f0355fb73006d-bf563b80f2dc; [linus-3f0355fb73006d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/user_space_filesystems.html) / evidence linus-3f0355fb73006d-e70b50923301; [linus-3f0355fb73006d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/user_space_filesystems.html) / evidence linus-3f0355fb73006d-d76f792c1348; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Device interrupts: For the hardware discussed in 2006, a userspace driver could poll, or rely on a kernel handler able to identify and silence its device. Merely deferring interrupt delivery leaves a device capable of repeatedly asserting the interrupt. He qualified the objection: hardware designed with a generic shutdown mechanism could change what a generic kernel handler can do. ([linus-bc3350d7a86cc7](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/userspace_io.html) / evidence linus-bc3350d7a86cc7-16875ca12e45; [linus-bc3350d7a86cc7](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/userspace_io.html) / evidence linus-bc3350d7a86cc7-bb5a7f87b6b8; [linus-bc3350d7a86cc7](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/userspace_io.html) / evidence linus-bc3350d7a86cc7-b6b6d5832c84; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Performance measurement: Test the whole workload and reconstruct cache state and execution order before attributing a result to one instruction. A lookup table can lose when it competes for cache, and an x86 sampling hit can appear after the instruction that caused the stall. His historical performance judgments are hypotheses to retest on the actual processor and workload, not current universal rankings. ([linus-6dc86ad458a6f3](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/lookup_tables.html) / evidence linus-6dc86ad458a6f3-bbeceaa592db; [linus-b523a1fcec4f83](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/benchmarks.html) / evidence linus-b523a1fcec4f83-151d116d00ce; [linus-ea06fcd26daea6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/page_zeroing_strategy.html) / evidence linus-ea06fcd26daea6-ac225a8ada9b; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Branches and conditional moves: In 2007 he argued that a predictable branch on an aggressive out-of-order processor can remove the unused path from the critical path, while a conditional move can retain dependencies on both alternatives. He also accepted counterexamples: unpredictable control flow, code density, predictor pressure and in-order processors can favor a conditional move. The processor and workload decide. - **inferred**: Page sizes: Larger pages can improve TLB behavior for specialized workloads but waste memory through fragmentation and can conflict with existing mapping semantics. His 2002 proposal treated giant pinned mappings as explicit, resource-accounted requests, while allowing that smaller superpages might eventually be transparent. His 2008–2009 arguments against broadly increasing base page size were workload- and architecture-specific, not a rejection of every large-page use. - **inferred**: Diagnostics and fatal assertions: For ordinary driver failures, prefer useful output and an error return when possible. A fatal assertion that hangs a graphical machine or hides the diagnostic defeats debugging. His 2002 qualification is important: fundamental corruption with no workable recovery can justify a fatal stop. Full crash dumps also have hardware, reliability and sensitive-data risks; controlled environments may make different choices. ([linus-8d3470fe28fc23](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/BUG.html) / evidence linus-8d3470fe28fc23-bc840ef0a586; [linus-8d3470fe28fc23](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/BUG.html) / evidence linus-8d3470fe28fc23-ece795f10f3f; [linus-8d3470fe28fc23](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/BUG.html) / evidence linus-8d3470fe28fc23-ac8d9ba5a257; [linus-c25299d35015fd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/crash_dumps.html) / evidence linus-c25299d35015fd-9e7aa32b1d66; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Hardware support and ports: A viable port needs interested developers, actual hardware and usable documentation; in 1994 he specifically rejected documentation constrained by nondisclosure. CPU architecture is only part of the work: in 1995 he expected peripherals, drivers and missing documentation to be harder for PowerPC. Continue old hardware support while it has practical users, but weigh the recurring cost when support becomes effectively unused. ([linus-7bbc785cf8b52c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/interview-linus-author-linux) / evidence linus-7bbc785cf8b52c-6bf828c6632c; [linus-05353192da10a1](https://www.linuxjournal.com/article/36) / evidence linus-05353192da10a1-3389ddde8d44; [linus-957a3fd5175e48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKkEe-PxW10&t=263) / evidence linus-957a3fd5175e48-b56ed7184092; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: AI-assisted development: He has moved from hoping models might flag suspicious review patterns to reporting that automated review sometimes finds issues but is sometimes poor. Generated reports need human validation and an accountable participant who can discuss and help fix the problem. He welcomes discovery of real old bugs, while warning that plausible false reports and superficial fixes consume maintainer attention. - **inferred**: Planning and evolution: Start from useful foundations and respond to actual hardware and user needs, while leaving room to change course.
