# Linus Torvalds: positions

Built from 34 accepted distinct works and 159,314 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Userspace compatibility. Torvalds treats a regression as disruption to an existing user's workflow, not merely a changed ABI or a newly introduced defect. An ordinary improvement that exposes an old bug can still need reverting. Security hazards and the cost of supporting obsolete hardware are qualified exceptions, not a general excuse for breaking users. His 2019 release decision and later explanation support the same practical rule. ([linus-304b9f77dac0a3](https://lkml.org/lkml/2019/9/15/241) / evidence linus-304b9f77dac0a3-bebbf837b86a; [linus-08aa2a6a795956](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s4wlrxFf2lM&t=1965) / evidence linus-08aa2a6a795956-9e93745d3c21; [linus-afd2746598daa0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nn-SGblUhi4&t=132) / evidence linus-afd2746598daa0-43561305801d; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Compatibility versus internal change. External behavior deserves protection, but internal interfaces and subsystems can be reworked substantially. In 2019 he described repeated incremental rewrites in a unified tree and said out-of-tree drivers should not constrain internal development. A compatibility layer can present old behavior over a new implementation. This distinction does not imply that every internal change is cheap or safe. ([linus-6578a80cdda3fc](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/25-years-later-interview-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-6578a80cdda3fc-0e4adc26648e; [linus-afd2746598daa0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nn-SGblUhi4&t=800) / evidence linus-afd2746598daa0-16041f205bb3; [linus-7bbc785cf8b52c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/interview-linus-author-linux) / evidence linus-7bbc785cf8b52c-215a31d98f05; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Configuration migration. In his 2025 sound Kconfig review, Torvalds objected to losing an existing setup when options were reorganized. He wanted old choices to supply defaults for the new structure, but not indiscriminately enable unrelated new subdrivers. He also noted that changing names can silently lose configuration during bisection. The cost of preserving behavior belongs to the reorganization, rather than being transferred to users. ([linus-546ff318d2ab76](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgO0Rx2LcYT4f75Xs46orbJ4JxO2jbAFQnVKDYAjV5HeQ@mail.gmail.com/) / evidence linus-546ff318d2ab76-dc9b820eb0eb; [linus-546ff318d2ab76](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgO0Rx2LcYT4f75Xs46orbJ4JxO2jbAFQnVKDYAjV5HeQ@mail.gmail.com/) / evidence linus-546ff318d2ab76-9e9111c734b1; [linus-546ff318d2ab76](https://lore.kernel.org/all/CAHk-=wgO0Rx2LcYT4f75Xs46orbJ4JxO2jbAFQnVKDYAjV5HeQ@mail.gmail.com/) / evidence linus-546ff318d2ab76-fe6bd8eecd76; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: New interfaces. Avoid a syscall or other durable interface merely because it can be implemented. In 2003 he favored a stable, portable base and wanted evidence of application demand before adding a hypothetical interface. His earlier account of syscall mistakes explains the cost: later improvements may need superset semantics and a compatibility path for the old call. A compelling actual use can outweigh that cost. ([linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-7d16515eeddc; [linus-7bbc785cf8b52c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/interview-linus-author-linux) / evidence linus-7bbc785cf8b52c-215a31d98f05; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: C and Rust over time. In 2016 Torvalds was unconvinced that choosing Rust solved the hardest kernel problems; in 2019 he saw no better replacement for C in the kernel proper. By 2021 he supported evaluating Rust as an addition for selected drivers and possibly other components, not a wholesale replacement. In 2024 he considered the integration too young to declare a failure. These are changing assessments, not proof that he considered language safety irrelevant. ([linus-b6d2497d71a413](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2247741/linux-at-25-linus-torvalds-on-the-evolution-and-future-of-linux-2.html) / evidence linus-b6d2497d71a413-bbc23a18cad9; [linus-6578a80cdda3fc](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/25-years-later-interview-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-6578a80cdda3fc-d1f0156a032b; [linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-c1302882d239; [linus-caebe7efd979f4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OM_8UOPFpqE&t=758) / evidence linus-caebe7efd979f4-1a62bc61b8c0; 4 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: C safety is more than the language. Torvalds finds C comparatively simple but acknowledges that its simplicity makes mistakes easy. Kernel C also relies on static checks, locking infrastructure and slower debug builds that test additional properties. He does not claim that these measures provide all of Rust's safety. Assess the language together with its tools, integration costs and the specific code being written. ([linus-caebe7efd979f4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OM_8UOPFpqE&t=651) / evidence linus-caebe7efd979f4-39f7e5bf461d; [linus-caebe7efd979f4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OM_8UOPFpqE&t=835) / evidence linus-caebe7efd979f4-b588ff2508e7; [linus-957a3fd5175e48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKkEe-PxW10&t=1119) / evidence linus-957a3fd5175e48-0bcafaa6b9e4; [linus-b6d2497d71a413](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2247741/linux-at-25-linus-torvalds-on-the-evolution-and-future-of-linux-2.html) / evidence linus-b6d2497d71a413-ea18807893cf; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Release cadence. His later release model takes ready work during an approximately two-week merge window, then spends roughly six to eight weekly release candidates stabilizing it. The 2008 account explains the trade-off: too short a window impedes integration, while a long stabilization period risks losing momentum. The numbers describe his process, not universal thresholds. A problematic late feature can be disabled or reverted. ([linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-3cd3b96ad638; [linus-d30f26be4ebd06](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf) / evidence linus-d30f26be4ebd06-34729162ca0d; [linus-d30f26be4ebd06](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf) / evidence linus-d30f26be4ebd06-2955528971e9; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Late changes. A noncritical fix near release must justify the risk of introducing a fresh regression, especially when another release is reasonably close. Torvalds has acknowledged accepting too many unnecessary late fixes himself. In a separate clock pull he accepted the work but objected to 22 of 134 non-merge commits having been committed in the previous 48 hours. Those figures describe that pull, not a general numerical cutoff. ([linus-f5107ddc45b2bc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRHqRWINWFQ&t=258) / evidence linus-f5107ddc45b2bc-8e67db9c74c3; [linus-375e3883627248](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/179338320614.1671436.10546377232510425224.pr-tracker-bot@kernel.org/T/) / evidence linus-375e3883627248-2c969f6ae19a; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Review and integration. In 2008 Torvalds favored exposing worthwhile work early in integrated trees and pipelining testing, rather than making every safeguard a serial gate. He explicitly did not mean that poor code should be merged. At the top of the review chain, he expects prior review and relies on trusted maintainers, while examining explanations and history. Early exposure and stabilization serve different purposes. ([linus-b84c7a5bad786d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/development_speed.html) / evidence linus-b84c7a5bad786d-9fbb24f3e64a; [linus-b84c7a5bad786d](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/development_speed.html) / evidence linus-b84c7a5bad786d-3db12e30a84d; [linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-a04435751c4e; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Maintainer trust. A scalable project needs people who integrate and review others' work, not just one person applying every patch. Trust is earned through demonstrated judgment and interaction; it does not require agreement on every decision or depend on an employer. Maintainership is also not exclusive ownership: another contributor can cross a subsystem boundary after discussion. Specialized knowledge concentrated in one person remains a continuity risk. ([linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=1756) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-c2aca2c0a832; [linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-8524afdb307b; [linus-f5107ddc45b2bc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRHqRWINWFQ&t=818) / evidence linus-f5107ddc45b2bc-a7e2c1462382; [linus-08aa2a6a795956](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s4wlrxFf2lM&t=693) / evidence linus-08aa2a6a795956-c4ee00ef10c3; 4 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Pull-request explanations. At kernel scale, the top-level maintainer cannot understand every change in detail. Torvalds therefore asks for explanations that convey the larger purpose and help locate trouble later. A merge message should summarize meaningful categories rather than enumerate every small update; a useful list of new drivers may still belong. A conflict is also a reason to inspect whether independently developed changes have incompatible aims. ([linus-f5107ddc45b2bc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRHqRWINWFQ&t=518) / evidence linus-f5107ddc45b2bc-8b95f747ccc4; [linus-375e3883627248](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/179338320614.1671436.10546377232510425224.pr-tracker-bot@kernel.org/T/) / evidence linus-375e3883627248-a0622ba3e0bb; [linus-f5107ddc45b2bc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kRHqRWINWFQ&t=640) / evidence linus-f5107ddc45b2bc-4a596734a3da; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Distributed version control. Local repositories let people experiment and commit without first obtaining central write access, then share work for others to pull. This reduces one source of coordination politics, but Torvalds explicitly corrected the stronger claim that it eliminates politics. He also conceded in 2007 that centralized systems can work in tightly controlled companies. The right choice depends on workflow, latency and collaboration structure. ([linus-09876410887a34](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/blog/10-years-of-git-an-interview-with-git-creator-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-09876410887a34-ce0b96d75883; [linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=1192) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-44c5b78c80eb; [linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=2319) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-150661c73b73; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Version-control performance and integrity. Git's initial requirements were distributed operation, sufficient speed and data integrity. Torvalds wanted a series of 50–100 patches applied in roughly half a minute, rather than waiting about that long for each patch. His original motivation for hashing everything was detecting corruption, not security. The performance target was specific to his large-kernel workflow and the tools available at the time. ([linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=660) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-5bfb3e4403b9; [linus-a8bb54066c8b4f](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCr_gb8rdEI&t=492) / evidence linus-a8bb54066c8b4f-127a589f9360; [linus-a8bb54066c8b4f](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCr_gb8rdEI&t=542) / evidence linus-a8bb54066c8b4f-0233f8bc8142; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Repository scale and usability. A small conceptual core does not make every interface or workload simple. Torvalds acknowledged Git's early usability problems, advised learning basic operations first, and suggested separating genuinely distinct projects when appropriate. He also recognized that splitting history can be expensive and that huge monorepositories and large files were not his original design target. Those limitations do not prove another team's monorepo choice wrong. ([linus-09876410887a34](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/blog/blog/10-years-of-git-an-interview-with-git-creator-linus-torvalds) / evidence linus-09876410887a34-a7ddd8b53728; [linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=2848) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-8dbb623184fa; [linus-a8bb54066c8b4f](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCr_gb8rdEI&t=2212) / evidence linus-a8bb54066c8b4f-9fb81915fedd; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: System boundaries. In the 2003 microkernel discussion, Torvalds argued that splitting a component can simplify it locally while making the whole system harder to debug through communication, coherence and cross-address-space races. He preferred boundaries with simple interface requirements and corrected an earlier example when challenged. This is not opposition to all layering: he praised shared caches and low-overhead abstractions where they simplify the whole. ([linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-5f33ec5cd62a; [linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-64b21bcb6d81; [linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-d82a2eb30c3a; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: VFS and shared caching. Torvalds describes pathname lookup as a shared performance and correctness problem: caching, locking, serialization and mount traversal belong in common VFS machinery rather than independent implementations in each filesystem. Filesystems handle misses. He describes RCU and specialized locking in that context; the lesson is to centralize the coherence contract where duplication would be costly, not to centralize every operation. ([linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-5351acf8c496; [linus-9405a9d93440bb](https://yarchive.net/comp/microkernels.html) / evidence linus-9405a9d93440bb-d82a2eb30c3a; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Lock-free programming. In 2005 Torvalds warned that avoiding locks does not automatically avoid cost: memory barriers can be comparable in expense, and ordering mistakes can be architecture-dependent and subtle. Larger-than-word accesses can require locking or sequence-based mechanisms with barriers. Specialized architectures and workloads can differ. His position is a demand for a complete cost and correctness argument, not a ban on lock-free algorithms. ([linus-1b5c4554df6af6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/locking.html) / evidence linus-1b5c4554df6af6-f935e0743a7f; [linus-1b5c4554df6af6](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/locking.html) / evidence linus-1b5c4554df6af6-7d3fb615805b; [linus-8d5e5ae7254bad](https://www.tag1.com/blog/interview-linus-torvalds-linux-and-git/) / evidence linus-8d5e5ae7254bad-5351acf8c496; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Performance measurement. Isolated operation timings can mislead when they omit cache competition or fail to exercise the processor as real work does. In 2003 he argued that a lookup table could lose to a few instructions once cache misses were included; his old bogo-mips example similarly failed to represent Pentium superscalar performance. Neither example supplies a universal modern benchmark. Test the actual workload and hardware. ([linus-6dc86ad458a6f3](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/lookup_tables.html) / evidence linus-6dc86ad458a6f3-bbeceaa592db; [linus-6dc86ad458a6f3](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/lookup_tables.html) / evidence linus-6dc86ad458a6f3-7f3b05e6eb25; [linus-7bbc785cf8b52c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/interview-linus-author-linux) / evidence linus-7bbc785cf8b52c-fae60d15c9b4; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Branches versus conditional moves. In 2007 he argued that a well-predicted branch on an aggressive out-of-order CPU can avoid putting both alternatives and the condition on the critical path. Conditional moves may help for genuinely unpredictable branches, code density or different processor designs. He acknowledged in-order processors and decompression-like workloads as counterexamples. These historical architecture comparisons require fresh measurement on current machines. ([linus-7482471cf1a5ae](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/cmov.html) / evidence linus-7482471cf1a5ae-0e3c9babe467; [linus-7482471cf1a5ae](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/cmov.html) / evidence linus-7482471cf1a5ae-f831fb567888; [linus-7482471cf1a5ae](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/cmov.html) / evidence linus-7482471cf1a5ae-9aaff36f14ed; [linus-7482471cf1a5ae](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/cmov.html) / evidence linus-7482471cf1a5ae-d2fd71a200c8; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Lock granularity and scalability. Finer locks can improve multiprocessor concurrency, but in 2001 Torvalds cautioned that excessive granularity can itself hurt performance. Scalability also includes smaller systems, not just large NUMA machines. Before Linux 2.2 he checked small-machine behavior because developers concentrating on larger machines could let it decay. Evaluate both contention and overhead across the machines that matter. ([linus-169e2539c1a323](https://www.osnews.com/story/161/interview-with-linus-torvalds/) / evidence linus-169e2539c1a323-d20ff5c26a8c; [linus-169e2539c1a323](https://www.osnews.com/story/161/interview-with-linus-torvalds/) / evidence linus-169e2539c1a323-b64c572e1a1f; [linus-c4aaf9a0e2371c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/article/3103) / evidence linus-c4aaf9a0e2371c-673e523247fd; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Real-time and preemption. In 2001 he gave fully preemptible uniprocessor patches low priority, wanting real-world evidence and investigation of latency causes rather than assuming the patches improved performance. In 2024 he described real-time integration as a long cross-cutting effort requiring agreement and driver updates. The later account establishes integration difficulty, not that every concern from 2001 remained unchanged. ([linus-169e2539c1a323](https://www.osnews.com/story/161/interview-with-linus-torvalds/) / evidence linus-169e2539c1a323-72d9e8078276; [linus-caebe7efd979f4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OM_8UOPFpqE&t=479) / evidence linus-caebe7efd979f4-05522ca48b26; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Code readability and style. Torvalds prefers small, logical functions, shallow indentation and expressions that communicate their meaning immediately. He favors explicit negative-error tests and preserving useful error codes. But style enforcement should not create needless churn: shared code should match its surroundings, while a future maintainer's preferences can matter in an isolated driver. A long line can be clearer than a mechanically split one. ([linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-27dd6527623c; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-49f3a2e6132d; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-2c904a10d21d; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-7fab6b349365; [linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-e060cf8cf416; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Reviewable code movement. His 2007 advice is to separate movement from cleanup, so reviewers can verify that the move preserved behavior and history tools have a better chance of tracking it. Even whitespace-aware history has limits when lines are split. The operational implication is to make mechanical and semantic changes independently inspectable; it is not a claim that tooling can always recover intent. ([linus-0c0acf43d8adcd](https://yarchive.net/comp/linux/coding_style.html) / evidence linus-0c0acf43d8adcd-e082a0f3fa65; [linus-403284294e7bd8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XpnKHJAok8&t=4130) / evidence linus-403284294e7bd8-66a27114b4b6; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Hardware support and porting. He weighs active users and the ongoing burden of accounting for old hardware; effectively unused support can be removed when that burden becomes unreasonable. For a new port, interested developers, accessible hardware and technical documentation matter, including documentation usable without nondisclosure. His 1995 forecasts reflected those practical constraints rather than CPU difficulty alone. ([linus-957a3fd5175e48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKkEe-PxW10&t=263) / evidence linus-957a3fd5175e48-b56ed7184092; [linus-7bbc785cf8b52c](https://www.linuxjournal.com/content/interview-linus-author-linux) / evidence linus-7bbc785cf8b52c-6bf828c6632c; [linus-05353192da10a1](https://www.linuxjournal.com/article/36) / evidence linus-05353192da10a1-3389ddde8d44; [linus-05353192da10a1](https://www.linuxjournal.com/article/36) / evidence linus-05353192da10a1-d086933b88b9; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: AI-generated work. Torvalds sees useful applications in review and prototyping, while warning that plausible false reports and superficial patches consume maintainer time. For an LLM-discovered issue he wants a human to validate the report, propose a fix and participate in revisions. His account allows that simple generated patches can be fine and that capabilities may change; the continuing requirement is understanding and maintaining the result. ([linus-5b89eaae184ee3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3NSSGt9bZag&t=234) / evidence linus-5b89eaae184ee3-c89e8537073e; [linus-b7a12ab5a710c1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=InvaowP8XH8&t=319) / evidence linus-b7a12ab5a710c1-f60684b2f584; [linus-b7a12ab5a710c1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=InvaowP8XH8&t=463) / evidence linus-b7a12ab5a710c1-0f42701ce258; [linus-5b89eaae184ee3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3NSSGt9bZag&t=591) / evidence linus-5b89eaae184ee3-cd3b66df4e58; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Communication and disagreement. Torvalds prefers asynchronous email for technical discussion because it accommodates time zones and avoids interruption; archived threads let newcomers recover context. He also acknowledged that kernel-list criticism can become harsh enough to drive contributors away. A direct technical objection and an avoidable personal cost are separate considerations. His preference does not mean all kernel developers use the same communication tools.
