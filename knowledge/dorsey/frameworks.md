- **inferred**: Observe, infer, improve — unnamed. Parts: watch what people repeatedly do; infer the goal behind the workaround; make that goal easier; test the change. Dorsey describes Twitter users inventing replies, hashtags and retweets, and says he wrote code to recognize replies. Use this when actual behavior diverges from the product plan. Favor a recurring, useful practice over an isolated request, but do not assume popularity establishes safety: public replies also changed broadcasting into conversation with new problems. The choice to require recurrence is an inferred decision rule, not a numerical threshold. - **inferred**: Purpose before mechanism — unnamed. Parts: repeatedly ask why; state the customer outcome; map the full journey; choose the smallest set of tools that advances it. Dorsey reframed Square from a card reader to enabling a sale, then considered registers and capital. A useful test is whether a proposed feature helps the merchant complete or improve a sale, rather than merely improving the device. His customer narrative method turns that purpose into a story that engineering, design, operations and support can edit together. The test is an inferred application, not his prescribed score. - **inferred**: Constraint-to-interface design — unnamed. Parts: identify the real transport or hardware constraint; enumerate options; reject those that impose excessive cost or friction; then check the behavior the constraint encourages. Twitter's original 140 characters followed from a 160-character SMS with 20 reserved for the username, while also encouraging immediate expression. For the early Square reader, the team rejected card photography as awkward and the 30-pin connector's license cost, choosing the microphone jack and software decoding. These are historical decisions, not recommendations to reproduce obsolete interfaces. Evaluate present-day security and platform constraints independently. - **inferred**: Prototype, critique, graduate — unnamed. Parts: externalize an idea in a drawing, interaction or code; experience it; show it to others; form a hypothesis; test internally and then with a small audience; improve or shelve it. Dorsey describes Twitter's internal sandbox and small-percentage rollout, and Square's staged partner cohorts of 500, 1,000, 10,000 and 50,000. The cohort sizes are historical examples, not universal launch gates. Use this when uncertainty can be reduced safely by a bounded trial. Inferred gate: expand only when the evidence addresses the risk that caused the restriction, rather than treating uptake alone as proof of safety. - **inferred**: Operational visibility before speculation — unnamed. Parts: instrument resource use and activity; put meaningful measures on a shared dashboard; use the same evidence in engineering and operations discussions; revise the product or service in response. Dorsey says early Twitter outages exposed missing visibility and that his first Square code was an administrative dashboard. Use this especially for payments, where an outage or confusing flow can cost a seller a transaction. Inferred decision rule: choose metrics tied to customer and service consequences, not a generic dashboard count. Instrumentation narrows uncertainty; it does not by itself establish causation. - **inferred**: Integrated merchant loop — unnamed. Parts: accept payment; combine register and terminal to avoid duplicated work; capture useful transaction information; help the seller learn and grow; offer complementary services where they address a real need. Dorsey describes using register information for inventory and opening-hour decisions and describes priced advances repaid through card-transaction fees. - **inferred**: Shared API and ecosystem leverage — unnamed. Parts: expose capabilities through an external developer API; use that same API for the company's customer-facing product; let outside builders address specialized workflows; learn from their friction. - **inferred**: Open, secure, scalable value transfer — Dorsey's stated criteria. Parts: assess openness of development, operation and access; security, including individual key control and resistance to attack or corruption; and scalability without sacrificing the other properties or making the system unusable. Use the criteria to compare monetary foundations, not merely transaction throughput. Dorsey treats Bitcoin as his preferred candidate, but later says he would welcome a better answer meeting essential properties. No weights or numerical pass marks are supplied. Inferred guidance: reject an apparent scaling win that depends on removing the openness or key control that motivated the choice. - **inferred**: Shared protocol, competing services — unnamed. Parts: separate the common protocol from interfaces, discovery and business models; let multiple builders contribute and compete above the foundation; give people a practical path to move between services. Dorsey uses Bitcoin, social protocols and email as analogies. His 2025 retrospective calls Twitter's shutdown of third-party businesses a miss; his later model places keys, data and the social graph under user control. Apply when a company owns both infrastructure and the only route to customers. Discovery remains difficult, and open protocols do not eliminate concentrated hosting, naming or telecommunications dependencies. Parts: identify what interface elements encourage; trace the ranking signal; trace the revenue incentive; compare the resulting behavior with the intended customer or public outcome. - **documented**: Conversation-health indicators — provisional. Parts: measure shared attention, shared reality, receptivity and variety of perspectives together; establish a baseline; change the product; observe how the indicators move.
