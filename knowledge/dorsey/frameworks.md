# Jack Dorsey: frameworks

Built from 21 accepted distinct works and 192,388 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Open, secure and scalable value transfer. Parts: assess openness of development, operation and access; security, including control of keys and resistance to corruption or attack; and scaling, including the cost to usability and efficiency. Use when comparing payment foundations or deciding where to invest. Dorsey treats security as the first requirement for money, not a feature to trade away casually for speed. Decision guidance, inferred: reject a candidate that cannot explain who controls access or keys; then compare resilience, practical throughput and user experience. He considers Bitcoin his best current candidate, but says he would accept a better answer. These are qualitative gates, not numerical thresholds. - **inferred**: Unnamed: map the stack and its gatekeepers. Parts: trace the actual transaction or communication through policy, intermediaries, infrastructure and interfaces; identify who can change rules or deny access; compare an improved abstraction with replacing the underlying foundation. Use when a polished interface hides fees, permissions or operational dependencies. Favor a surface-layer fix when it removes the immediate friction without claiming to change the underlying institution. Consider a protocol-level alternative when the underlying gatekeeper is the problem and a credible transition exists. Dorsey sees Bitcoin as a possible replacement for conventional financial foundations, not evidence that a legal or operational transition is automatic. - **inferred**: Unnamed: verify resilience in public. Parts: inspect implementation and operating history; examine disagreements and who can contribute or run the system; identify remaining failure points; expect future attacks. Use for security-sensitive infrastructure whose claims depend on more than a vendor's assurance. Decision guidance, inferred: prefer systems where relevant parties can inspect both rules and outcomes, but do not treat source availability as proof that anyone has audited or secured them. Dorsey sees Bitcoin's public development and operating history as evidence of resilience while explicitly expecting further attacks. - **inferred**: Unnamed: deliberate base, experimental edge. Parts: distinguish foundational monetary rules from application experiments; weigh durability and predictability against speed; learn from faster-moving projects without assuming their changes belong in the base layer. Use when a proposed protocol change promises new functionality but could affect long-lived monetary guarantees. Dorsey's threshold is qualitative: move slowly where security and durability are essential, while recognizing the value of faster sandboxes. Inferred operational step: require a specific reason the change must live in the foundation rather than above it, and test the alternative before committing. - **inferred**: Unnamed: shared foundation, competing interfaces. Parts: establish a permissionless common protocol; let independent providers compete on interfaces, discovery, convenience and services; make departure possible with the user's keys, data or graph. Use when evaluating whether a platform must own its whole stack. Dorsey sees common infrastructure as a way for competitors' contributions to benefit one another, not a reason that businesses cannot earn revenue. Inferred decision test: identify which assets a user can actually carry away and whether discovery or infrastructure dependencies recreate a gatekeeper. Portability is an objective, not proof that every implementation delivers it. - **inferred**: Unnamed: decentralization and discovery test. Parts: separate transport from search, ranking and distribution; list dependencies such as domains, servers and telecom providers; test whether newcomers can find useful people or content. Use when an architecture is decentralized on paper but users depend on a small number of services to navigate it. Dorsey points to difficult discovery in earlier decentralized systems and acknowledges that Nostr discovery can be clumsy. Inferred guidance: do not declare the design successful until users can find what they need and can change providers; centralized discovery may be valuable, but should not be the only available route. - **inferred**: Unnamed: custody choice and responsibility. Parts: distinguish hosted custody, self-custody and assisted key management; explain who holds responsibility, what convenience costs and what happens when help is needed; observe which path people actually choose. Use for wallet onboarding and recovery decisions. Dorsey wants custodial businesses to offer non-custodial options, while acknowledging that users may value assistance or pay a company to secure assets. Inferred guidance: do not force maximum self-management on a user who cannot safely operate it; make the trade-off and available alternatives legible. - **inferred**: Unnamed: fund the commons without directing it. Parts: fund developers to choose important work; delegate grant decisions to knowledgeable independent organizations; publish usable infrastructure and allow others, including competitors, to benefit; build a separate business above it where appropriate. - **inferred**: Unnamed: observe, enable, repeat. Parts: watch what users actually do, especially behavior the original design did not anticipate; identify a recurring workaround; make it easier with a small change; observe the result. Use when a product's emerging purpose differs from its initial specification. Dorsey's Twitter example includes user-created replies, hashtags and manual retweets; he says he added support for replies. Decision guidance, inferred: prioritize repeated, consequential behavior over the founder's original story, but do not assume every workaround deserves a permanent feature. - **inferred**: Unnamed: define the user's job, then bound the product. Parts: name the outcome sought rather than the first device or implementation; choose clear inputs, outputs and exclusions; sequence adjacent capabilities only when they support that outcome. Use to avoid both a narrow hardware definition and an incoherent collection of features. Inferred decision guidance: add a capability when its contribution to the job is clear and its complexity can be managed without making the seller perform it. - **inferred**: Unnamed: prove trust in expanding cohorts. Parts: agree constraints with partners; deploy to a limited cohort; show results; increase exposure as evidence warrants. Dorsey describes Square moving through groups of roughly 500, 1,000, 10,000 and 50,000 users to earn bank and card-network trust. Use when introducing a payment system into partners' risk-sensitive infrastructure. Those figures describe his historical account, not universal rollout thresholds. Inferred guidance: define failure and escalation criteria with partners before each expansion, and pause if the evidence no longer justifies the next cohort. - **inferred**: Unnamed: time, money and network signals. Parts: observe whether people use a product without payment, whether they pay, and whether they invite others. - **inferred**: Unnamed: trace incentives to product behavior. Parts: identify who pays; trace the metrics and pressures that follow; inspect resulting product and policy choices; compare those incentives with user benefit. Dorsey contrasts Square's early growth from sellers making more sales with Twitter's advertising pressures, including competitive imitation and reactive moderation. Inferred guidance: prefer a model in which the business grows when users accomplish their goal; test countervailing pressures rather than assuming a payment model is inherently healthy. He exempts some advertising models from a blanket condemnation and treats Bitcoin-based alternatives as experiments or counterfactuals. - **inferred**: Trust and procedural fairness. Parts: provide meaningful transparency; make decisions procedurally fair and appealable; offer algorithmic choice; protect privacy. - **inferred**: Social protocol principles and algorithm choice. Parts: resist corporate and government control; reserve removal to the author; let individuals build, select or decline relevance algorithms.
