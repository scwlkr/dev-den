# Jack Dorsey: positions

Built from 21 accepted distinct works and 192,388 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Public conversation infrastructure: Dorsey helped build Twitter as a company-controlled service; he later judged ownership of the entire storage, discovery and distribution stack a mistake. His preferred architecture separates an open protocol from businesses that compete in interfaces and services. A protocol is not automatically immune to pressure: infrastructure and discovery can still become concentrated. This is a retrospective change in architectural preference, not evidence that Twitter originally operated as a fully open protocol. - **documented**: Developer APIs: Outside builders can discover uses and integrations the platform owner would not anticipate, including niche workflows and legacy-system connections. Dorsey also learned that an accessible API can overload a service through ordinary, nonmalicious use. His later Square approach was more deliberate because money movement raises the cost of surprises. Openness and capacity protection are both requirements, not mutually exclusive choices. - **documented**: First-party use of platform APIs: Dorsey credits Alyssa with insisting that Square customer-facing products use the same API offered to external developers. In his account this slowed initial development but simplified subsequent work and exposed internal teams to developer pain. The evidence supports a shared-interface discipline, not a claim that every internal service must expose every capability publicly. ([dorsey-9e760c7bcba694](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AydYygEeTJs&t=1194) / evidence dorsey-9e760c7bcba694-9de32d561846; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Discovery in decentralized systems: An open transport is not enough if people cannot find useful content or applications. Dorsey points to Usenet and IRC as examples where difficult discovery helped create demand for centralized alternatives. In 2023 he acknowledged that Nostr discovery remained clumsy and discouraged users. His response is to build usable discovery while preserving alternatives, rather than treating decentralization alone as a complete product. - **documented**: Algorithmic choice: In 2021 testimony Dorsey advocated transparency or meaningful control over consequential algorithms while acknowledging Twitter needed improvement. Later he argued for an algorithm marketplace in which people can select, build or decline ranking, rather than accept one company’s default. He credits Stephen Wolfram for the store idea. Choice is a proposal for distributing control, not proof that every user can assess an algorithm’s effects. - **documented**: Moderation and safety: In 2018 Dorsey said expression must be balanced with privacy and physical safety, and that Twitter was responsible for enforcing rules against harassment and silencing. These are meaningfully different governance models; the supplied evidence does not resolve their legal or safety tensions. - **documented**: Enforcement errors and appeals: Dorsey’s 2021 testimony treats mistakes by both automated and human enforcement as inevitable and calls for straightforward appeals. His earlier account distinguishes removal decisions from automated down-ranking or interstitials and prioritizes severe reports. He also admitted that explanations for ranking and filtering were weaker than removal notices. A reviewer should not equate having a policy with having adequate notice or correction. - **documented**: Portability and identity: In 2018 Dorsey supported user control, withdrawal and portability of data but admitted Twitter allowed export, not import. He later favored moving between social clients or wallets while retaining control of keys, data and graph. He values Nostr signatures as evidence that a message came from the holder of a key. That identification is conditional on key control and does not by itself establish a real-world identity. - **documented**: Bluesky governance: Dorsey’s 2021 testimony presented Bluesky as an early, complex attempt to develop decentralized standards and recommendation choice. In a 2025 interview he qualified the organizational history: independence after Twitter’s sale was probably appropriate, but outside funding introduced incentives he opposed at the protocol layer. - **documented**: Bitcoin as internet money: In 2018 Dorsey was uncertain whether the internet would have one native currency or several. By 2022 he described Bitcoin as the only current candidate meeting his monetary criteria; in a later interview he said he would welcome a better answer if one met the necessary properties. His preference is strong but conditional on security, censorship resistance, privacy and practical usability, not a demonstrated guarantee of adoption. - **documented**: Bitcoin utility versus speculation: Dorsey sees store-of-value adoption as potentially useful on a path toward remittances and currency, while acknowledging that an asset expected to appreciate can encourage saving instead of spending.
