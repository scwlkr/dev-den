# Casey Muratori: positions

Built from 36 accepted distinct works and 425,131 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Performance budgets. In 2023, Muratori defined performance as the resources consumed while a program does its job, including time, energy, network traffic and storage. Choose the resources that matter to users and measure them alongside correctness. A faster result that exhausts battery or consumes excessive bandwidth is not necessarily a better result. ([casey-0610f7f8e7370b](https://www.computerenhance.com/p/performance-excuses-debunked) / evidence casey-0610f7f8e7370b-f5010a3daf09; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Performance-aware architecture. Think about performance before the architecture makes improvement expensive, but defer detailed tuning when there is a credible local path to the required budget. A replaceable hash table can wait; an API that hides a synchronous request in every object operation may require a wide rewrite to batch later. ([casey-a182d2c7851838](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8xBJPa_480Q&t=1965) / evidence casey-a182d2c7851838-04f5d823c85f; [casey-ec87fbe5d368bb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ffDXc6oup3Q&t=3523) / evidence casey-ec87fbe5d368bb-89c345d5ab23; [casey-ee28d9ae950727](https://www.computerenhance.com/p/me-and-prime-talk-game-engine-basics) / evidence casey-ee28d9ae950727-c16f110a571b; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Premature optimization. Muratori distinguishes expensive, brittle hand-tuning without evidence of importance from early awareness of architectural cost. He also criticizes his own earlier tendency to project his experience onto the historical saying. His later investigation treats its origin and quantitative rationale as questions to research, not as settled slogans. ([casey-ec87fbe5d368bb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ffDXc6oup3Q&t=3291) / evidence casey-ec87fbe5d368bb-b839cda5dfe2; [casey-afb4baca4de395](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hpj6r6CjJf8&t=309) / evidence casey-afb4baca4de395-0363019a87ed; [casey-f6d937f72cbadc](https://www.computerenhance.com/p/theroot) / evidence casey-f6d937f72cbadc-9b31d8a90daa; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Hotspots and rewrites. Do not assume either that performance is always concentrated in a few functions or that every slow system needs a rewrite. His 2023 discussion uses reported company rewrites as counterexamples to blanket claims. A quick, slow first version can be a valid strategy if the team has the skill and plan to replace it. ([casey-0610f7f8e7370b](https://www.computerenhance.com/p/performance-excuses-debunked) / evidence casey-0610f7f8e7370b-2a2eda75d276; [casey-0610f7f8e7370b](https://www.computerenhance.com/p/performance-excuses-debunked) / evidence casey-0610f7f8e7370b-082b823bc2b0; [casey-0610f7f8e7370b](https://www.computerenhance.com/p/performance-excuses-debunked) / evidence casey-0610f7f8e7370b-f5261d8a0d46; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Hardware ceilings. Estimate the required work and machine capacity before accepting an implementation as fast. Compare the measured result with a plausible range, including data movement and dependencies, not just peak arithmetic. A ceiling is a diagnostic reference rather than a promise; his suggested roughly 2× tolerance in one discussion was a ballpark judgment, not a universal target. ([casey-4231bba6844632](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ge3aKEmZcqY&t=4950) / evidence casey-4231bba6844632-1b9a430135a2; [casey-4231bba6844632](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ge3aKEmZcqY&t=4992) / evidence casey-4231bba6844632-55ea8d0d01dd; [casey-6b19e33ca50ad1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=apREl0KmTdQ&t=927) / evidence casey-6b19e33ca50ad1-7c9db8460d93; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Stopping optimization. Stop when the remaining cost is acceptable for the job and further effort has poor value. In The Witness planting work, he left available threading and gather improvements undone after responsiveness was sufficient. A real external limit can also justify stopping, but an unexplained slow service should not automatically be treated as a physical limit. ([casey-4231bba6844632](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ge3aKEmZcqY&t=7070) / evidence casey-4231bba6844632-48a54f656767; [casey-6b19e33ca50ad1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=apREl0KmTdQ&t=3623) / evidence casey-6b19e33ca50ad1-6a4d01a6d113; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Assembly literacy. Teach programmers to read generated assembly and understand basic execution and memory costs; do not require most of them to write assembly by hand. One instruction set can establish the basic literacy, while architecture-specific semantics still need investigation. Compiler Explorer provides a short feedback loop, not a substitute for measuring the complete workload. - **documented**: Language choice. A predictable source-to-machine mapping is valuable for performance work, but language labels do not determine speed by themselves. Muratori points to both fast and slow Java, and recommends bulk compiled operations for suitable Python workloads. For his own established C workflow, a small prospective language improvement may not repay migration and tooling costs. - **documented**: Virtual calls and small functions. His objection is not that short functions or function pointers are inherently bad. When implementations are unknown, pervasive virtual dispatch can also prevent inlining, fusion or vectorization; compiler visibility and link-time optimization change the outcome. Inspect generated behavior before attributing all cost to the indirect call itself. - **documented**: Domain hierarchies. His 2025 historical critique targets compile-time inheritance hierarchies that mirror the domain and make cross-cutting work awkward, not every definition of object-oriented programming. He values type checking, accepts virtual functions in suitable places and allows that an object-oriented design can solve the same problems with different boundaries. - **inferred**: Data representation. For mutually exclusive alternatives, he favors discriminated unions and type-directed operations; for entities combining independent capabilities, a structure with feature flags can fit better. Consider whether new actions or new types are more frequent. The choice must also account for memory, safety and which combinations the representation permits. - **documented**: Entity-component organization. System-owned property collections can make a cross-entity operation direct: the physics system owns physics data and an entity ID associates it with other data. Muratori presents this as a useful alternative to object-owned properties, not a universal architecture; he explicitly says he does not personally use ECSs. - **documented**: Reusable API boundaries. Expose representations that can responsibly be promised stable, provide useful operations, and accept client representations where one fixed form would obstruct integration. Source access alone is insufficient if customers lack the time or expertise to alter internals. Escape hatches can help later users, but they carry upgrade and maintenance risk. - **documented**: Bottom-up design. For difficult or unfamiliar systems, implement the necessary work and try several real uses before fixing a reusable abstraction. A design document can precede production if it records experiments rather than an imagined solution. Prior experience can reduce the need for fresh prototypes, but it does not guarantee that every integration detail has been remembered. - **documented**: Immediate-mode GUI. In the 2005 presentation, application code drives the update path, issues controls and handles their results in ordinary control flow. Application data stays with the application; the UI retains interaction state such as hot and active items. This is an API organization, not a claim that no state exists or that every GUI should use his implementation. - **documented**: GUI trade-offs. Immediate-mode control flow makes it straightforward to omit a control for a frame and to attach interaction to changing game data. Muratori also identifies harder selective redraw and possible one-frame layout lag. Multiple passes can avoid the latter at additional complexity; his preference for accepting lag is conditional on the application. - **inferred**: Reuse and specialization. Reuse can be sensible, especially for a well-optimized library, but inspect the extra work it brings into the actual task. A general raycast for arbitrary directions can be a poor fit for repeated downward-only queries. Specialization pays when its setup and maintenance costs are justified by repeated use and a meaningful delay. - **documented**: SIMD and threading. First understand and simplify the core work, then consider processing independent items in SIMD and distributing work across cores. Muratori usually works out SIMD throughput before thread distribution because workers execute the same kernel. This is a preference, not a universal ordering: available core count, bandwidth and workload structure change the payoff. - **documented**: Benchmark interpretation. A tiny benchmark can isolate a hardware mechanism but cannot by itself rank languages generally. For comparisons, use multiple relevant workloads, examine source and implementations, and account for startup, semantics, shared native libraries and uneven programmer skill. Avoid preventing compiler transformations if ordinary compiler optimization is what the test is intended to evaluate. - **inferred**: Debugging surprising timings. Start with an explicit dependency and throughput model; then challenge the measurement as well as the model. Timestamp-counter ticks are not necessarily boosted core cycles, so check frequency and corroborate with performance counters. An observed capability does not establish the undocumented hardware mechanism proposed to explain it. - **documented**: Testing. Select tests by expected total engineering benefit: bugs and downstream work avoided versus test creation, maintenance and friction. He used regression tests for core RAD routines. He does not make test-driven development his general default, while allowing it can be an intelligent choice for a particular project. - **documented**: Organizational boundaries. Conway-style boundaries make coordination and joint iteration more expensive, narrowing the designs a team can practically produce. Place team divisions where that cost does the least harm. His temporal refinement adds that old organizational boundaries can remain in a long-lived product; he treats these as useful hypotheses, not laws of physics. - **inferred**: Microservices and distributed calls. Service boundaries may support organizational coordination, but they are not automatically performance improvements. Network communication and failure handling impose costs, and individually synchronous calls can create preventable serial latency. Combining services or batching can help when coordination, ownership and actual dependencies permit it. - **documented**: Version control. His small-team system preserves conflicting versions and lets people reconcile when necessary, rather than blocking backup or sharing immediately. - **documented**: Safety-critical recursion. A recursion restriction can be reasonable where stack exhaustion has severe consequences and bounds must be clear.
