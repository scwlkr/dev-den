# Casey Muratori: principles

Built from 87 accepted distinct works and 1,166,408 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Do not dismiss performance before investigating the product. Define the relevant user-visible resource costs, establish a budget, and test whether a claimed bottleneck or business constraint actually applies. A concentrated hotspot or an unprofitable optimization can be real in a particular codebase; neither is a universal excuse.  Independent support includes the performance-excuses essay, the Witness workload discussion, and the performance-aware course announcement. - **inferred**: Separate early performance awareness from early hand optimization. Keep batching, data access, and a plausible repair path available while writing simple code; spend effort on brittle low-level changes only when their benefit matters. His own historical research also records a correction: he had initially imposed his preferred meaning on the premature-optimization phrase instead of establishing its context. - **inferred**: Learn to read generated assembly before assuming source-level simplicity explains CPU work. Compare compiler output and measurements for the target configuration; most developers need not routinely write assembly. This is useful even in a managed language, where the compiler or VM mediates the source-to-machine mapping. Compiler-specific behavior and hardware still need checking. - **inferred**: Optimize toward a justified target, not indefinitely toward peak. Compare achieved time with a plausible hardware model and the actual product requirement; stop when the remaining benefit does not repay complexity or time. Muratori left the improved Witness routine single-threaded because it was already fast enough, though further work was possible. A theoretical ceiling remains a diagnostic, not a delivery promise. - **inferred**: Organize data around operations that actually consume it, not solely around names in the user’s domain model. Examine cross-type actions, access patterns, and expected change before choosing boundaries. A system-owned property collection, tagged union, or ordinary structure may fit better than a hierarchy; a hierarchy can still work when it fits the task. - **inferred**: Evaluate dispatch by the information it hides, not just the indirect-call instruction. Small functions are compatible with optimization when the compiler can see and combine them; unknown runtime types may block inlining, loop transformations, or SIMD. Whole-program visibility and compiler capabilities can change the outcome, so inspect the actual build rather than declaring every virtual call slow. - **inferred**: Do not introduce accessors merely to protect against hypothetical future behavior. Use direct access when it is the clearest contract; add an accessor when the operation actually needs behavior or that need is highly likely. This is not an argument against stable public contracts or validation where they serve clients. His approximately 90% likelihood comment is an informal judgment, not a calibrated threshold. - **inferred**: Use type information and assertions to catch concrete mistakes, while handling expected failures as normal program states. Derive allocation size and pointer type together; check narrowing and capacity assumptions; report or recover from plausible file and device failures. Do not leave an assertion on an ordinary missed frame deadline. Assertions document invariants, but do not replace production error handling. - **inferred**: Match debug storage and identity to lifetime. Historical values should usually be copied rather than represented by pointers to transient objects; data needed across DLL reload must survive unloading. A short-lived pending edit can use a cheaper identity strategy only if its lifetime and concurrency assumptions hold. Clear the edit after applying it so it does not repeat. - **inferred**: Evaluate reuse and custom work against the actual project, including long-term ownership. Existing libraries and engines can save substantial work and may already be optimized; they can also carry unnecessary work or resist required changes. Building or forking a component is justified only if the team can maintain it and the alternatives do not fit. Handmade Hero’s from-scratch scope is educational, not a universal production mandate. - **inferred**: Treat architectural boundaries as choices that restrict joint work and optimization. Place team and software boundaries where the loss of coordination hurts least, and avoid adding tiny boundaries solely because encapsulation is fashionable. Human limits and organizational needs can make boundaries worthwhile; Conway’s law is his useful hypothesis, not a physical law or a guarantee of clean software.
