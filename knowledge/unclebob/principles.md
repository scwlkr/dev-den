# Robert C. Martin (Uncle Bob): principles

Built from 61 accepted distinct works and 268,975 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Organize around reasons for change.  Group work that changes together and separate responsibilities responding to different business functions. In Martin’s Employee example, pay, hours reporting, and persistence answer to different organizational needs; changing one must not casually damage another. Bug fixing and refactoring are activities of programmers, not the business responsibilities that define the module. A service can still violate this rule if it mixes unrelated concerns. - **inferred**: Protect policy by directing source dependencies inward.  A UI, database adapter, or framework may call business logic at runtime without the business source naming that mechanism. An application-owned interface lets an outer implementation depend on the core. Inspect imports and boundary data, not merely runtime call arrows. The resulting insulation is asymmetric: a core change may still break a plugin, and introducing boundaries requires translation work. - **documented**: Make the application’s purpose visible before its tools.  A reader should find use cases and domain concepts rather than only framework categories. Treat web delivery as I/O and keep business rules callable without a server where practical. This is not a ban on Rails or web applications: it is a request to stop the delivery mechanism from defining every business object. - **documented**: Defer consequential commitments, not necessary work.  Keep database, framework, and delivery decisions replaceable while information is scarce, and use provisional mechanisms to demonstrate working slices. Some decisions, including a programming language, may be needed early. Deferring a final GUI choice does not mean delaying every GUI experiment, and replacement remains potentially expensive. - **inferred**: Seek strategic closure, not a ban on modification.  Identify likely variations and arrange for some extensions to require little change to already-correct code. Martin repudiated the absolute wording of his 1996 definition in 2013: significant systems cannot be closed against every change. Polymorphic interfaces or templates can provide a variation point, but their cost must be justified by the change being protected against. - **documented**: Judge substitutes by client-visible behavior.  A replacement must respect the expectations of its clients: it should not demand stronger preconditions or offer weaker postconditions. Martin’s ellipse and circle example illustrates a misleading substitution where a client’s expectations are ignored. In 2020 he corrected his own earlier tendency to frame the principle as inheritance-specific; interfaces and duck types are also relevant. He also permits economic trade-offs rather than treating compliance as costless. Corroboration: medium (2 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Create interfaces for real consumers or boundaries.  Small client-oriented interfaces limit irrelevant dependencies, but a separate interface for every client or hypothetical variation can become unmanageable. Martin commonly introduces one when a test needs a substitute or a dependency must be inverted; direct use can be sufficient otherwise. A dynamic-language boundary may be a conceptual protocol rather than a declared interface. Corroboration: medium (2 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Use tests to preserve the ability to change code.  A fast, trusted suite reduces the fear of renaming, extracting, and moving behavior; run it after small changes and investigate or revert failures. Tests are maintained code, so avoid mirroring production internals and making harmless refactorings break many tests. A passing suite supplies evidence, not proof of all requirements or behaviors. - **inferred**: Keep development feedback fast.  Slow tests are run less frequently, so more unverified changes accumulate and refactoring becomes riskier. Isolate slow disks, servers, and external services for the frequent suite, while preserving targeted checks of real connections. Martin sees slowness as a possible design signal, not conclusive proof of bad architecture. Measure the actual bottleneck before blaming a particular testing tool. - **documented**: Prefer test-first, while evaluating the actual cycle.  Martin defines TDD as test-first and recommends very small cycles because they localize errors and encourage coverage. In 2016 he argued that short cycle size may matter more than ordering alone, and that equally disciplined test-last work could behave similarly. He later accepted a mutation-verified test-after suite as potentially professional while retaining his preference for test-first. These are his interpretations and judgments, not proof that practices are equivalent. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Do not confuse coverage with correctness.  Martin aims asymptotically at 100% line and branch coverage but acknowledges the goal may not be reached. Executing a line does not show that an assertion checks it, and exhaustive paths become combinatorial. Distinguish uncovered implementation from requirements nobody specified; review and user feedback address the latter. Trivial code may be covered indirectly, but a short line can still have substantial semantics. Corroboration: medium (2 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Keep hard-to-test edges humble.  Separate significant computation and presentation decisions from physical devices and visual fiddling so they can be tested independently. A driver mock can show that software sent a command, not that a physical bell rang; observation or sensing addresses a different question. Martin sometimes tests exploratory code afterward or rewrites it test-first. His exception for thin views is not an excuse to leave substantial logic untested. - **inferred**: Use patterns to solve an observed design problem, not to decorate code.  Shared names and recognizable forms can communicate a solution, but searching for places to force a pattern introduces unnecessary structure. An adapter can isolate an existing server that cannot readily change; a factory can localize concrete construction. Choose the mechanism for the dependency or variation at hand, and consider whether a simpler direct call suffices. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **documented**: Treat frameworks as useful but asymmetric commitments.  A framework can supply substantial capability quickly, yet its conventions and evolution may not match the application’s future needs. Keep high-level policy apart from framework inheritance and annotations where that protection pays for itself. Martin acknowledges translation overhead and says it may not matter for a disposable month-long application; isolation makes replacement possible, not necessarily easy. - **documented**: Keep improvement continuous, but allow controlled disorder.  Make small cleanups during ordinary work rather than assuming a large separate rewrite will fix everything. - **documented**: Separate honest forecasts from commitments.  Communicate uncertainty and say when a requested deadline cannot support the requested scope; offer a smaller or different outcome. Martin warns that an unqualified promise to try may be heard as a commitment even when behavior will not change. He recognizes management and sales constraints and expects experienced developers to explain alternatives rather than merely confront people. - **inferred**: Make release evidence repeatable and responsibilities explicit.  Martin proposes quick, repeatable demonstrations of expected behavior and favors unit and acceptance tests, while allowing other methods in principle. Completed sprint features should be technically deployable; the business decides whether to release. His proposed professional oath is revisable, and even a strong test suite does not establish complete correctness or remove the need for safety-specific assurance. - **inferred**: Share ownership and knowledge, not just tasks.  Code belongs to the team and business rather than to a guarded individual territory. Pairing is one way to distribute understanding so teammates can cover for one another, but Martin does not demand pairing continuously and acknowledges that some tasks or pairs work better separately. - **inferred**: Treat rules and metrics as aids to judgment.  Martin describes his clean-code rules as accumulated personal guidance open to improvement, not universal law. He recommends using complexity, method length, and coverage to inspect work rather than turning them into management targets. He allows rare emergency fixes that temporarily violate normal practice, with cleanup negotiated afterward; an emergency exception should not become the routine operating model.
