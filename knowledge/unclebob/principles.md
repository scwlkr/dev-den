# Robert C. Martin (Uncle Bob): principles

Built from 129 accepted distinct works and 527,818 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Write for the next reader as well as the machine. Choose names and structure that reveal intent, and vary assumed background according to whether the audience is a stable team or a broad public. Reduce accidental complexity without pretending that essential complexity disappears. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Treat small functions as a readability technique, not a line-count law. Extract a meaningful operation when its name clarifies the caller; reverse and reorganize the extraction if the reader must navigate excessively. Runtime and stack limits can justify measured exceptions. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **documented**: Group code by the people and responsibilities that drive change. Payroll calculation, operational reporting and persistence can answer to different interests even when they concern the same employee. - **documented**: Protect working code against selected changes, not all possible changes. Open-closed design aims to add an expected variation with little modification to existing modules, but every significant system must change somewhere. Martin repudiated his own earlier absolute wording and recommends learning which variation actually matters. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **documented**: Judge substitution by the client-visible contract, not a real-world family resemblance. A replacement should not demand stronger preconditions or deliver weaker postconditions. Martin later corrected his earlier tendency to frame Liskov substitution as inheritance alone; the concern also covers interface implementations and duck types. - **documented**: Keep interfaces focused on what their clients need. This reduces unrelated dependencies and, in some environments, unnecessary recompilation or redeployment. Do not create one interface per individual client automatically: excessive fragmentation creates its own maintenance cost. - **documented**: Match organization to scale and risk. Martin reports not diligently applying Clean Architecture to applications below roughly 5,000 lines, while treating boundaries as critical in larger systems. A small component inside a large system must still respect that system’s boundaries. The line count is his heuristic, not a universal cutoff. - **documented**: A fast, trusted regression suite is a means of maintaining design, not merely a release ritual. Make a small structural change, run the relevant tests and correct or revert on failure. The benefit depends on both speed and confidence; Martin would accept a different testing approach that met those conditions. - **inferred**: Keep professional commitments honest. Give uncertainty rather than a single unjustified promise; when a requested date is infeasible, offer a smaller scope or another practical option. Managers retain legitimate business constraints, but developers should not hide known defects or convert pressure into a false assurance.
