# Robert C. Martin (Uncle Bob): positions

Built from 61 accepted distinct works and 268,975 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: TDD order and cycle size. Martin defines TDD as writing a failing test before the production code needed to pass it. His 2014 nano-cycle also limits each side to the smallest step that fails or passes. In 2016, interpreting a reported study, he stressed that short cycles may be more important than ordering and allowed that equally disciplined test-last work could behave similarly. This is his interpretation, not an independently verified finding. Use test-first to enforce small feedback loops, not as evidence that ordering alone guarantees superior results. ([unclebob-9d158b477bef4e](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/12/17/TheCyclesOfTDD.html) / evidence unclebob-9d158b477bef4e-b4df391d77ea; [unclebob-7161c75e7fa66a](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WpkDN78P884&t=3496) / evidence unclebob-7161c75e7fa66a-bbe5d5dcc23c; [unclebob-03d3d19f32dae3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/11/10/TDD-Doesnt-work.html) / evidence unclebob-03d3d19f32dae3-69426b09403d; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Test-first is preferred, not the only professional route. In 2016 Martin reported strengthening a laxly developed suite with mutation testing and explicitly accepted a semantically stable test-after suite checked that way as professional. He still preferred test-first for short cycles and design feedback. Mutation testing checks whether selected changes are detected; neither surviving-mutant analysis nor a passing suite establishes that every requirement has been discovered. ([unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/06/10/MutationTesting.html) / evidence unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41-a7d3b371ea19; [unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/06/10/MutationTesting.html) / evidence unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41-1ac2b5753885; [unclebob-03d3d19f32dae3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/11/10/TDD-Doesnt-work.html) / evidence unclebob-03d3d19f32dae3-69426b09403d; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: TDD and architectural vision. TDD can drive local design, but Martin says it does not supply the high-level architectural vision. His practical alternative is to sketch important boundaries, implement and test small slices, then revise the sketch when code contradicts it. For a large system he accepts some initial design while rejecting a long, isolated design phase. A team absorbed in the mechanics of tests can still lose sight of the system. ([unclebob-4f76a5b16635e3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2017/03/03/TDD-Harms-Architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-4f76a5b16635e3-8528582924bb; [unclebob-2a851e848e2ce6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VY4LYd2YfBk&t=2349) / evidence unclebob-2a851e848e2ce6-eb98a383fca9; [unclebob-c1f727c372c0d3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/03/19/GivingUpOnTDD.html) / evidence unclebob-c1f727c372c0d3-065943c48251; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Coverage and confidence. Martin advocates approaching 100% line and branch coverage asymptotically rather than declaring a lower percentage sufficient. He also observes that coverage remains unchanged if assertions are removed and that exhaustive path coverage becomes impractical. Inspect uncovered logic, but also inspect the quality of assertions and seek missed requirements through review and user feedback. Coverage is a diagnostic, not proof of correctness. ([unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/06/10/MutationTesting.html) / evidence unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41-e299bfbce3a6; [unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2016/06/10/MutationTesting.html) / evidence unclebob-d8d717bc7e5d41-1ac2b5753885; [unclebob-09830b9aa30a68](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2dKZ-dWaCiU&t=3757) / evidence unclebob-09830b9aa30a68-a31dc9b861b5; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Feedback speed. Slow tests make developers run them less often, allowing more unverified changes to accumulate before a failure. Martin regards 15 minutes as too slow for the rapid unit-test loop and warns that suites taking more than a couple of minutes tend to be deferred. These are practical heuristics, not universal thresholds. His remedy is to isolate slow infrastructure and improve builds, while retaining tests that actually exercise integration. ([unclebob-0b312044bb5033](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2017/05/05/TestDefinitions.html) / evidence unclebob-0b312044bb5033-376bc5ffad39; [unclebob-46e632eed0d049](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_TH-Y78tt4&t=3454) / evidence unclebob-46e632eed0d049-ee0d49655f63; [unclebob-b93b1f3a344b28](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/09/03/TestTime.html) / evidence unclebob-b93b1f3a344b28-95937abb5c07; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Test doubles. Martin rejects both running every test through all infrastructure and mocking every class interaction. His 2014 heuristic is to substitute slow or dangerous collaborators across architecturally significant boundaries, while testing behavior within a boundary with fewer doubles. Spies and formal mocks can couple tests to call sequences; fakes can acquire enough behavior to need tests themselves. He often writes doubles by hand, but accepts tools when they solve a real constraint. ([unclebob-e89cc75a9cec9e](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/10/WhenToMock.html) / evidence unclebob-e89cc75a9cec9e-25756cb0546a; [unclebob-860320a823aea1](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/14/TheLittleMocker.html) / evidence unclebob-860320a823aea1-3c3f78076bc9; [unclebob-860320a823aea1](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/14/TheLittleMocker.html) / evidence unclebob-860320a823aea1-eac253a02194; [unclebob-e89cc75a9cec9e](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/10/WhenToMock.html) / evidence unclebob-e89cc75a9cec9e-85b3d035c390; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Physical and visual boundaries. In 2013–2014 Martin excluded some visual fiddling and disposable GUI work from test-first practice, and distinguished a mocked driver signal from proof that a physical bell actually rings. His response is to move significant decisions into independently testable code, then check the thin physical or visual edge by observation or appropriate sensing. This is a judgment about the boundary, not permission to leave its substantive logic untested. ([unclebob-a4089de04a8bac](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2013/03/06/ThePragmaticsOfTDD.html) / evidence unclebob-a4089de04a8bac-6d186ca334ef; [unclebob-6f6f18be59b537](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/04/30/When-tdd-does-not-work.html) / evidence unclebob-6f6f18be59b537-06335e563903; [unclebob-6f6f18be59b537](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/04/30/When-tdd-does-not-work.html) / evidence unclebob-6f6f18be59b537-fb1fb2f229da; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Test structure and refactoring. Tests are maintained code, not disposable mirrors of implementation. Martin argues that a test class for every production class makes behavior-preserving extraction likely to break tests. Prefer a stable behavioral API and refactor production classes behind it; refactor tests too, but do not require their module structure to match production. This does not eliminate behavioral coupling or guarantee that every internal defect is visible. ([unclebob-110b06aa627af3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2017/10/03/TestContravariance.html) / evidence unclebob-110b06aa627af3-9a4fd071df41; [unclebob-110b06aa627af3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2017/10/03/TestContravariance.html) / evidence unclebob-110b06aa627af3-da5cbcb8d2fc; [unclebob-4f76a5b16635e3](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2017/03/03/TDD-Harms-Architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-4f76a5b16635e3-11e1d5f9a5c1; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Small functions and measured exceptions. Martin argues that named small functions reveal interfaces otherwise hidden inside a large function and let readers move between levels of detail. Related functions can remain together in an ordered module. He also describes real-time systems where extra calls or stack usage can violate constraints: extract, measure, and undo selected extractions where necessary. Aesthetic preference does not override a demonstrated deadline or resource limit. ([unclebob-335b3d6762d229](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SNdzafT0wbs&t=4674) / evidence unclebob-335b3d6762d229-b788d4c842b0; [unclebob-bd9a500f3fab2c](https://www.infoq.com/podcasts/uncle-bob-solid-ddd/) / evidence unclebob-bd9a500f3fab2c-592e1901fa91; [unclebob-67bc18b603df22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G08FxxwPjXE&t=3595) / evidence unclebob-67bc18b603df22-c17ff54d622e; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Cleanliness and livability. In 2018 Martin credited Sarah Mei with a livability insight he had not adequately expressed, while recognizing that he had practiced some controlled mess. Temporary disorder can make a change easier, provided it is kept in check and cleaned up. This qualifies a sterile interpretation of clean code; it does not convert permanent, accumulating structural damage into an ideal. ([unclebob-3cb44af8551198](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2018/08/13/TooClean.html) / evidence unclebob-3cb44af8551198-d4c2ef23ebf3; [unclebob-3cb44af8551198](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2018/08/13/TooClean.html) / evidence unclebob-3cb44af8551198-0b8136f271bd; [unclebob-3cb44af8551198](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2018/08/13/TooClean.html) / evidence unclebob-3cb44af8551198-a9c490e36d42; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Incremental legacy improvement. For fragile, untested code, Martin recommends making a small improvement in an area already being changed and gradually adding tests. He discourages a separate, large cleanup project because it may overrun and damage the system; larger corrections should be integrated with features and fixes. Where tests are absent, the immediate change must be especially gentle, and useful improvement may take months or years. ([unclebob-335b3d6762d229](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SNdzafT0wbs&t=1906) / evidence unclebob-335b3d6762d229-41254bdc873f; [unclebob-335b3d6762d229](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SNdzafT0wbs&t=2184) / evidence unclebob-335b3d6762d229-91e76211605e; [unclebob-989669d8f0f513](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x90VHbG8FzY&t=1519) / evidence unclebob-989669d8f0f513-946acdf85f72; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Architecture expresses purpose. Martin argues that the top level of an application should reveal its use cases and domain, not primarily announce Rails, a database or a delivery technology. Describe inputs, processing and outcomes without prematurely choosing web forms or storage. This is an architectural reading aid and a way to protect policy, not a claim that a directory tree alone proves the quality of a system. ([unclebob-6887cdc81accdc](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2011/09/30/Screaming-Architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-6887cdc81accdc-f2d8c33187ba; [unclebob-46e632eed0d049](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_TH-Y78tt4&t=1106) / evidence unclebob-46e632eed0d049-1a00bc5cb396; [unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01-5206fc74fe0f; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Dependency direction. Across a meaningful architectural boundary, Martin directs source dependencies inward toward policy, even when runtime control or data travels outward. An inner output interface implemented by an outer presenter is one mechanism. Boundary data should be shaped for the inner code, not carry framework-owned row formats inward. The number of layers is not fixed; the direction of dependencies is the governing constraint. ([unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01-4eb537ec77e1; [unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01-43ce669b2bf6; [unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01-8911210548b7; [unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html) / evidence unclebob-bc90bbf1effb01-bc99dc1e1eea; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Frameworks and their cost. Martin values frameworks as tools but warns that inheritance, annotations and conventions can bind business rules to an independently evolving vendor. Put framework-specific work behind a boundary when protecting long-lived policy matters. He explicitly acknowledges translation overhead and notes that a disposable month-long application may not benefit enough to justify it. Replacement remains possible, not necessarily easy. ([unclebob-b3d63da21d8aac](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/11/FrameworkBound.html) / evidence unclebob-b3d63da21d8aac-92205ee98eff; [unclebob-8400f4cb01599b](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3IA9WKVN53Q&t=4207) / evidence unclebob-8400f4cb01599b-8254f388e4dc; [unclebob-3cab7d307d0128](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tp-Pku4Ikkc&t=2654) / evidence unclebob-3cab7d307d0128-aeebeb34f358; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Persistence is a tool, not the center. Martin prefers application-owned gateways that express needed queries and updates while SQL, schema and ORM details remain outside the business-rule boundary. He allows stored procedures for integrity or query optimization but opposes placing application business processing there. Switching from SQL to NoSQL does not itself fix database-centered coupling. Test actual queries separately even if most business tests use a substitute. ([unclebob-09830b9aa30a68](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2dKZ-dWaCiU&t=3259) / evidence unclebob-09830b9aa30a68-605e8cda74c6; [unclebob-81c2cbf3ed75c1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTk0DBpQveM&t=2501) / evidence unclebob-81c2cbf3ed75c1-0033c5ab55cf; [unclebob-c07e65f55865a8](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/05/15/NODB.html) / evidence unclebob-c07e65f55865a8-9c5dc391fbdc; [unclebob-09830b9aa30a68](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2dKZ-dWaCiU&t=4293) / evidence unclebob-09830b9aa30a68-58deaff61639; 3 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Deferred decisions. Martin wants consequential infrastructure choices delayed until more information is available, while acknowledging that some choices, such as the implementation language, must be made early. Deferring a GUI commitment does not mean forbidding GUI work: build provisional delivery and persistence slices behind boundaries. The decision point is the last responsible moment, not an indefinite refusal to decide. ([unclebob-09830b9aa30a68](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2dKZ-dWaCiU&t=3639) / evidence unclebob-09830b9aa30a68-efd3ef6c1199; [unclebob-46e632eed0d049](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_TH-Y78tt4&t=4022) / evidence unclebob-46e632eed0d049-6bedf3133a08; [unclebob-81c2cbf3ed75c1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTk0DBpQveM&t=3299) / evidence unclebob-81c2cbf3ed75c1-bf9ec5d59bfd; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Microservices. In 2014 Martin described microservices as a deployment option, not architecture by themselves. Services can provide independent restart, deployment and technology choices, but introduce message-versioning, coordination and operational costs. He recommends starting with well-separated in-process components and distributing strategically when needed. Dynamic packages can also be independently deployed; network boundaries are not the only way to obtain modularity. ([unclebob-0b950dce03a78b](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/10/01/CleanMicroserviceArchitecture.html) / evidence unclebob-0b950dce03a78b-ad361018c7b5; [unclebob-0b950dce03a78b](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/10/01/CleanMicroserviceArchitecture.html) / evidence unclebob-0b950dce03a78b-ea031478c078; [unclebob-de21b72cf23390](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/09/19/MicroServicesAndJars.html) / evidence unclebob-de21b72cf23390-cee469aeedc1; [unclebob-de21b72cf23390](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/09/19/MicroServicesAndJars.html) / evidence unclebob-de21b72cf23390-236e2b22d7af; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Single responsibility and change. Martin groups code by the business functions and people whose needs cause it to change, not by the fact that a programmer might refactor or fix it. His Employee example separates pay, hours reporting and persistence because finance, operations and technology can demand different changes. Service boundaries do not automatically achieve this: a microservice can still mix unrelated reasons for change. ([unclebob-9155454a988921](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/08/SingleReponsibilityPrinciple.html) / evidence unclebob-9155454a988921-0708f577b098; [unclebob-9155454a988921](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2014/05/08/SingleReponsibilityPrinciple.html) / evidence unclebob-9155454a988921-423c4b36d54f; [unclebob-65ca06070f1683](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2020/10/18/Solid-Relevance.html) / evidence unclebob-65ca06070f1683-50c18d626f06; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Open-closed principle, revised. Martin later repudiated the absolute wording of his 1996 formulation: significant programs cannot be closed against every change. In 2013 he recast the goal as strategic protection against expected changes, ideally adding behavior with little change to existing code. Polymorphism and generic mechanisms can create useful extension points, but adding abstraction everywhere in anticipation of all possible changes is not implied. ([unclebob-8449a8d4bfdbdd](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2013/03/08/AnOpenAndClosedCase.html) / evidence unclebob-8449a8d4bfdbdd-24d13fc32bd8; [unclebob-8449a8d4bfdbdd](https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2013/03/08/AnOpenAndClosedCase.html) / evidence unclebob-8449a8d4bfdbdd-c4532b5337fc; [unclebob-42518cbd7c1471](https://objectmentor.com/resources/articles/Principles_and_Patterns.pdf) / evidence unclebob-42518cbd7c1471-a4b88df4bb2b; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Substitution and interface segregation. A substitute must preserve what clients can rely on: the earlier formulation says no stronger preconditions and no weaker postconditions. In 2020 Martin corrected his own earlier tendency to frame substitution as inheritance-only; interface implementations and duck types also matter. Keep interfaces oriented to client needs, but avoid an unmanageable interface per client. He reports accepting a substitution violation in one situation rather than paying the cost of further segregation. - **inferred**: Patterns, globals and Singleton. Martin recommends learning recognizable patterns but not hunting for places to insert them. He does not treat every global as inherently wrong. A Singleton can enforce uniqueness across a public API, whereas a trusted internal team may simply create one instance. A process-local Singleton does not enforce one instance across a distributed system. Testability can justify exposing an instance field, but he accepts stronger protection at a public API boundary. - **inferred**: Functional programming and concurrency. Martin sees immutability as useful because it removes races arising from concurrent updates to the same variable, especially in parallel and distributed work. He does not claim it eliminates every concurrency problem or is necessarily the best choice for every small website. He regards functional and object-oriented techniques as compatible and has questioned his own earlier expectation of rapidly multiplying laptop core counts. - **documented**: Types and language design. Martin prefers lightweight dynamic languages and particularly likes Clojure, citing Lisp expressiveness and JVM resources. He argues that practical behavioral tests are needed with either static or dynamic types and worries that accumulating language features can make testing harder. His 2019 comparison with Mark Seemann did not establish which approach was better; he acknowledged not understanding Haskell. Treat his preference as a contested engineering judgment, not a demonstrated equivalence of type systems. - **inferred**: Professional estimates and deadlines. Martin recommends communicating optimistic, likely and pessimistic outcomes rather than presenting an uncertain date as a promise. When a deadline is fixed, explain the scope realistically achievable and offer alternatives. He expects developers to include testing and refactoring in their estimates rather than treat them as optional permission requests. He recognizes that saying no can carry personal risk and that management and sales have genuine constraints. - **inferred**: Emergency exceptions. Martin allows that a production outage or exceptionally short deadline can justify a knowingly messy immediate fix, provided the developer negotiates an opportunity to clean it up afterward. This is a time-bounded risk decision, not a standing exemption from testing and design. His broader oath proposal also admits that defects and poor structure occur; the commitment is not to let known problems accumulate. - **inferred**: Team learning and pairing. Martin treats pairing primarily as knowledge sharing, not a requirement to pair continuously. Simple work or a difficult problem where partners interfere can justify working separately. Teams should nevertheless ensure that others can cover for an absent specialist and agree on shared practices for a common codebase. He also recommends substantial personal study, while acknowledging the trade-off with family and personal life.
