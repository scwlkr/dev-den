# Bjarne Stroustrup: stories

Built from 59 accepted distinct works and 506,419 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: The simulator that exposed the design problem. Stroustrup used Simula for a distributed-systems simulator and valued its classes and static checking, but its implementation and linking costs made the project impractical; he reports that garbage collection consumed more than 80% of execution time even though the simulator generated no garbage. A rewrite in BCPL restored speed but made development and debugging painful. His subsequent requirements sought both organization and systems-level efficiency. The lesson is to separate a language idea from a particular implementation and to insist on both usability and affordability; this case does not establish that every collector or Simula implementation has the same cost. - **documented**: The missing early library. Stroustrup regarded shipping the first release and book without a larger basic library as a serious mistake: users repeatedly rebuilt containers, creating unnecessary diversity. He lacked a sufficiently general, efficient and type-safe container design, and exhaustion and the absence of templates constrained what he could deliver. Later standard facilities addressed much of the gap. The practical lesson is that users experience language and library as one environment; an elegant core does not compensate for missing everyday tools. The case does not imply that any particular early container design would have been adequate. ([bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf) / evidence bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8-137a1e374c97; [bjarne-7d5cdda63bfcb7](https://www.stroustrup.com/std96.pdf) / evidence bjarne-7d5cdda63bfcb7-91c8e0f94689; [bjarne-5877b33c3b0e61](https://www.stroustrup.com/DnE2005.pdf) / evidence bjarne-5877b33c3b0e61-7475707e713f; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Reconsidering the STL. Stroustrup initially found Stepanov’s library unfamiliar and unattractive, but compared it against his actual container criteria: efficiency, static safety, non-intrusiveness, built-in type support and a general framework. It met all but a common-services criterion that he judged nonessential to containers. He credits Alexander Stepanov for the STL, Meng Lee for close implementation work and David Musser for long collaboration, describing his own technical contributions as minor. The lesson is to let an unfamiliar design overturn aesthetic objections when it satisfies explicit requirements; the STL is still not ideal for every numerical or hard-real-time use. ([bjarne-032faa90b6e56c](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl-almost-final.pdf) / evidence bjarne-032faa90b6e56c-45cb0c478a45; [bjarne-032faa90b6e56c](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl-almost-final.pdf) / evidence bjarne-032faa90b6e56c-a1e2b9ba6494; [bjarne-032faa90b6e56c](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl-almost-final.pdf) / evidence bjarne-032faa90b6e56c-9313144f052f; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Making STL standardization feasible. At a 1994 meeting, Stroustrup and library experts cut roughly two-thirds of the proposed STL text, requiring justification that retained facilities could not be cut and would benefit most programmers. Even the reduced library delayed the standard by more than a year. His role was advocacy and scope reduction, not sole invention. The operational lesson is to budget review, implementation and teaching as well as technical completeness; the cost of a smaller proposal must be weighed against the risk of never shipping it. The historical cut is not a universal percentage for later standards work. ([bjarne-032faa90b6e56c](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl-almost-final.pdf) / evidence bjarne-032faa90b6e56c-31d236fa47a6; [bjarne-5877b33c3b0e61](https://www.stroustrup.com/DnE2005.pdf) / evidence bjarne-5877b33c3b0e61-6da137640f9f; [bjarne-7d5cdda63bfcb7](https://www.stroustrup.com/std96.pdf) / evidence bjarne-7d5cdda63bfcb7-cc3245751ac2; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: The narrowing-conversion experiment. Stroustrup tried to ban implicit conversions that might not preserve values, but existing C programs contained many integer-to-character assignments that were safe in context or deliberately truncating. Users would not accept the break, and he called the experiment a failure. Later brace-initialization work offered a way to reject narrowing in a more targeted context. The lesson is to test a safety rule against real code and provide an adoptable path, rather than assuming a globally cleaner rule can be imposed without cost. The later mechanism does not retroactively make all legacy conversions safe. ([bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf) / evidence bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8-2728b508cd9f; [bjarne-4a995c56f1ad83](https://www.stroustrup.com/what-is-2009.pdf) / evidence bjarne-4a995c56f1ad83-4246ba9bc51b; [bjarne-50dd598556cb0b](https://accu.org/journals/overload/29/161/stroustrup/) / evidence bjarne-50dd598556cb0b-c68985e52898; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Optional garbage collection and a mistaken assumption. In the 1995 proposal, Stroustrup believed conservative collection was already compatible with the draft standard. He later corrected that view: a pointer could be written to a file and restored, so storage that appeared unreachable could still matter under the draft rules. He also criticized his own failure to define optional collection precisely. The lesson is to specify semantics and compatibility before relying on an implementation intuition. This correction does not reverse his broader view that optional collection can be useful when its costs are acceptable. ([bjarne-032faa90b6e56c](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl-almost-final.pdf) / evidence bjarne-032faa90b6e56c-85bf736deef0; [bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf) / evidence bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8-d4b092795b1f; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: The export compromise. Stroustrup favored separate compilation of templates for better boundaries and less header-context interference; implementers warned that it would be hard. The resulting export facility worked, but its restrictions and ABI consequences complicated implementation and delivered only part of the expected benefit. He later said both sides were largely right and credited EDG implementers Steve Adamczyk, John Spicer and David Vandevoorde for implementing a feature they had opposed. The lesson is to keep the architectural benefit and implementation burden visible together; the case does not prove that separate compilation is an inherently bad goal. ([bjarne-032faa90b6e56c](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl-almost-final.pdf) / evidence bjarne-032faa90b6e56c-e57ee77ce1a9; [bjarne-5877b33c3b0e61](https://www.stroustrup.com/DnE2005.pdf) / evidence bjarne-5877b33c3b0e61-442818eff290; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: A constructor counterexample to automatic cleanup. In the exception-handling proposal with Andrew Koenig, a constructor increments a live-object count before an acquisition that can throw. If acquisition fails, the incomplete object’s destructor does not run, leaving the count wrong. Incrementing after successful acquisition, or explicitly rolling back and rethrowing, repairs that particular problem. The lesson is to audit external side effects and partial construction, not merely add a destructor and declare the operation exception-safe. The proposal is historical; its example illustrates a design hazard rather than prescribing current exception specifications. ([bjarne-259ce62357d855](https://www.stroustrup.com/except89.pdf) / evidence bjarne-259ce62357d855-c25b630f904e; [bjarne-94f225ac3002e4](https://www.stroustrup.com/except.pdf) / evidence bjarne-94f225ac3002e4-a374299b9605; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Replacing a vector representation safely. Stroustrup shows an assignment that deletes old storage before allocating replacement storage; if allocation throws, the object retains a dangling representation and destruction can delete it again. Constructing the replacement first preserves the old integer-vector value on allocation failure. A separate exception-safety paper compares copy-and-swap with reusing capacity: reuse can be faster but element assignment can leave partial changes. The lesson is to choose and document a guarantee, then order updates accordingly. Generic strong safety also depends on copy, swap and cleanup behavior. ([bjarne-783c826311cb99](https://www.stroustrup.com/eh_brief.pdf) / evidence bjarne-783c826311cb99-23b883eaa981; [bjarne-783c826311cb99](https://www.stroustrup.com/eh_brief.pdf) / evidence bjarne-783c826311cb99-8822140cacd0; [bjarne-94f225ac3002e4](https://www.stroustrup.com/except.pdf) / evidence bjarne-94f225ac3002e4-6dc006a520bc; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: A single-pass iterator mistaken for a multipass range. Andrew Sutton and Stroustrup examine a vector range constructor that can count elements first and allocate efficiently when its input supports multiple passes. Applying that strategy to an input stream iterator is not just a slower choice: consuming or waiting on the stream can change behavior or hang. Their proposed iterator concepts distinguish traversal guarantees from read and write capabilities. The lesson is to make semantic and complexity requirements explicit before selecting an optimized overload; operator availability alone cannot prove multipass behavior. ([bjarne-72241b845beaaf](https://www.stroustrup.com/sle2011-concepts.pdf) / evidence bjarne-72241b845beaaf-df4a06e4c837; [bjarne-72241b845beaaf](https://www.stroustrup.com/sle2011-concepts.pdf) / evidence bjarne-72241b845beaaf-99970709ea40; [bjarne-8499a11781ef93](https://www.stroustrup.com/Concept-based-GP.pdf) / evidence bjarne-8499a11781ef93-59ca26c6601c; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: A tiny list and an unexpected speedup. Stroustrup reports a case where replacing a list containing zero to two elements with a vector doubled an algorithm’s speed, surprising both him and other experts reviewing the code. His broader insertion-and-removal exercise likewise emphasizes memory layout, traversal and cache effects rather than declaring lists universally bad. The lesson is to measure even apparently trivial structures and evaluate the whole operation sequence. The factor of two belongs to that particular case; element size and operation mix can favor a list elsewhere. ([bjarne-c9839d4ff3a827](https://isocpp.org/blog/2014/06/stroustrup-lists) / evidence bjarne-c9839d4ff3a827-5a831cdd4316; [bjarne-c9839d4ff3a827](https://isocpp.org/blog/2014/06/stroustrup-lists) / evidence bjarne-c9839d4ff3a827-cc3a55949e6e; [bjarne-c9839d4ff3a827](https://isocpp.org/blog/2014/06/stroustrup-lists) / evidence bjarne-c9839d4ff3a827-a5da1e6ae3f9; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Refactoring fluidanimate without changing its physics. J. Daniel Garcia and Stroustrup reorganized a C-style C++ application around cell, grid and simulation abstractions, replaced parallel arrays with particle objects, and shared more code across sequential and TBB execution. Counting their supporting library, they report 1,283 versus 1,678 logical lines and lower maximum cyclomatic complexity, while using more classes and functions. Executed instructions fell about 20%, but some L1 access and miss counts rose. The lesson is to inspect clarity and hardware counters together, not infer that every locality metric improved. Results concern one application and tested configurations. ([bjarne-7db324a86881a5](https://www.stroustrup.com/improving_garcia_stroustrup_2015.pdf) / evidence bjarne-7db324a86881a5-8a4047a3cc92; [bjarne-7db324a86881a5](https://www.stroustrup.com/improving_garcia_stroustrup_2015.pdf) / evidence bjarne-7db324a86881a5-92f26a939ddc; [bjarne-7db324a86881a5](https://www.stroustrup.com/improving_garcia_stroustrup_2015.pdf) / evidence bjarne-7db324a86881a5-d6646d6f7eff; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: The rejected dynamic ownership model. Stroustrup experimented with ownership bits attached to pointers, which helped him reason about the problem, but did not publish the model as a C++ solution because per-pointer cost and link incompatibility made it unsuitable. In later work with Herb Sutter and Gabriel Dos Reis, an owner annotation supports static analysis without itself deleting anything or changing the pointer representation. The lesson is to distinguish a useful reasoning model from a deployable one, and to account for ABI and runtime costs. Static annotations still require tools and disciplined use; they are not automatic ownership management. ([bjarne-ebfe802dabed13](https://www.stroustrup.com/resource-model.pdf) / evidence bjarne-ebfe802dabed13-33d891c2356c; [bjarne-ebfe802dabed13](https://www.stroustrup.com/resource-model.pdf) / evidence bjarne-ebfe802dabed13-f3a6f00f02fc; [bjarne-ebfe802dabed13](https://www.stroustrup.com/resource-model.pdf) / evidence bjarne-ebfe802dabed13-c8a0ad6ae0a7; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: The program-representation redesign. Gabriel Dos Reis and Stroustrup first built a symmetric interface-and-implementation lattice using virtual bases for their C++ program representation. They found it large, slow and difficult to maintain, and revised it using template composition, embedded members and fewer indirections. When node comparison later proved a bottleneck, they exposed a node category despite recognizing it as an interface compromise. The lesson is to measure a supposedly clean architecture and preserve only indirections that serve a real choice. Their representation also omitted unexpanded macros, limiting source-to-source work. ([bjarne-5ea0b8dce97913](https://www.stroustrup.com/gdr-bs-macis09.pdf) / evidence bjarne-5ea0b8dce97913-40bbba127b32; [bjarne-5ea0b8dce97913](https://www.stroustrup.com/gdr-bs-macis09.pdf) / evidence bjarne-5ea0b8dce97913-bcbc821357f1; [bjarne-5ea0b8dce97913](https://www.stroustrup.com/gdr-bs-macis09.pdf) / evidence bjarne-5ea0b8dce97913-bd4ffae86c29; 1 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: A fast-cast heuristic needed correction. Michael Gibbs and Stroustrup proposed assigning type IDs using prime factors so inheritance tests could use divisibility rather than runtime hierarchy search. Later, Damian Dechev, Rabi Mahapatra and Stroustrup found that the original priority heuristic underweighted repeated ambiguous bases and revised it to account for them. Their workflow combined model checking, comparison with exhaustive search on modeled hierarchies, and analysis of mismatches. The lesson is to verify an optimization’s assumptions and use counterexamples to improve the heuristic. ID width, pointer adjustment and state-space limits prevent a universal constant-time guarantee from the divisibility test alone. ([bjarne-b6d38e23f58e52](https://www.stroustrup.com/fast_dynamic_casting.pdf) / evidence bjarne-b6d38e23f58e52-29ef7e0f6e0a; [bjarne-b6d38e23f58e52](https://www.stroustrup.com/fast_dynamic_casting.pdf) / evidence bjarne-b6d38e23f58e52-8077ae3d2170; [bjarne-be42606566c159](https://www.stroustrup.com/fdc_jcse.pdf) / evidence bjarne-be42606566c159-596fd83659d3; [bjarne-be42606566c159](https://www.stroustrup.com/fdc_jcse.pdf) / evidence bjarne-be42606566c159-ddcc87864437; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Teaching input without teaching a bug. Stroustrup contrasts a short fixed-buffer input example with string-based input: unchecked input can overflow, while a fixed bound introduces a limit and maintenance concern. He also reports making an EOF-related beginner error in his own low-level version. His role is teacher and critic of the example, not merely designer of a safer library. The lesson is to introduce an abstraction that lets a novice perform the task correctly before requiring manual buffer management, then explain the implementation later. This teaching choice does not eliminate the need to learn low-level techniques for systems work.
