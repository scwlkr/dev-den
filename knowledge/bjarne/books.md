# Bjarne Stroustrup: books

Built from 81 accepted distinct works and 720,818 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: The Design and Evolution of C++ — Stroustrup, 1994. Explains design rationale, alternatives and mistakes under constraints including type safety, hardware access and abstraction. Its useful framework is to evaluate a design against its real purposes and trade-offs rather than an abstract ideal of perfection. The author explicitly distinguishes the book from instruction in programming techniques. - **documented**: The C++ Programming Language — Stroustrup. Established by the evidence as a deeper treatment for experienced programmers. Its first edition also introduced and defined the language without prescribing one programming technique; the second edition placed greater emphasis on effective programming. Use it for depth rather than as a first introduction to programming. - **documented**: A Tour of C++ — Stroustrup. A compact conceptual overview for readers who already program, not an exhaustive reference or a beginner’s programming course. The third-edition discussion identifies RAII, concurrency and C++20 facilities among its subjects. Use the overview to choose techniques, then consult detailed references. - **documented**: Programming: Principles and Practice Using C++ — Stroustrup. Introduces programming to beginners using library abstractions and practical techniques rather than requiring mastery of low-level language details first. The supplied evidence establishes vector and string before pointers in his described course, and regular expressions as a text-manipulation teaching example; the sequence is audience-dependent. - **documented**: The Annotated C++ Reference Manual — Margaret Ellis and Bjarne Stroustrup. Defines the language independently of a particular implementation, including the then-new templates and exceptions, rather than serving as a programming-technique textbook. Stroustrup wrote the manual proper except for the preprocessor section adapted by Ellis; they jointly wrote the annotations. The manual proper became the ANSI standardization basis in March 1990. ([bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8](https://www.stroustrup.com/hopl2.pdf) / evidence bjarne-faf11ea2080fa8-f75a2b2cfd9f; 1 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Foundations for Native C++ Styles — Andrew Koenig and Bjarne Stroustrup. A historical major essay on concrete types, generic algorithms, libraries and runtime interfaces as complementary ways to express application concepts. It teaches invariant-based design and separation of algorithms from containers. Its skepticism about the template constraints then envisioned is historical and must be read alongside Stroustrup’s later concepts work. - **inferred**: C++ in 2005 — Stroustrup. Revisits design and evolution, including STL iterators, exception-safety guarantees, embedded constraints and ideas for C++0x. Its practical lesson is to reassess early design against implementation and user experience, while crediting collaborators such as Dave Abrahams. Its predictions about the next standard are historical, not descriptions of what was adopted.
