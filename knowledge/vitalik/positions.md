# Vitalik Buterin: positions

Built from 57 accepted distinct works and 250,874 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Independent verification and block capacity. Buterin treats affordable validation as part of a blockchain’s security, not merely an operator convenience. In 2014 he warned that having every full node process every transaction could concentrate validation among large businesses. In May 2021 he argued that larger blocks also affect bandwidth, storage, synchronization and censorship resistance. His practical position is not simply to minimize block size: affordable writes matter too. Evaluate a proposed increase against consumer-node costs, catch-up and denial-of-service headroom, and the risk of users abandoning expensive L1 transactions for more centralized alternatives. - **documented**: L1 scaling in an L2-heavy system. In October 2024 Buterin cautioned against an irreversible loss of L1 verification for a modest change in where activity happens. In February 2025 he nevertheless argued that roughly tenfold L1 scaling could be valuable for force inclusion, exits and interoperability, provided verification improves first. His illustrative censorship-bypass calculation reached $4.50 at $2,500 per ETH, 15 gwei and 120,000 gas; the implied scaling needed for a sub-dollar cost depended on uncertain fee elasticity. Gas-limit increases are easy to make and hard to reverse. - **inferred**: Sharding and shared security. In April 2021 he described random assignment of validators to committees as a way to distribute verification while making a targeted shard attack harder than attacking a small independent chain. Tight coupling can revert the wider chain when a bad shard block is discovered, preventing dependent effects from surviving independently. This does not eliminate networking, adaptive-attack or accountability concerns. Evaluate the committee size and attacker assumptions, the number of available participants, and the consequences of reorganization rather than equating sharding with merely increasing block size. - **inferred**: Validity is not data availability. A proof that a transition is valid does not establish that the data needed to reconstruct or exit the system is available. In 2021 Buterin explained why fraud proofs alone cannot establish availability: a publisher can reveal previously withheld data after a challenge. Data-availability sampling instead combines erasure coding, random requests and reconstruction, with correct coding separately verified. Its security still depends on sufficient online sampling and network participation. Apply separate acceptance tests for computation, availability and the ability to act on the data. - **documented**: Optimistic and ZK rollups. In January 2021 Buterin expected optimistic rollups to lead general-purpose use in the short term, while validity-proof systems could prevail more broadly as proving improved. Optimistic designs accept roots subject to a timely fraud challenge; ZK designs prove batches and avoid that challenge delay, but incur proving and implementation costs. His 2022 complexity analysis further favored SNARKs long term because fraud proofs expose synchrony, censorship and incentive assumptions to the wider system. These are conditional comparisons, not proof that any particular rollup is secure. ([vitalik-50e61fec4e1f3b](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/01/05/rollup.html) / evidence vitalik-50e61fec4e1f3b-1a574a42c8be; [vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XW0QZmtbjvs&t=4666) / evidence vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba-f20f0f1ad9d6; [vitalik-14cdbc6b31dc34](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2022/02/28/complexity.html) / evidence vitalik-14cdbc6b31dc34-1be615c20f98; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Sidechains, validiums and Plasma. Buterin distinguishes systems by what happens when their operators or data providers fail. In October 2023 he described a validium’s validity proof as preventing direct invalid-state theft while withholding can still prevent withdrawal. In November 2023 he considered SNARK-enhanced Plasma useful for improving some validiums, but not a complete security solution for every EVM application: shared pools and collateralized positions create difficult exit incentives. Rollups remain his stronger security and developer-experience benchmark; lower-value applications may nevertheless rationally accept weaker guarantees. ([vitalik-e4f2c1afb8d645](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/10/31/l2types.html) / evidence vitalik-e4f2c1afb8d645-76b625a7ab87; [vitalik-09ef70eede81d5](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/14/neoplasma.html) / evidence vitalik-09ef70eede81d5-982404446ab8; [vitalik-e4f2c1afb8d645](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/10/31/l2types.html) / evidence vitalik-e4f2c1afb8d645-6fe54955f4fa; 2 independent supporting works)

- **inferred**: Emergency exits are a capacity problem. A nominal right to exit is not enough if many users must exercise it before the same deadline. In February 2025 Buterin estimated that a week could accommodate about 7.56 million 120,000-gas exits, versus about 121 million idealized 7,500-gas exits, under his assumptions. Fees and throughput are separate constraints. Test mass censorship, a hostile upgrade and per-asset withdrawal requirements, then compare total demand with available L1 space over the actual dispute window. Do not present the illustrative numbers as current capacity guarantees. ([vitalik-329bab0ed23d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/14/l1scaling.html) / evidence vitalik-329bab0ed23d09-4e425b5cfc96; [vitalik-09ef70eede81d5](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/14/neoplasma.html) / evidence vitalik-09ef70eede81d5-5a4066e868f2; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Proof of stake and attack recovery. Buterin’s preference for proof of stake rests partly on avoiding continuous mining expenditure and on making some attacks attributable and slashable. In 2020 and the 2021 interview he differentiated conflicting finality votes, which can produce signed evidence, from censorship, where recovery may require social coordination around a minority fork. He explicitly acknowledged that such coordination was largely untested. Stake lockup also has an opportunity cost, and staking concentration remains an empirical concern; the comparison is not a claim of costless or automatic security. ([vitalik-a8d27c6a0425df](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2020/11/06/pos2020.html) / evidence vitalik-a8d27c6a0425df-c7297f4e6723; [vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XW0QZmtbjvs&t=2286) / evidence vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba-cf85ad6c1e03; [vitalik-7634806eec8d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/pos_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-7634806eec8d09-5dd7ddf1826f; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Long-range attacks and weak subjectivity. His July 2014 discussion treated transactions-as-proof-of-stake as a useful proof of concept, while noting scalability and re-signing problems. By November 2014 he argued that selling obsolete keys or re-signing transactions could make its assumed protection strategically unstable. His later model instead requires a recent authenticated checkpoint for new or long-offline proof-of-stake nodes, because withdrawn validators no longer have deposits at risk. Regularly online nodes can update their own checkpoint; initial and returning nodes have an additional social trust requirement. - **documented**: Finality thresholds and validator participation. In October 2024 he framed faster economic finality, more validators and low node overhead as competing goals. Candidate approaches included stronger signature aggregation, slowly rotating Orbit committees and two-tier staking, each with different security and centralization costs. An illustrative 80% quorum instead of 67% could let a 21% minority prevent a clean victory, but would make pauses in finality more common. These are roadmap options, not deployed guarantees; committee-based designs also complicate interpretation of stake thresholds. - **documented**: Consensus claims must expose their network model. The 2018 high-fault-tolerance construction depends on bounded latency and clock disparity and on observers watching continuously. Its illustrative 512-validator committee with 5% honest stake has a very small probability of containing no honest participant, conditional on sampling and timing assumptions. If both the overlay’s timing assumptions and the underlying chain’s threshold assumptions fail together or in consecutive rounds, the construction can fail. It is not a general escape from asynchronous consensus bounds or a guarantee for offline observers. - **inferred**: Client diversity. Independent implementations reduce correlated implementation risk and monopoly power, but disagreement between clients can itself split consensus. In March 2023 Buterin noted that a faulty client below one-third share can fail without halting the network, whereas a bug affecting a client between one-third and two-thirds can halt finality while blocks continue. These thresholds describe the stated scenario, not every possible bug. Measure actual client shares and independence, test cross-client agreement, and distinguish a client that cannot propose from one that produces invalid consensus messages. - **documented**: MEV and block production. Transaction ordering creates revenue opportunities that can favor sophisticated, large-scale operators. In the 2021 interview Buterin discussed separating specialized searchers from simpler proposers, while acknowledging that this firewall could have a fatal flaw. In December 2021 he considered centralized production with decentralized validation and censorship-resistant bypasses a possible endgame; he did not call that outcome certain. By October 2024 he emphasized the remaining tension: giving stakers meaningful inclusion authority can itself create MEV incentives to consolidate or outsource. - **documented**: Multidimensional gas. A single weighted gas limit treats different resources as interchangeable even when bandwidth, execution and state access have independent worst cases. In May 2024 Buterin favored separate targets, limits and prices where they safely admit more useful work, while noting harder builder packing and EVM subcall accounting. The draft EIP-7999, coauthored by Anders Elowsson, Buterin and Maria Silva and created in 2025, proposes a unified fee budget across dimensions. It remains a draft; independent limits can also make censorship by stuffing a particular resource cheaper. - **documented**: Fee-market incentives. The coauthored EIP-1559, created in 2019 and subsequently revised, specifies an adjustable base fee, an elasticity multiplier of two, and a bounded adjustment denominator of eight. Burning the base fee is intended in part to remove a miner incentive to manipulate it for additional fee revenue; priority fees remain separate. The authors also warn that clients must handle maximum-size bursts and that the relationship between issuance and demand-driven burning makes future supply change uncertain. The EIP’s creation date should not be applied to every revision. - **documented**: Complexity should be judged at interfaces. In February 2022 Buterin distinguished internally difficult components with simple external interfaces from complexity that spreads assumptions and interactions across the protocol. Pairing-based BLS can absorb complexity while simplifying aggregation; fraud-proof systems may look simpler internally but impose network and incentive caveats elsewhere. He treats this as a moderate preference, not an absolute rule: large implementations still contain bugs, interfaces can change, and some systemic complexity can avoid a much larger internal burden. Specification length alone is not an adequate risk score. - **documented**: Statelessness and commitment choices. In 2021 Buterin described Verkle trees as a way to obtain compact witnesses at the price of more complicated cryptography and eventual quantum exposure. In October 2024 he compared them with STARKed binary trees: the latter offer a different balance of hash confidence and proving time. He also noted that numerous individually small, updatable proofs for mempools or RPC use may favor direct Merkle branches over STARKs. Choose by workload, worst-case witness size, migration cost and cryptographic assumptions, not proof size alone. - **documented**: ZK-EVM compatibility. Buterin’s August 2022 taxonomy treats compatibility as a spectrum rather than a ranking. Type 1 preserves Ethereum execution-layer consensus logic and is suitable for proving L1, but inherits difficult proving costs. Type 2 preserves EVM behavior while changing external structures; Type 3 changes some behavior or precompiles; Type 4 compiles high-level languages to a different target and can break bytecode-dependent tooling. His long-term hope was multiple independent Type 1 implementations. The right choice depends on required equivalence, prover performance and migration risk. - **documented**: L1 proving must handle adversarial blocks. In October 2024 Buterin emphasized both independent prover correctness and worst-case proving latency: an attacker can deliberately construct a block that is much harder to prove than an average one. His discussion targeted proving any block in roughly four seconds, using combinations of parallelism, proof optimization and gas repricing. Specialized hardware may be a more acceptable trade-off for an L2 than for L1, where broad proof-generation capacity and avoidance of a single-vendor bottleneck matter. These were research targets, not achieved guarantees. - **documented**: Enshrinement is a trade-off, not a minimalism contest. In September 2023 Buterin argued that protocol support can reduce shared fixed costs and make user operations legible to inclusion mechanisms, while also imposing coordination and maintenance costs on everyone. Account abstraction may add protocol machinery while removing the fixed assumption that all accounts use one signature scheme. He considered private-mempool enshrinement premature without a sufficiently trustworthy encryption approach. Assess the specific benefit of native support, the alternatives above L1, and whether the proposed rule restricts future users unnecessarily. - **documented**: Do not overload Ethereum consensus with application disputes. In May 2023 Buterin distinguished voluntary reuse of staked ETH, where losses remain with participating validators and users, from designs expecting Ethereum’s social consensus to fork or reorganize to rescue an application. External duties can import political disputes, raise validator attention and software costs, and favor large applications that are perceived as too important to fail. His currency-oracle crisis is a hypothetical, not an observed incident. Prefer application-specific recovery mechanisms and explicitly test whether users have time and capacity to exit. - **documented**: Bridge security is directional. In October 2023 Buterin argued that checking another chain’s signatures does not prevent its validators from signing an invalid transition. A validity-proof bridge can restrict that failure but still cannot ensure withheld data is available. Two-way verification, upgrade governance and exceptional forks remain separate requirements. Reducing bridge dependence through origin-chain assets and atomic swaps is another option. - **inferred**: Privacy and authenticity are different properties. In 2016 Buterin stressed that a blockchain authenticates data but does not make it confidential; encryption and key management must be designed separately, and transaction metadata can remain visible. Zero-knowledge proofs can establish a property without disclosing inputs, while optimistic private contracts may reveal their code in a dispute. Multiparty computation introduces participant and communication assumptions, including the possibility that participants retain shares and collude later. Choose the privacy mechanism against the actual leakage and adversary model, not a generic privacy label. - **documented**: Formal analysis and its boundaries. In the 2021 interview Buterin valued proofs within their model but warned that incentive and implementation failures outside the model may be more important. In May 2026 he argued for end-to-end verification where the security goal is simpler than the implementation, while emphasizing incomplete specifications, unverified components and hardware leakage. Independent implementations help but can share an error. His position is to combine formal proofs with testing, review and explicit assumptions, not to infer that a checked theorem automatically captures the intended security property. - **documented**: History and state have different persistence costs. In October 2024 Buterin argued that historical data can be verified against commitments and served by a distributed network, provided at least one provider retains the requested data. He preferred building distributed history storage before relying on pruning, and noted compatibility issues for older clients. State expiry is harder: dormant assets must remain recoverable and existing applications must remain workable. Partial expiry with proof-based resurrection is one option, but grouping creates renewal and griefing incentives. These were research directions, not completed protocol decisions. - **documented**: Future VM simplification. In October 2024 Buterin described EOF as a way to make EVM code more analyzable, but noted that retaining both old and new behavior adds protocol complexity. In May 2025 he proposed considering a longer transition to RISC-V or another prover-friendly VM, potentially running existing EVM contracts through an interpreter. His large performance estimate was prospective, not guaranteed, and a new VM would take longer to benefit developers. The operational choice is between incremental compatibility gains and a more disruptive, potentially simpler long-term architecture.
