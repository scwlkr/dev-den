# Vitalik Buterin: positions

Built from 130 accepted distinct works and 522,002 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Independent verification. Ethereum should remain verifiable by a meaningful number of users on accessible hardware, because validators cannot impose invalid rules on clients that reject them. In 2017 Buterin allowed that not everyone needs a full node if a sufficiently diverse population validates; in 2024 he pursued statelessness and proofs to make verification cheaper. The practical test is not whether a benchmark node works, but whether ordinary participants can keep up under adversarial load. ([vitalik-4701d1bc2ca340](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/05/08/coordination_problems.html) / evidence vitalik-4701d1bc2ca340-f49e8bf764b9; [vitalik-3058c3c3e458b7](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/10/23/futures4.html) / evidence vitalik-3058c3c3e458b7-7d684a6e445b; [vitalik-adec5a26b2dff4](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/05/17/decentralization.html) / evidence vitalik-adec5a26b2dff4-9c0b8f2a0189; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Client diversity. Independent implementations contain client-specific failures and reduce the political leverage of a monopoly implementation, but disagreements can themselves split consensus. His March 2023 analysis uses one-third as an important operational boundary: failure of a client below that share need not stop finality, while a larger affected share can stop finality even if blocks continue. The guidance is to measure correlated implementation share, not just count nodes. ([vitalik-04ead53cc3ed62](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/03/31/zkmulticlient.html) / evidence vitalik-04ead53cc3ed62-3ac13f9572ff; [vitalik-04ead53cc3ed62](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/03/31/zkmulticlient.html) / evidence vitalik-04ead53cc3ed62-2e54932eb9ec; [vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XW0QZmtbjvs&t=6082) / evidence vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba-c8b49c4ab463; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Proof of stake. Buterin moved from the 2014 Slasher contingency, which retained low-reward proof of work, toward a strong preference for proof of stake on energy, issuance and attack-recovery grounds. Slashing can punish provable equivocation, but censorship recovery may require social coordination. He also acknowledges stake concentration, lockup costs and weak subjectivity; lower electricity use alone does not prove decentralization. ([vitalik-09f4dd55219dce](https://blog.ethereum.org/2014/01/15/slasher-a-punitive-proof-of-stake-algorithm) / evidence vitalik-09f4dd55219dce-f1d17673f27c; [vitalik-a8d27c6a0425df](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2020/11/06/pos2020.html) / evidence vitalik-a8d27c6a0425df-c7297f4e6723; [vitalik-a8d27c6a0425df](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2020/11/06/pos2020.html) / evidence vitalik-a8d27c6a0425df-c2b52f884b90; [vitalik-7634806eec8d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/pos_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-7634806eec8d09-5dd7ddf1826f; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Weak subjectivity. By November 2014, Buterin treated a recent trusted checkpoint as necessary for new or long-offline proof-of-stake nodes: withdrawn validators can sign old histories without current deposits at risk. His earlier suggestion that particular long-range attacks could be constrained by timestamps or endorsements was not a solution to every long-range attack. A deployment must specify how checkpoints are obtained and refreshed, rather than promising trustless synchronization from arbitrary absence. ([vitalik-1b86035904bdd7](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2014/05/15/long-range-attacks-the-serious-problem-with-adaptive-proof-of-work) / evidence vitalik-1b86035904bdd7-3627170a62b0; [vitalik-a084525d5e4ed2](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2014/11/25/proof-stake-learned-love-weak-subjectivity) / evidence vitalik-a084525d5e4ed2-94fe18aefb05; [vitalik-7634806eec8d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/pos_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-7634806eec8d09-0f3cea2fb608; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Economic finality. Security claims should identify the signed violation, the capital at risk and the losses actually enforceable. The 2017 proof-of-stake FAQ describes accountable safety as requiring at least one-third of stake to violate a slashable rule if conflicting blocks finalize. This is different from saying one-third of all stake can always attack or that censorship can be automatically proved. Buterin and Virgil Griffith's Casper FFG paper describes a historical proof-of-stake finality overlay on proof of work, not the later beacon-chain protocol. ([vitalik-7634806eec8d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/pos_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-7634806eec8d09-88b99c077fb6; [vitalik-7634806eec8d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/pos_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-7634806eec8d09-f304c9bcf4a2; [vitalik-42c57df8a3f520](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.09437) / evidence vitalik-42c57df8a3f520-da218bf95fc9; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Finality speed and validator access. In October 2024 Buterin sought faster finality and a lower staking minimum without imposing prohibitive signature-processing costs. Brute-force aggregation, slowly rotating Orbit committees and two-tier staking trade cryptographic complexity against attack cost and participation. A small committee is not equivalent to the whole validator set: only implicated participants may be penalized. Choose parameters against worst-case economic security and node load, not latency alone. ([vitalik-0c860552d137f6](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/10/14/futures1.html) / evidence vitalik-0c860552d137f6-d9c525e87f91; [vitalik-0c860552d137f6](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/10/14/futures1.html) / evidence vitalik-0c860552d137f6-bb09f22d51f5; [vitalik-43dbe2338f3532](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/06/30/epochslot.html) / evidence vitalik-43dbe2338f3532-cde09eeb14c5; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Slot latency and geography. Buterin favored approximately four-second blocks in a 2015 Casper discussion, but his October 2024 analysis emphasizes that four-second slots imply an approximately two-second network-latency bound and may exclude distant validators. Preconfirmations can improve typical user experience without fixing the worst case when a proposer is unavailable. Faster confirmation should be evaluated against geographic participation and the economic incentives to colocate. ([vitalik-90ad0d519fdc80](https://blog.ethereum.org/2015/10/18/vitaliks-research-and-ecosystem-update) / evidence vitalik-90ad0d519fdc80-c7618cb57e85; [vitalik-0c860552d137f6](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/10/14/futures1.html) / evidence vitalik-0c860552d137f6-4c6f8d30fbef; [vitalik-6bac86e6398bf3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rNSnYIjoqOM&t=5648) / evidence vitalik-6bac86e6398bf3-e08f7cce417b; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Network assumptions. Fault-tolerance percentages are conditional, not portable labels. His 2018 high-fault-tolerance construction depends on bounded latency and clock differences and on observers actively watching consensus. If both its timing assumptions and the underlying threshold assumptions fail together or in consecutive rounds, the overlay can fail. For any proposed protocol, separate safety and liveness under synchrony, partitions and offline recovery before comparing percentages. ([vitalik-0e412dfcd50a31](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2018/08/07/99_fault_tolerant.html) / evidence vitalik-0e412dfcd50a31-768aecbe1362; [vitalik-0e412dfcd50a31](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2018/08/07/99_fault_tolerant.html) / evidence vitalik-0e412dfcd50a31-e760d2637982; [vitalik-7634806eec8d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/pos_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-7634806eec8d09-e3093a490773; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Scaling and shared security. The scalability trilemma frames the conflict between throughput beyond a consumer node, decentralized participation and resistance to substantial attackers. Randomly assigned committees can share security across shards, unlike independent chains whose security is divided, but sampling alone is vulnerable to stronger bribery and adaptive-attack models. A design must distribute state, execution and networking work while explaining how a light verifier detects invalidity and missing data. ([vitalik-144cf7610a496a](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/04/07/sharding.html) / evidence vitalik-144cf7610a496a-185751372ab4; [vitalik-144cf7610a496a](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/04/07/sharding.html) / evidence vitalik-144cf7610a496a-c6491fbb4de2; [vitalik-5e6980cdfe7298](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/sharding_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-5e6980cdfe7298-67ea4030b39e; [vitalik-5e6980cdfe7298](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2017/12/31/sharding_faq.html) / evidence vitalik-5e6980cdfe7298-91686d623d75; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Data availability. Validity and availability are separate guarantees. A fraud proof can demonstrate incorrect computation when inputs are available, but cannot by itself prove that a publisher withheld data. Data-availability sampling combines erasure coding and random requests, with correct coding and enough online clients as additional requirements. EIP-4844 created separate blob space, but Buterin explicitly noted in March 2024 that it did not itself implement sampling. ([vitalik-144cf7610a496a](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/04/07/sharding.html) / evidence vitalik-144cf7610a496a-63080c62813a; [vitalik-144cf7610a496a](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/04/07/sharding.html) / evidence vitalik-144cf7610a496a-db40e12250d7; [vitalik-144cf7610a496a](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/04/07/sharding.html) / evidence vitalik-144cf7610a496a-9707860f4541; [vitalik-81da7bf4e1d477](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2024/03/28/blobs.html) / evidence vitalik-81da7bf4e1d477-6d6a17c4e643; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Rollups versus validiums and sidechains. Buterin distinguishes security by concrete exit and data guarantees rather than branding. A rollup publishes sufficient data to the base layer and uses validity proofs or fraud challenges; a validium can prevent invalid state transitions while data withholding can strand funds; a sidechain depends more directly on its own consensus. In 2021 he nevertheless regarded an available sidechain as a pragmatic near-term option, provided its weaker security was understood. ([vitalik-50e61fec4e1f3b](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/01/05/rollup.html) / evidence vitalik-50e61fec4e1f3b-5448e1a4a2b8; [vitalik-e4f2c1afb8d645](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/10/31/l2types.html) / evidence vitalik-e4f2c1afb8d645-76b625a7ab87; [vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XW0QZmtbjvs&t=4808) / evidence vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba-28aa8f998e3c; [vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XW0QZmtbjvs&t=4978) / evidence vitalik-24c69fa0f165ba-37cce08f8772; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Validity versus fraud proofs. In 2021 Buterin expected optimistic rollups to mature sooner for general-purpose execution but validity proofs to become preferable as proving improved, because valid withdrawals need not wait through a fraud challenge. His 2022 complexity argument also favors validity proofs for encapsulating more of the security machinery. This is conditional on proof-system soundness, implementation quality and practical proving cost; validity proofs do not provide missing data. ([vitalik-50e61fec4e1f3b](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/01/05/rollup.html) / evidence vitalik-50e61fec4e1f3b-1a574a42c8be; [vitalik-14cdbc6b31dc34](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2022/02/28/complexity.html) / evidence vitalik-14cdbc6b31dc34-1be615c20f98; [vitalik-81bc7f865d7a80](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2020/08/17/philosophy.html) / evidence vitalik-81bc7f865d7a80-c10362213d36; 3 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Plasma. Plasma can give owners exit rights without publishing all transaction data, but ownership, monitoring and challenge deadlines are substantive assumptions. His 2019 critique identified problems with shared state; in November 2023 he revisited Plasma with SNARKs and parallel UTXO representations, while retaining the warning that EVM pools and collateralized debt positions are not universally protected. It can improve some validiums without becoming equivalent to a data-available rollup. ([vitalik-b9d0ef2ac12d3b](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2019/08/28/hybrid_layer_2.html) / evidence vitalik-b9d0ef2ac12d3b-578a096bbc07; [vitalik-09ef70eede81d5](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/14/neoplasma.html) / evidence vitalik-09ef70eede81d5-5fb9aaf1d189; [vitalik-09ef70eede81d5](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/14/neoplasma.html) / evidence vitalik-09ef70eede81d5-982404446ab8; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: L1 capacity as an L2 backstop. Buterin opposes both indiscriminate block growth and making L1 prohibitively scarce. In February 2025 he argued for roughly tenfold L1 scaling if verification improvements preserve decentralization: force inclusion, asset issuance, cross-L2 operations and mass exits all need real capacity. His illustrative calculation puts a 120,000-gas bypass at $4.50 under stated prices; fee elasticity and simultaneous demand make the result uncertain. ([vitalik-04ead53cc3ed62](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/03/31/zkmulticlient.html) / evidence vitalik-04ead53cc3ed62-284754847d5f; [vitalik-329bab0ed23d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/14/l1scaling.html) / evidence vitalik-329bab0ed23d09-d099263450fb; [vitalik-329bab0ed23d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/14/l1scaling.html) / evidence vitalik-329bab0ed23d09-ed93f3f8e041; [vitalik-329bab0ed23d09](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/14/l1scaling.html) / evidence vitalik-329bab0ed23d09-4e425b5cfc96; 2 independent supporting works)

- **documented**: Resource pricing. A single gas total is convenient but can underuse safe capacity when calldata, execution and state have different bottlenecks. Dencun's EIP-4844 separated blob prices and limits; Buterin's May 2024 analysis considers further dimensions, while acknowledging harder block packing and EVM subcall accounting. The later draft EIP-7999, coauthored with Anders Elowsson and Maria Silva, explores a common fee budget across dimensions; it is a proposal, not evidence of deployment. - **documented**: Fee-market incentives. The coauthored EIP-1559 adjusts a base fee around a target and allows temporary block expansion; burning the base fee is intended to reduce block producers' incentive to manipulate it for revenue. The EIP also warns clients to handle maximum-size bursts and does not promise a fixed ETH supply: issuance and demand-driven burn determine the outcome. Its April 2019 creation date does not date every revision of the living specification. - **documented**: MEV and block production. Specialized ordering can create economies of scale that pull validators toward centralized operators. Buterin supports both reducing extraction and separating sophisticated building from simpler validation, while recognizing that builders can then censor. By May 2024 he favored stronger proposer-selected inclusion lists and less builder discretion than in earlier builder-heavy approaches. Inclusion is not the same as fair ordering, and any authority retained by stakers can itself become MEV-relevant. - **documented**: Sequencing models. In June 2024 Buterin presented based rollups, a server with proof and exit safeguards, and fast committee chains as legitimate design categories with different trade-offs. In an August 2025 interview he reported becoming less enthusiastic about based L2s and more interested in independent sequencers that absorb low-latency demand, potentially replaceable through decentralized governance. Neither position removes the need for practical censorship bypass and adequate L1 exit capacity. - **documented**: ZK-EVM compatibility. His August 2022 taxonomy treats Ethereum-equivalent Type 1, EVM-equivalent Type 2, repriced Type 2.5, partially compatible Type 3 and source-language-oriented Type 4 as trade-off points, not a quality ranking. Type 1 supports reuse and L1 verification but inherits expensive proving of existing components; less exact types improve prover efficiency while breaking some assumptions or tools. - **documented**: EVM evolution. Buterin's September 2024 position emphasized the durable value of EVM familiarity and targeted precompiles. In May 2025 he proposed a more ambitious transition toward RISC-V or another prover-friendly VM, preserving old EVM behavior through an interpreter; the large performance gain was a prospective estimate, not a measured guarantee. The underlying choice is between incremental compatibility and the cost, delay and risk of a larger migration. - **documented**: Account abstraction. Programmable validation enables recovery, alternative signatures and contract-paid gas, but moving rules out of the protocol can transfer complexity to mempools and fee payment. Buterin's early EIP-86 minimalism evolved through EIP-2938 and ERC-4337; in 2023–2024 he considered selective enshrinement for efficiency and censorship-resistant inclusion. Any design must address shared-state multi-invalidation and bound unpaid validation work without foreclosing privacy or post-quantum accounts. - **documented**: Privacy and proof limits. A zero-knowledge proof can establish eligibility or correct computation without exposing inputs, but it does not automatically conceal transaction metadata, prevent coercion or create shared private state that nobody knows. MPC can provide shared private computation under additional participant assumptions; a malicious majority may compromise privacy. Stealth addresses additionally need private gas funding and scanning, while wallet recovery must protect private data as well as signing keys. - **documented**: Oracles and consensus scope. Smart contracts can escrow assets but cannot derive external prices or physical facts from computation alone. Buterin prefers application-selected oracles over a canonical L1 price oracle, because disputed real-world facts can import political conflicts into consensus. His 2023 position allows validator restaking when losses stay with consenting participants, but warns against making Ethereum social consensus responsible for rescuing external applications. - **documented**: Governance and collusion. Buterin opposes making tightly coupled coin-holder voting the default for base-layer decisions. Small holders may rationally sell votes even when collective harm exceeds the bribe, and borrowing, wrappers or custodians can separate voting power from economic exposure. Delays, constrained powers and opt-in upgrades reduce capture payoffs but do not solve every governance problem, especially discretionary public-goods allocation. His critique is not a rejection of every on-chain vote or of all experimentation with futarchy.
