- **documented**: Ethereum Whitepaper — major essay, historical living edition dated 2014; metadata also refers to 2013. Buterin's platform thesis is that a general-purpose contract layer can share blockchain security and an economic environment across applications. It teaches state-transition modeling, separation of consensus from execution, persistent contract accounts, Merkle commitments, and metered computation. Its oracle example also shows that on-chain escrow does not eliminate trust in external prices. Treat issuance, proof-of-work, and scalability proposals as historical design arguments, not the current Ethereum specification. - **documented**: Obfuscation — two-part technical essay series dated June and July 2026 in the supplied sources. Buterin explains indistinguishability obfuscation and compares a conservative construction tower with Diamond iO. The taught lens is to separate asymptotic feasibility, concrete runtime, and the maturity of security assumptions: the simpler Diamond construction improves efficiency but remains impractical and relies on newer assumptions needing scrutiny.
