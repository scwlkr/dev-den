# Jonathan Blow: stories

Built from 51 accepted distinct works and 321,859 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Braid image corruption, 2016. Blow investigated a recurring stripe by tracing image data backward through the asset pipeline. Finding it in source BMPs as well as DDS output weakened his initial suspicion of compression; debugger assertions eventually exposed an asynchronous JPEG-loading path behind an apparently synchronous interface. Waiting for completion fixed the immediate problem, and he temporarily removed JPEG support to eliminate the old threading path. The operational lesson is to inspect the data at successive boundaries and verify completion semantics before blaming the last visible transformation. The account does not prove that every similar stripe has the same cause. - **documented**: Bitmap conversion mistake, 2016. During cleanup Blow rushed an in-place vertical flip and byte swap and overlooked a middle row being swapped twice. He later identified his own desire to go fast as part of the failure and preferred an understandable loop guard to obscure bounds arithmetic. The useful practice is to stop and reason through boundary cases before optimizing the edit, then test representative dimensions. The supplied account does not independently validate every odd-height case, so its suggested correction should still be tested rather than copied as a proven general algorithm. - **documented**: Destruction policy misread, 2016. Blow spent about an hour analyzing how to avoid modifying an entity array during iteration before remembering that Braid schedules destruction rather than performing it immediately. Old two-pass collection code suggested an obsolete restriction. He later searched for and removed redundant destruction buffers, then checked that the game compiled and ran. The lesson is to establish the current invariant before designing a workaround, and to remove misleading remnants when safe. A successful compile and run is useful evidence, not exhaustive validation of a broad cleanup. - **documented**: Braid particle randomness, 2016 retrospective. Reconstructing particles deterministically allowed rewind without storing every particle state, under constraints including fixed lifetime and emission behavior. Blow found correlations after repeated reseeding and shipped a fixed-seed table plus mixing workaround. He explicitly did not recommend the workaround as a general RNG solution and acknowledged both limited expertise and the pressure of finishing the game. Later, a cleaner replacement removed code but appeared more expensive than the visual workload required. The case separates a shipping decision from a claim of technical optimality. - **documented**: Unsafe asset-tool output, 2016. Blow used a separate experimental repository for a disruptive Braid refactor and tested the complete packaging and launch path on another machine, exposing missing checked-in headers and configuration. - **documented**: Quaternion and line representations. Blow reports that an earlier slope–intercept representation worked until lines rotated toward vertical, producing special cases and ill-conditioned arithmetic. He moved to an implicit line equation, spending an extra number to gain more orientation-independent behavior. The broader decision is to price exceptional cases and numerical conditioning alongside storage size. The representation does not transfer unchanged to a line in 3D: an implicit linear equation there describes a plane, while point-plus-direction is a general line representation. - **documented**: Mipmapping and the ideal filter, 2001–2002. Blow tested progressively wider filters and saw prominent ringing on some images beyond roughly 14 Kaiser samples, with no apparent improvement even at much greater widths. He then tested an ideal frequency cutoff using Fourier transforms; objectionable ripples remained. This changed his understanding: antialiasing is a means, while the actual objective is acceptable visual output. Filter in linear light when conserving light energy matters, but inspect real images as well as frequency response. The figures are absent from the supplied material, and sample-specific thresholds are not universal. - **documented**: Lerp as a real workload test, 2004. Blow ported a small 3D application to his experimental language and deliberately implemented its fundamental Vector3 with the database facilities under evaluation rather than hiding the hard part behind C++ wrappers. The demonstration ran at 30 frames per second on a 1.5 GHz Pentium-M despite linked-list databases and significant interpreter overhead. His conclusion was that this specific workload had headroom, not that database scans or interpreter costs were generally negligible. Stress the proposed mechanism directly, then distinguish a successful demonstration from a broad performance claim. - **documented**: Compiler team alignment, 2020. Blow said that when the compiler team grew, he had assumed talks and source code adequately conveyed the project's unusual goals. Contributors filled gaps with familiar language-design practices, and changes could diverge faster than he could review them. He attributed part of the failure to his own communication and planned to improve alignment before expanding again. The lesson is to make design constraints inspectable and reviewable before treating headcount as throughput. This is a report about his project, not a measured staffing law. - **documented**: Language, engine, and game together. Blow and the team built a new language and engine alongside a substantial game, carrying over some editor and animation code while writing a new renderer. He later estimated that reusing The Witness's engine might have saved two to four years, while emphasizing that the counterfactual is uncertain and other work overlapped. The project illustrates both the value of a serious application for testing infrastructure and the schedule risk of coupling several ambitious undertakings. He expects to reuse the resulting foundation rather than repeat the full investment. - **documented**: Inertia-tensor collaboration, 2004. Blow and Binstock developed an approach that decomposes a triangle-mesh calculation into tetrahedra and sums their contributions. Blow preferred calculating the covariance or second mass moment first for a more symmetric interpretation. He also corrected his initial impression of novelty after finding prior work, and credited Binstock with fixing calculus errors and producing the first working implementation. The case shows both mathematical reframing and attribution discipline. The linked derivation and sample code are not supplied, so this summary is not an implementation specification.
