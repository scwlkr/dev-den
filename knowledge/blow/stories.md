# Jonathan Blow: stories

Built from 80 accepted distinct works and 595,304 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Braid image corruption, 2016: Blow traced a recurring stripe backward from compressed textures to source bitmaps, initially suspecting the compressor or bitmap writer. Debugger assertions exposed a JPEG load that appeared synchronous through its API but actually completed asynchronously; waiting fixed the corruption. The operational lesson is to inspect each pipeline boundary and its timing contract before blaming the final output stage. This establishes the cause of his specific corruption, not that every similar stripe is an asynchronous-load defect. - **documented**: Braid entity destruction cleanup, 2016: As the original programmer, Blow spent time reasoning that destruction during iteration might be unsafe, then remembered that entities were scheduled for later destruction rather than removed immediately. Old two-pass buffering code had preserved the appearance of an obsolete restriction. He removed the misleading idiom after checking the real invariant. The lesson is to verify current lifetime and mutation rules before redesigning around historical code; the cleanup still needed normal testing. - **documented**: Rewindable particles, 2016 retrospective: Blow reconstructed particles from time, deterministic per-particle seeds, and cheap evolution equations rather than storing every particle state. Constant emission and bounded lifetime made active indices recoverable. His original reseeding produced visible correlations, and a fixed table plus mixing shipped as a workaround under time pressure. He later acknowledged inadequate RNG understanding and questioned replacements that cleaned up code but cost more runtime. The method depends on the stated particle constraints and does not make its original random-number workaround a general recommendation. - **documented**: Compiler declaration fast path, 2018: Blow instrumented a common declaration case and implemented a shortcut, discovering that imports, conditional compilation, and address-space placement constrained what could safely be skipped. A noisy measurement suggested only about 1.3 percent improvement, so he archived and removed the change rather than keep complexity because the work was already done. The figure is not a universal rejection threshold: the decision reflected this compiler, the measurement uncertainty, and the available alternative of a later scheduler redesign. - **documented**: Polymorphism and yielding, 2018: While debugging an order-sensitive compiler failure, Blow used seeded scheduling to reproduce a bad processing order. He found that a yielded inference path left a type slot populated, causing a later attempt to mistake unfinished work for completed inference. He first made cleanup explicit and tested it, then rearranged the logic to remove that extra bookkeeping. The case illustrates why a passing ordinary build is insufficient when correctness depends on scheduling; the transcript does not expose enough code to reconstruct the full implementation. - **documented**: Compiler type query false positive, 2018: Blow investigated an assertion expansion that triggered an outer-stack-frame error. A type query needed the variable's type, not its runtime value, so requiring a closure capture rejected a valid case. He added context-sensitive handling and tested both the type-query case and the real capture error. He also regarded the added flag and processing as a cost worth revisiting. The lesson is to preserve the intended safety check while separating semantic contexts, not to disable the check globally. - **documented**: Procedure-call redesign, 2018: A real need for configurable string concatenation exposed Blow's mistaken assumption that nothing useful should follow a varargs parameter. His first compiler redesign made related paths more complicated, and he stopped rather than declare a nearly working result sufficient. He regretted not saving a local checkpoint of the earlier design. A follow-up completed the feature and removed specialized wrappers. The case supports both checkpointing risky work and containing rare foreign-call complexity, not an assumption that the first redesign succeeded. - **documented**: Fog animation and draw frequency, 2018: Blow observed inconsistent fog fading and found that animation state was updated inside drawing. When wrapping caused the same content to be drawn more than once per frame, the update happened multiple times. He moved the update out of drawing. Earlier he had mapped opacity state to square size to verify that the data changed independently of the shader. The case shows how to separate simulation frequency from rendering frequency and expose intermediate state; it does not settle the final fog design. - **documented**: Mirror gameplay and animation timing, 2018: Blow debugged mirrored characters that disappeared when they should duplicate. An anticipation delay meant their source positions still coincided when old merge logic ran, even though the gameplay operation was already underway. He criticized himself for spending too long in the debugger before reasoning through the timing. The practical lesson is to state which position and time each subsystem observes and test transitions, not merely final poses. - **documented**: Mipmap filtering, 2001–2002: Blow evaluated filters using frequency response and actual images. Wider Kaiser filters produced prominent ringing on some test images, and even a Fourier-domain ideal cutoff retained objectionable ripples. He revised his assumption that antialiasing itself was the end goal: the goal was acceptable visual artifacts. Filtering gamma-encoded values also required conversion to linear light to preserve energy. These are image- and pipeline-dependent findings, not a single filter-width rule for every texture. - **documented**: Unified LOD seams, 2003: Blow began with a height-field simplification while requiring a plausible route to arbitrary meshes. In the later generalization, he moved from reasoning about octree neighbors to triangles and seam-vertex provenance: a triangle touches at most three chunks through its vertices. Rewriting seam references during simplification reduced explicit neighbor-case reasoning. He also dropped the one-level neighbor constraint because it could propagate unwanted detail. The articles describe a developing system and samples, not a turnkey production renderer. - **documented**: Building the game and language together: Blow deliberately used a substantial game to test the compiler, allowing real engine work to reveal missing features and rough edges. He also recognized the cost: the game and engine grew beyond their initial scope, and compiler work competed with shipping the game. His account supports dogfooding as a way to discover real constraints, but not as a free validation strategy or a reason for every team to build its own language. - **documented**: Compiler team alignment, 2020: Blow said that, when the team grew, he had assumed talks and source code made the project's unusual goals sufficiently clear. Contributors understandably applied familiar language-design practices, and changes could diverge faster than he could review them. He took responsibility for the communication failure and wanted better alignment before scaling again. The lesson is not that delegation is inherently bad: he also delegates specialized backend debugging to the teammate who knows that code. - **documented**: Cross-machine packaging test, 2016: For disruptive Braid cleanup, Blow used a separate repository and tested the complete build, packaging, upload, download, and launch path on another machine. That exposed missing checked-in headers and configurations hidden by his development environment.
