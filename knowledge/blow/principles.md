# Jonathan Blow: principles

Built from 51 accepted distinct works and 321,859 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **inferred**: Remove work before making each operation cheaper. Inspect whether a representation forces repeated manipulation, synchronization, or calls that can be eliminated. Only then tune the remaining hot path. This does not mean instruction-level work is never worthwhile: Blow learned SIMD for Braid particles, then stopped when it was time to work on the rest of the game. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Judge an abstraction by the benefit it actually delivers. A shared interface, format, or pattern can still leave the application-specific work and failure modes intact. Ask what code, errors, or constraints really disappear, and what new interactions are introduced. Do not reject all abstraction: a useful representation can eliminate whole categories of work. - **inferred**: Budget feature interactions, not just feature count. A feature can be valuable alone while its combinations with inference, overloads, tooling, or other features consume disproportionate implementation and correctness capacity. Spend that capacity on the problems users actually encounter, and reconsider features that demand complex special cases. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Protect iteration speed as an engineering capability. Fast compile, edit, and debug cycles allow more ideas to be tried while preserving attention and morale. A clean build can also avoid incremental-build failure modes. Do not generalize a particular demonstration's timing to another codebase or machine; measure the cycle your team actually experiences. - **inferred**: Make common mistakes difficult and diagnostics useful. Put a repeatedly forgotten check in the iteration mechanism, language rule, or project-specific analysis where appropriate. Explain failures at the relevant source location and suppress errors caused only by recovery placeholders. Such checks reduce debugging but cannot establish correctness outside the cases they model. - **inferred**: Evaluate tools on real, demanding programs. A compelling toy demonstration is not enough if the difficult mechanism is bypassed. - **inferred**: Preserve the ability to inspect and reproduce the software you ship. Keep dependencies visible and available, choose updates explicitly, and verify the packaged artifact on a separate machine. This shifts update responsibility to the team but reduces dependence on remote availability and hidden machine state. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Treat performance as a property of the workload and its distribution. Investigate occasional long frames and actual causes, not just averages or a microbenchmark's favored case. A sophisticated rendering technique should be compared against a reasonably engineered simple baseline on representative data. Precise instruction measurement still has a place after the larger behavior is understood. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Design around data transformations and lifetimes, not object labels alone. Examine what is processed together, what must remain live, and whether independently allocated objects create scattered accesses. This is a workload-sensitive argument, not proof that every use of RAII or reference counting is slow. Explicit lifetime events can also make correctness easier to audit. - **inferred**: Evaluate safety as a concrete trade-off without dismissing its goal. Compare prevented failures, debugging cost, restrictions, and experimental friction. Domain-specific static checks and debug allocators can be useful without providing the guarantees of a sound static system. Blow explicitly does not claim his approach is proven better than Rust's; security-critical or untrusted environments may value guarantees differently. - **inferred**: Do not let a familiar data structure become a requirement. Restate the problem in terms of the actual relationships and invariants, then decide how to accelerate that formulation. The triangle-based seam database replaced octree-centered reasoning; the inventory experiment stored a relation once instead of maintaining forward and reverse structures. Simple initial representations may need later indexing. Corroboration: medium (2 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **inferred**: Use the error measure that matches the application. Average error can conceal bad worst cases; a mathematically bounded geometric error can still miss texture or shader artifacts. Decide which values must remain exact, which failures users perceive, and where a worst-case bound is worth its cost. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). Scope-bound cleanup can nevertheless be valuable when it reliably closes a small, well-defined profiling zone on every return path. - **inferred**: Do not mistake a shipping compromise for an ideal design. Time pressure and exhaustion can make an imperfect workaround rational, but its assumptions should remain visible for later investigation. Likewise, a broad cleanup can introduce bugs even when its goal is sound, so test the changed behavior rather than declaring the refactor safe. - **inferred**: Choose team size against coordination and understanding, not an assumed linear schedule gain. More people can help some projects but also distribute system knowledge, create layers, and require management. Blow accepts the opposite cost of understaffing: he can miss things happening in his own organization. The right size depends on the work and the team's ability to coordinate. - **inferred**: Choose rules and architecture according to the actual domain. ECS can solve a general engine provider's flexibility problem without being mandatory for a team making one custom game. Garbage collection may fit users and workloads whose constraints differ from a high-frame-rate game. A useful convention becomes harmful when elevated into an unquestioned universal rule. - **inferred**: Test platform claims on the platform itself. A checked-in port or binding that has never been run is not sufficient evidence that others can use it. Blow accepted responsibility for insufficient oversight of untested platform code. Apply this to the release path as well as the module in isolation; testing on a developer's machine can conceal missing dependencies.
