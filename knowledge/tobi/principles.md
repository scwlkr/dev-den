# Tobias Lütke (Tobi Lütke): principles

Built from 86 accepted distinct works and 693,413 source words. Compiled reasoning with source support and limits below.

- **documented**: Make the user's intent easier without making the user feel incompetent. Design for the person who is anxious, time-constrained, or unfamiliar with the domain, not just for a trained operator. When users predictably misunderstand an affordance, investigate the software rather than assuming a deficiency in them. This is an accountability stance, not a claim that all mistakes are caused by interfaces. Corroboration: high (3 distinct works; this measures source count, not truth). - **documented**: Prefer capability over tool identity. Learn the problem, retain the ability to make things, and practice extracting value from new tools rather than defining oneself permanently by a framework. Ruby can be a particularly good personal fit without becoming a universal requirement. This does not erase the value of specialists: Lütke also values demonstrated depth and explicitly recognizes exceptional specialists. - **inferred**: Choose between a hack and a refactor by opportunity cost and accumulated consequences. A quick implementation can deliver value and create resources for later repair; repeated shortcuts can also make the product difficult to change. Compare time to useful feedback, the cost of the debt, and whether the underlying design is likely to be reused. Neither speed nor architectural purity wins automatically. - **documented**: Keep the team close to the real problem, but do not outsource solution design to requests. Observe users, support tickets, and the setting in which work happens; ask what is difficult and why. Customer requests are evidence of needs, not an automatic product specification. The team remains responsible for synthesizing a coherent solution, while being willing to revise it when users reveal a missed fact. - **inferred**: Use data to inform judgment, not to replace the goal. Select metrics with a plausible relationship to business health, use experiments and rollout monitoring, and look for proxy gaming or shifts hidden by aggregates. Preserve attention to quality that a metric cannot yet represent. Lütke's earlier compass-metric guidance and later skepticism of conventional KPI targets are best read together, not as a rejection of measurement. - **documented**: Benchmark what can be measured; use judgment for long-horizon uncertainty. An infrastructure performance question may have a direct test, while a market or platform bet requires assumptions about changing conditions. Do not pretend a strategic forecast has the certainty of a benchmark, or substitute a speculative bet when a practical measurement is available. - **documented**: Keep decision authority clear while bringing relevant perspectives into the room. Some decisions belong near customers; others benefit from being brought inward for wider product context. A leader should participate in detail when competent and helpful, but not arbitrarily override a team. Lütke changed his earlier hands-off management view after judging it harmful to his product work; he does not claim his preferred level of involvement works in every industry. - **documented**: Adapt hiring and development to the actual talent market. Look for agency, demonstrated craft, response to setbacks, and capacity to learn rather than treating credentials or elapsed years as decisive. In Ottawa, Lütke connected hiring for potential with coaching and longer expected tenure. This is a local strategy, not proof that experience or degrees lack value, and not a substitute for assessing the work a role requires.
