- **inferred**: The Hacker Way — iterative product learning. Build a sound foundation, release small changes, compare variants, learn from use, and repeat. Use it when the product can safely be changed and real behavior is more informative than internal forecasts. Zuckerberg described testing thousands of versions in parallel, not a required scale. Inferred decision rule: favor the smallest release that can answer a consequential question, but add production review and stronger safeguards where errors are costly. He also acknowledges that a more polished release culture can work for other products and companies. - **inferred**: Unnamed — fit the model to the job. Identify the task, latency, host, privacy, and cost constraints; choose an appropriately sized model; adapt it with relevant data; and consider distillation or a small router before invoking a larger model.
